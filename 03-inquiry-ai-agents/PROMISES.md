# Premise

The paper makes substantive claims that point outward for fuller development — to "Wecker, in preparation" or "Wecker, in review, 2026" (anonymised as "Author, …" in the anon build via `refs/deny-list.yml`), and to author-originated framings and observational records that are not separately cited. This document catalogues every such reference in the *granted-agency* revision and assesses its load-bearing weight: how badly the paper's argument would be hurt if the promise turned out to be unredeemed (companion work doesn't appear, observational record is questioned, framing is attacked).

**Three categories** of "promise" appear:

1. **Explicit Wecker/Anon citations** — formal pointers to companion work, with "in preparation" or "in review, 2026" tags. These are the load-bearing concentration that `ARG-EXPAND` Gap #6 identifies as the paper's largest single risk.
2. **Implicit companion-work pointers** — phrases like "companion work" or "deferred to companion work" without a tagged citation, embedded in body prose or footnotes.
3. **Author observational and framing claims** — claims explicitly attributed to the author's own observation or terminology, on which downstream arguments rest.

**Load-bearing labels** used below:

- **Spine-critical** — failure of this promise would undermine a central premise of the paper. The paper does not survive without the promise being redeemable in some form.
- **Section-load-bearing** — failure would significantly weaken a specific section's argument; the paper survives but is materially diminished.
- **Specific-claim-load-bearing** — failure would weaken a specific claim or paragraph; the paper's main argument is robust to it.
- **Decorative** — the reference points to additional treatment, but the paper's argument is self-contained without it.

Confidence labels are my confidence in the assessment, not in the redeemability of the underlying promise.

**Disposition labels** (Δ column, rc1 → granted-agency):

- **=** — no change; promise text identical, surrounding paragraph identical.
- **CTX↑** — promise text unchanged but *surrounding support strengthened* in the revision (paper now offers more in-text material to defend the same promise).
- **LOAD↑** — promise text unchanged but *load on the promise increased* in the revision (the paper now claims more, so the unpaid debt is larger).
- **FMT** — promise restated in a different format (e.g., moved into a numbered list) but the content is unchanged.
- **NEW** — added in granted-agency; not present in rc1.

**Most exposed under granted-agency (after CTX↑ on items 7, 8, and I2):** all four spine-critical ACA pointers now have substantial in-paper lineage to fall back on. # Recommendations

1. **Highest priority** — make the **ACA companion (Wecker, in preparation)** accessible in some form before the reviewer cycle closes. Even a draft chapter or a substantial-blog-post version of the formal argument, citable separately, would lessen the spine-critical exposure of items 3, 7, 8, I2. The granted-agency revision's in-paper expansion is the prerequisite; the companion accessibility is the second-stage protection.

2. **Second priority** — for **items 1, 2, 9**, prepare a one-page "what the companion will contain" supplement that can be made available to reviewers on request. Not a full companion; just a structural outline demonstrating that the formalism exists in draft form. This is cheap insurance.

3. **Bibliography management** — the *Wecker, in preparation* and *Wecker, in review, 2026* references currently appear as plain text (per `bin/build.README.md` convention) rather than as pandoc citations. When the companion(s) are actually submitted or preprinted, replace plain-text mentions with proper `[@key]` citations and add YAML entries under `refs/entries/`. The `deny-list.yml` substitutions can then be updated to replace `Wecker` → `Author` at the citation level rather than the prose level, which is more robust.

4. **For Category 3 (author observational/framing claims)** — items C1 and C2 in particular would benefit from either (a) a companion-paper publication that documents the observational methodology, or (b) a public archive of the observational records (with appropriate privacy considerations) that reviewers could consult. The current state — "the author's observational record" — is a weak rhetorical position even when the underlying claim is robust.
