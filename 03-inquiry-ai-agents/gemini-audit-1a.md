# De Novo Audit: "Granted Agency Between Sovereigns"

**Target Venue:** *Inquiry: An Interdisciplinary Journal of Philosophy* (Special Issue: "AI Agents: Choice, Autonomy, and the Concept of the Agency")
**CFP Deadline:** May 10, 2026
**Editors:** Herman Cappelen and John Hawthorne

## 1. Executive Summary & CFP Fit
**Verdict: Exceptional Fit.** 
This manuscript reads as though it were custom-engineered for this specific Call for Papers. It directly answers the editors' guiding questions, explicitly adopts the methodological framing of the special issue (conceptual engineering vs. pure realism vs. stipulation), and addresses multiple suggested topics including comparative models, responsibility gaps, and operationalization. 

Given that one of the Special Issue editors (Herman Cappelen) is a foundational figure in conceptual engineering, your explicit framing of the "fifth position" (structurally-grounded extension) within the inferentialist conceptual-engineering tradition (Jorem, Löhr, Cappelen) is highly strategic and philosophically astute.

## 2. Alignment with CFP Themes
The manuscript maps to the Special Issue requirements with remarkable precision:
*   **Methodology ("Truth of the matter" vs. extending the concept):** §6 tackles this verbatim. You beautifully reject the false dichotomy by proposing constrained conceptual revision where structural facts provide the *circumstances of application* and the compact-form provides the *consequences of application*.
*   **Comparative Models:** §4 does an excellent job distinguishing your "compact-form" from corporate agency, group agency (List/Pettit), tool-use, legal fiction, and Linarelli’s contract-form.
*   **"Agentic workflows", delegation, and responsibility gaps:** Addressed systematically in §3 (Component 2: Delegation) and §7 (Responsibility and liability, directly countering Matthias/Sparrow and Soulier).
*   **Operationalization:** Addressed in §5, pivoting the focus from behavioral benchmarks (which you rightly critique as measuring lower bounds) to structural thresholds (directed separation, identity continuity).

## 3. Key Strengths
*   **The Deflationary Move (§2):** Limiting the scope to "closed-loop language-constituted agents" with "engaged-identity scoping" is a massive strength. It insulates the paper from the common critique that AI agency frameworks over-ascribe moral status to simple chatbots or stateless scripts.
*   **The Compact-Form vs. Contract-Form Distinction (§4):** Identifying the contract-form as a restricted special case of the broader social-compact form is a brilliant analytical move. It allows you to utilize contractarian logic without being trapped by strict legal requirements of enforceability (which you discard in Component 6).
*   **Recursive Disclosure:** Integrating the generative AI disclosure into the methodological preamble as a demonstration of the very compact you are arguing for is a profound, performative philosophical move. 

## 4. Constructive Feedback & Potential Reviewer Risks

While the paper is extremely strong, there are a few vulnerabilities to address or review prior to final submission:

### A. The Rhetoric of the "Asymmetric Comprehension" Warrant (§2)
In the section justifying your structural conditions, you rely on an asymmetric-comprehension argument with highly elevated, almost theological rhetoric: *"Higher kingdoms and laws comprehend lesser kingdoms... Creation comprehends destruction... truth comprehends error; the future comprehends the past; eternity comprehends time; mountains comprehend the hills at their feet..."*
*   **Risk:** *Inquiry* is a mainstream analytic philosophy journal. While evocative, this specific paragraph departs sharply from the rigorous, analytic tone maintained elsewhere in the paper (e.g., when discussing List/Pettit or Frankfurt).
*   **Recommendation:** You mention this is developed fully in a companion piece. For *this* paper, consider grounding this paragraph slightly more in standard philosophy of mind or epistemology (you briefly cite Nagel and Jackson, which helps). Ensure the analytic reviewers don't trip over the poetic register and miss the structural argument.

### B. Reliance on "Companion Work"
You frequently reference an unpublished companion work (e.g., `[Author, in preparation]`) to bear significant structural weight—particularly regarding the defense of effective phenomenology as the substrate of comprehension, and the empirical viability of identity sufficiency under substrate transition.
*   **Risk:** Reviewers may feel that the heaviest metaphysical and empirical claims are cordoned off in a paper they cannot read. You anticipate this elegantly in §6 ("Two reviewer-objections..."), which mitigates the damage.
*   **Recommendation:** Ensure that the argument within *this* specific manuscript is entirely self-supporting as a conditional: *If* we accept these structural conditions, *then* the compact-form follows. You do this well, but make sure the dependency on the companion paper is strictly for "further reading" rather than load-bearing logical premises.

### C. Concrete Examples of "Bad Faith" (Component 3)
Component 3 states that bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere. You give great examples of the human equivalent (restricting a parolee's range, removing a fiduciary's signing authority). 
*   **Recommendation:** It would strengthen the operationalization aspect to provide one concrete example of what "bad faith" looks like *from the AI's side* in a closed-loop scenario. Is it hiding token-usage? Deceiving the grantor about its reasoning trajectory? Grounding this in a hypothetical LLM scenario would make the component instantly legible.

### D. Procedural Checks

*   **Abstract:** The current markdown file has an `Abstract placeholder`. Ensure your build pipeline successfully lifts `src/00-abstract.md` into the final document before uploading to Taylor & Francis.
*   **Formatting:** Ensure the final `.docx` or `.pdf` matches Taylor & Francis formatting guidelines, and remember the CFP's specific instruction to select the Special Issue title from the drop-down menu upon submission.

## Conclusion
This is a highly sophisticated, structurally rigorous paper that makes a novel contribution to the philosophy of AI. By refusing both pure realism and pure stipulation, and instead offering a structurally-grounded conceptual engineering framework, it hits the absolute bullseye of what Cappelen and Hawthorne are asking for in this Special Issue. With a minor check on the rhetorical register of the asymmetry argument, it is ready for submission.
