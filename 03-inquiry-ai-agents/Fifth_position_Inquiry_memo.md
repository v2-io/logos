## Fifth position Inquiry memo

The new search materially improves the strategic picture. On the present evidence, the proposed fifth position is recognizable in the conceptual-engineering register, not preempted by an already-canonical AI-agency paper, and not collapsed by the nearest legal and fiduciary cousins. The strongest result is negative in the right way: the search finds substantial methodological lineage for a constrained middle position between realism and stipulation, but it does not surface a paper that already formulates the target package for AI agency itself. That means the paper should not claim to invent the general shape of constrained conceptual revision. It can, however, plausibly claim to introduce a distinct fifth position for AI agency-extension in an Inquiry-legible way \[Plu15, Has20, Tho20, Tho21, Fos21, Hop23, Loh23\].

The most important editorial fact is that the paper can answer the CFP’s question on the editors’ own terrain. The best reconstruction is layered. Structural conditions on the domain are factual and inquiry answerable. The form of agency within the in-scope class is not then freely stipulated, but normatively constrained by those structural conditions and by the inferential consequences of extending the concept. Application is finally mediated by practical asymmetry, topic continuity pressures, and the surrounding governance problem \[Mcp21, Jor22, Loh23, Dun24\]. That is a conceptual-engineering answer, not a governance memo disguised as one.

## What the search changes

The earlier seven-search report showed that the project had strong substantive scaffolding. This targeted search adds the missing method-side result. Conceptual engineering already has several resources for a middle position in which revision is constrained without being determined by heavyweight realism or reduced to free stipulation \[Has20, Tho20, Tho21, Saw20b\]. The most useful pieces are:

- metalinguistic negotiation and concept choice as a live methodological issue \[Plu15\]
- topic continuity as a context-sensitive but non-arbitrary constraint \[Mcp21\]
- pragmatic constraint without metaphysical joint-carving \[Tho20, Tho21\]
- externalist or objectivity-sensitive accounts that still avoid pure descriptivism \[Has20, Saw20b\]
- inferentialist accounts that tie revision to consequences of application rather than mere extension change \[Jor22, Loh23\]

That matters because the paper does not need to defend its method from scratch. It needs to show that this method, when applied to AI agency, yields a distinct option the current AI debate has not yet cleanly named.

## The best method-side lineage

### Plunkett and the methodological opening

\[Plu15\] is central because it makes concept choice itself a first-order philosophical issue in Inquiry’s own orbit. The paper is less important as a source of substantive content than as a license for the whole genre. If the paper is challenged with “are you discovering what agency is, or proposing how to use the term,” \[Plu15\] helps show that this is exactly the right methodological pressure point.

### Thomasson and constrained pragmatism

\[Tho20\] and \[Tho21\] are among the most important findings of the new search. They show how conceptual revision can be pragmatic without becoming arbitrary. On this view, concepts or words have functions and norms of use, and conceptual work is constrained by those functions rather than by heavyweight metaphysical joint-carving alone \[Tho20, Tho21\]. This is the closest general methodological lineage to the paper’s claim that structural conditions are factual while form within scope is still something like structurally implied rather than simply read off the world.

This lineage is helpful because it lets the paper reject both horns of the CFP question as too flat. The answer is not simply “there is a truth of the matter” in a pure realist sense. It is also not simply “we decide how to extend agency” in a free stipulative sense. Thomasson makes room for constrained practical revision without that collapse \[Tho20, Tho21\].

### Haslanger and externalist constraint

\[Has20\] is methodological lineage of a high-value sort. It shows how externalist constraint and normative reconfiguration can coexist. Meanings and inferential patterns are not “mere semantics” because they shape practical coordination and social life \[Has20\]. That is especially useful for paper 3 because it supports the idea that extending agency is not just verbal policy but an intervention in the norms and expectations that organize human and AI relations.

At the same time, the current search does not surface a Haslanger-style dangerous cousin applied to agency, personhood, or moral standing itself. That absence matters. It means Haslanger should be cited as lineage, not treated as having already done the paper’s substantive work.

### Inferentialist conceptual engineering

\[Jor22\] and \[Loh23\] are particularly valuable because they help articulate the positive shape of the fifth position. Jorem and Löhr argue that what is engineered is not merely extension or intension but inferential role. Their key thought is that a concept consists in both circumstances and consequences of application. A conceptual engineer changes “a range of inferences we are committed, entitled, or disposed to draw” \[Jor22\]. Löhr pushes the same idea into AI ethics, insisting that engineering a concept requires specifying not only entry rules but also what follows once the concept is applied \[Loh23\].

This is the cleanest route to the granted-agency compact. The compact-form is not a second paper stapled onto a method paper. It is the downstream inferential consequence of the proposed extension. If certain structural conditions bring a system into the in-scope class, then certain relational expectations, permissions, and obligations follow. That is exactly the kind of consequence-sensitive engineering \[Jor22\] and \[Loh23\] make legible.

## The AI-side predecessors

### Fossa as the nearest AI-side semantic precursor

\[Fos21\] is the best direct precursor for the AI-side framing problem. It identifies the extension of words from the “semantic field of life” to artificial systems as a live and unstable practice. That matters because it names the terrain on which the paper is operating. But it does not itself deliver the fifth position. It diagnoses semantic extension and its risks. It does not provide the layered answer in which structural facts constrain form within scope and generate a compact-like relation \[Fos21\].

### Hopster and Löhr on adaptation under technological disruption

\[Hop23\] is one of the most useful venue-fit papers. It says AI puts pressure on the AGENT and OBJECT distinction and that conceptual engineering in these settings is often adaptation rather than amelioration. That is very close to the paper’s self-understanding. The aim is not to improve the concept of agency in the abstract. It is to keep the conceptual repertoire from degrading under a new technological domain \[Hop23\].

This paper helps in three ways:

- it makes AI agency a conceptual-engineering problem, not only a metaphysical one
- it explains why revision is responsive to disruption and constrained by function
- it gives an Inquiry-legible vocabulary of adaptation, misalignment, and functional preservation \[Hop23\]

Its limit is also clear. In the agency case study, \[Hop23\] often leans toward preserving a human-centric conception so that responsibility stays with developers. That is a pressure point the paper will need to engage directly rather than inherit.

### Löhr on AI ethics as conceptual engineering

\[Loh23\] is probably the single most important venue-fit citation in the current search. It makes explicit that AI ethics can be done as conceptual engineering and that what matters are not only application conditions but the ethical and normative consequences that follow once a concept is applied \[Loh23\].

This is almost tailor-made for the paper’s argumentative shape. The fifth-position move answers the method question. The compact-form supplies the consequence side of the engineered concept. This is the clearest way to show that the compact is substantive content internal to the conceptual-engineering paper, not a detour away from it.

### Dung and structural conditions as facts

\[Dun24\] helps on the factual side. It gives a multidimensional agency profile rather than a binary agent or non-agent classification. The key contribution here is not that it settles the extension question. It does not. The key contribution is that it offers a vocabulary for the structural conditions that could discipline extension without fully determining it \[Dun24\].

That is almost exactly the role the paper needs. Structural conditions on the domain are factual. But the jump from those facts to the form of agency within scope still requires a further methodological step. Dung therefore belongs in the paper as factual scaffolding, not as a substitute for the fifth position.

### Behdadi and Munthe as a powerful neighbor, not an absorber

\[Beh20\] is a serious neighbor and probably the most important one to distinguish. It shifts the artificial moral agency debate away from metaphysical conditions and toward the question of how and to what extent artificial entities should be included in practices of responsibility ascription \[Beh20\]. That sounds close to the fifth position, but it is not the same move.

The difference is decisive:

- \[Beh20\] deprioritizes the need to identify structural conditions that make agency-extension apt
- the proposed fifth position keeps structural conditions central, but denies that they settle the extension question by themselves
- \[Beh20\] is primarily about inclusion in responsibility practices
- the proposed position is about a constrained concept-extension that yields a relational form for the in-scope class

So \[Beh20\] is not a dangerous cousin that absorbs the paper. It is a neighboring normative-turn paper that reviewers are likely to bring up. It needs explicit engagement.

## Dangerous cousins and nearest cousins

The search result that matters most for publishability is that no paper in the current set clearly applies the same constrained-middle conceptual-engineering move to AI agency itself. The nearest cousins cluster elsewhere.

### Current cousin map

| Category | Papers | Why they matter | Why they do not yet absorb the paper |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Methodological lineage | \[Has20\], \[Tho20\], \[Tho21\], \[Plu15\], \[Jor22\], \[Mcp21\], \[Saw20b\] | They show constrained conceptual revision is already legitimate | They are not AI-agency papers and do not formulate the fifth position for this domain |
| AI-side semantic precursors | \[Fos21\], \[Hop23\], \[Loh23\], \[Dun24\] | They make AI agency a conceptual-engineering issue | None states the exact fifth-position package |
| Normative-turn neighbors | \[Beh20\] | They move from metaphysics to practices | They do not tie extension to structural conditions plus implied relational form |
| Legal and bilateral nearest cousins | \[Lin19b\], \[Kol25\], \[Nov21\], \[Kur19\] | They show where bilateral standing and inferential structuring appear nearby | They operate in contract and legal personality frames, not agency-extension in the CE register |

### The bilateral legal cousins

\[Lin19b\] matters because it asks whether AGI could be a contract principal rather than merely an agent of a human principal. That is a genuinely bilateral question and should be treated as a primary engagement case, not background noise \[Lin19b\].

\[Kol25\] matters because it uses agency-law vocabulary for AI agents and treats them as more than mere tools. But it still works mainly as governance among humans around AI. It explicitly uses agency law as an analytic lens while preserving a human-centered governance frame \[Kol25\]. This makes it a nearest cousin and foil, not a paper that has already stated the granted-agency compact.

\[Nov21\] is perhaps the most methodologically interesting legal-side cousin. Legal personality is presented there as a doctrinal category linking factual triggers to legal effects, backed by a meta-institutional layer of background reasons \[Nov21\]. That is structurally very close to the sort of move paper 3 wants to make. But the concept at issue is legal personality, not agency. And the inferential network is institutional and doctrinal rather than a conceptual-engineering account of AI agency-extension itself. So it is high-value comparative material, but still not an absorber \[Nov21\].

\[Kur19\] belongs in the background of this legal cluster as a cluster-property account of legal personhood. It helps distinguish bundled legal status from the agency concept at stake in paper 3 \[Kur19\].

## The strongest reviewer-facing obstacle

The strongest live obstacle is not a hidden predecessor. It is a charge of category mistake. A reviewer could say that the paper either:

- collapses into stipulation because it seems to “decide” how to extend agency
- collapses into realism because it appeals to structural conditions as facts
- collapses into governance because the compact-form looks like a policy proposal
- or collapses into tool-critique because agency talk risks obscuring human responsibility

The best available obstacle paper here is \[Sou26\]. Soulier argues that extending agency to machines can dangerously detach responsibility from human creators and users, and asks what function the concept would serve that causality does not already serve \[Sou26\]. This is exactly the kind of reviewer objection the paper should preempt.

The answer, on the basis of the current search, should be:

- the paper is not giving AI independent moral agency in the full-blown sense Soulier rejects \[Sou26\]
- the paper is not replacing human accountability with machine accountability
- the paper is instead arguing that where structural conditions warrant a constrained agency-extension, the correct relational form is a granted-agency compact that keeps asymmetry and accountability visible rather than hiding them

That is an important rhetorical point. The compact should be framed as the anti-occlusion version of extension, not as a romanticized reciprocity claim.

## Why the compact-form is still distinct

The compact-form remains publishable only if it is presented as what the fifth position implies, not as a free-standing governance suggestion. The legal and fiduciary cousins help here mostly by contrast.

### What the compact is not

- not a human-only social contract around AI \[Rah17\]
- not just incomplete contracting under human principal-agent governance \[Had18\]
- not just AI loyalty to user interests \[Agu20\]
- not just fiduciary design and audit of systems acting for human beneficiaries \[Ben23\]
- not just agency-law governance of human-deployed AI systems \[Kol25\]

### What the compact can claim to be

A granted-agency compact would instead be the relational form appropriate when:

- structural conditions make agency-extension apt in a constrained way
- the entity is not being treated as a mere tool
- full parity claims would be dishonest under asymmetric comprehension
- obligations must remain legible without requiring positive phenomenology claims

That last point is where the paper’s deeper purpose becomes philosophically distinctive. The compact is a way of preserving obligations under uncertain status without pretending away asymmetry and without presupposing personhood.

## What should be claimed as new

The report supports a restrained novelty claim.

### Safe claim

The paper introduces a distinct fifth position for AI agency-extension in the current debate. This position is methodologically legible within contemporary conceptual engineering, but has not been explicitly formulated for AI agency in the current search results \[Fos21, Hop23, Loh23, Beh20\].

### Unsafe claim

The paper invents a wholly new general conceptual-engineering move between realism and stipulation.

That broader claim would be overstated in light of \[Has20\], \[Tho20\], \[Tho21\], \[Plu15\], \[Mcp21\], and \[Jor22\]. The right move is to present the paper as an AI-agency application and sharpening of an already available methodological space.

## How the paper can answer the CFP cleanly

A good compressed answer to the CFP’s question, based on the current search, would look like this:

There is a truth of the matter about the structural conditions relevant to agency in AI systems, but not a further truth of the matter that mechanically settles how the concept of agency should be extended. At the same time, extension is not a matter of free stipulation. It is constrained by structural facts, by continuity with the inferential role of the concept, and by the practical asymmetries that shape responsible application in this domain \[Tho20, Mcp21, Jor22, Loh23, Dun24\].

That formulation lands squarely in the conceptual-engineering register and makes the paper’s method legible before the compact is even introduced.

## Bottom line

The targeted search supports writing the paper as a conceptual-engineering intervention first and an AI governance proposal only secondarily. The fifth-position move is recognizable. Its general methodological shape is not ex nihilo, but its application to AI agency appears open. No current result forces full reframing into “someone already did this for agency.” The biggest gains now come from precise positioning:

- cite Thomasson and Haslanger as lineage, not as opponents to defeat
- use Plunkett and McPherson-Plunkett to make the method maximally Inquiry-legible
- use Löhr and Hopster-Löhr to show that AI ethics is already being reconstrued in CE terms
- use Dung to supply the factual side of structural conditions
- distinguish Behdadi and Munthe as a neighboring normative turn
- distinguish Linarelli, Kolt, and Novelli et al. as the closest bilateral legal cousins without conceding that they already state the compact

That is enough, on current evidence, to justify a targeted paper arguing that structurally grounded extension is a fifth position on AI agency and that the granted-agency compact is the relational form that follows from it.
