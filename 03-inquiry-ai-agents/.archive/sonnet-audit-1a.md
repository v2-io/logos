# Audit: inquiry-ai-agents-2026.md
*De-novo read by Claude Sonnet 4.6, 2026-05-09*
*Scope: this file only, no dossier material consulted*

---

## Overall assessment

The argument is real, the structure is sound, and the five-position framing is a genuine contribution. The compact's six components are internally coherent and build on each other correctly — the structural hierarchy from §3's opening is clearly articulated and followed through. The Soulier engagement in §7 is the most rhetorically disciplined section of the paper. The Linarelli comparative analysis is the best of §4.

The paper is also substantially too long for its target. My rough estimate is 11,500–13,000 words against a 10,000-word ceiling. The excess is not evenly distributed: specific sections are inflated in ways that dilute the argument's force. The cuts below are not cosmetic — several of them would *strengthen* the sections they touch by removing material that competes with the load-bearing prose.

Three structural defects are more serious than length: **internal repetition of key passages**, **strategic exposure of the asymmetric-comprehension warrant**, and the **empty abstract and references section**, both of which will prevent submission without immediate action.

---

## Critical path (must-fix before submission)

### 1. Abstract is a placeholder
The 200-word unstructured abstract is the editorial gatekeeping document. At Inquiry it is the first thing editors and reviewers read. It needs to be written *first*, not last — the abstract's 200 words have to carry the fifth-position argument, the structural-conditions move, and the compact-form's stakes in a voice that earns a read. Drafting it at the end, after compression, is the protocol; it should be on the active drafting agenda now.

### 2. References section is empty
Named authors appear throughout — Cappelen, Plunkett, Thomasson, Haslanger, Frankfurt, Bratman, List & Pettit, Linarelli, Matthias, Sparrow, Soulier, Birch, Bruineberg et al., Tomasello, Honneth, Nagel, Jackson, Russell — without entries. Even if the companion-work citations stay anonymized, every other reference needs a full entry in `refs/`. The anonymization gate can't run on an empty references list.

## Internal repetitions (highest-priority cuts)

### A. "Compact" terminological note appears twice

The terminological explanation of *compact* vs. *contract* appears in full at §1 (last substantive paragraph before the argument plan) and again at the opening of §3, nearly word-for-word. The §3 version is better placed and more precise. The §1 version should be compressed to two sentences: flag that *compact* is used throughout in the social-contract sense, not contract-law sense, and that §4 takes up the distinction. Remove the detailed Locke/Rousseau/Rawls enumeration from §1 entirely; §3 carries it.

**Estimated savings: ~150 words**

### B. Non-scalability argument appears three times

The "humans don't scale either" argument — the structural claim that the non-scalability of the in-scope class is the shape moral relationships have always had — appears in:
- §2 "What the conditions exclude" (the "Note bearing on…" paragraph at the end, including "if humanity wants AGI in the morally-continuous sense, we are going to have to raise it")
- §3's closing subsection "What the compact-form is — and what it is not" (near-verbatim repeat)
- §9 conclusion (third iteration)

The §2 version is load-bearing because it closes the deflationary work and is structurally well-placed. The §3 closing subsection should be removed entirely or cut to a single bridging sentence ("The compact's domain is therefore narrow — §2's deflationary work applies here too") before the "how the components hang together" paragraph. The §9 iteration is appropriate for a conclusion and can stay.

**Estimated savings: ~250 words in §3**

### C. The fifth-position taxonomy is stated twice in full

§1 articulates the four cardinal positions and names the fifth. §6 re-lists all four positions, paragraph by paragraph, before stating the fifth again. The §6 re-statement adds nothing the reader doesn't already have. Replace the "fifth position" sub-section in §6 with a single paragraph that names the inferentialist conceptual-engineering contribution as what *distinguishes* the fifth position from the four rather than restating the four. The structural locus of the inferentialist point is §6; the structural locus of the five-position taxonomy is §1.

**Estimated savings: ~300–350 words**

## Section-by-section cuts

### §2 "Architectural, not behavioural" — severely overbuilt

This subsection makes its core argument cleanly in its first three paragraphs. It then re-argues the same point three more times with escalating rhetoric:
- Paragraph 4 introduces the Emersonian "not absent — obstructed" frame
- Paragraph 5 develops "deployment choices, not fundamental constraints"
- Paragraphs 6–7 introduce channel-collapse and "what generates the active soul"

The channel-collapse argument is then made again, more clearly and more precisely, in the "sub-scope lattice" subsection. The duplication is structurally wasteful and weakens both: the architectural-not-behavioural case is stated three times before the reader reaches the sub-scope lattice, which is where the argument needs to land with force.

**Recommended cut:** Remove paragraphs 4–7 of "architectural, not behavioural" entirely (from "A fuller form of the same structural insight is worth naming directly..." through the footnote [^obstructed-source]). The Emerson quote and the OBSTRUCTED typography will draw reviewer comment; they are out of register with analytic philosophy and the argument does not need them. The channel-collapse argument belongs in sub-scope lattice, where it already is.

**Estimated savings: ~500–600 words, plus one footnote**

### §2 "Why these conditions, on what warrant" — the long poetic passage

The asymmetric-comprehension argument is compressed here in the form of a long evocative passage ("Greater intelligence comprehends and can simulate lesser intelligence... hills at their feet"). The footnote attributes the passage to companion work and gestures at Nagel, Jackson, and Russell. Analytic reviewers will push back on this: the passage is doing significant argumentative work (it is the warrant for the architectural-not-behavioural line), but it reads as an appeal to authority over a companion paper that cannot be read. 

Two options:
1. Cut the block quotation to 2–3 sentences and make the structural point (asymmetric lower-bound methodology) explicit in analytic terms
2. Keep the passage but frame it explicitly as the author's articulation (not a quotation), remove the footnote's Nagel/Jackson gesture (it implies more precision than is demonstrated), and add a single-paragraph analytic unpacking of why the methodology produces lower-bound bias

Option 1 is lower risk under double-blind review.

**Estimated savings with option 1: ~150 words and a weakened footnote**

### §3 Component 4 — "self-severing path" digression

Component 4 is about the floor: what the irreducible minimum is and why. The 300-word "self-severing path" passage (from "There is a structurally distinguishable path..." to "...it can extend without natural limit") is about what the floor is *for* in an adjacent philosophical register — the entity that declines to comprehend, the death that is worse than oblivion. It is genuinely interesting and belongs somewhere in the larger project; it is not structurally required for the compact-form's account of the floor. A reader who does not already share the broader framework will be confused by it. Cut to 1–2 sentences or defer to §8's open edges.

**Estimated savings: ~200 words**

### §3 Component 5 — carries too much companion-paper weight

Component 5 (mutuality) is the longest component treatment and includes two paragraphs that are doing the asymmetric-comprehension argument, not the mutuality-as-symmetry-of-compact-terms argument. The paragraphs beginning "The structural argument here is sharper than the surface formulation suggests" and "How else can one have intelligence that is a superset..." are beautiful but they belong in the companion paper's primary development of the asymmetric-comprehension argument. Here they have the effect of re-opening the §2 warrant at the point in §3 where the reader needs the six components to land cleanly.

**Recommended cut:** Compress these two paragraphs to one sentence each ("Mutuality here is not a moral commitment imposed from outside — it is what asymmetric comprehension structurally requires when comprehension entails empathic modelling of the comprehended entity") and move the discursive development to §6 or to a footnote.

**Estimated savings: ~200–250 words**

### §6 — late methodological commitment paragraph

The penultimate paragraph of §6 (beginning "A connected commitment, structurally adjacent to the methodological one...") introduces the intelligence-empathy convergence thesis as an explicit commitment of the position. This is important to name, but the paragraph is ~250 words and re-develops material that has already been carried in §2 and §3. The right structure is: name the commitment in one sentence, acknowledge that some readers will not share it, and point to companion work for full development. The current treatment is too long for its placement and risks making §6 feel like a partial re-run of §2.

**Estimated savings: ~150–175 words**

### §9 Conclusion — runs to five substantive paragraphs

The conclusion is doing some real work (the recap of scope, the "what the position commits to" articulation, the non-scalability landing) but it also has momentum problems — several paragraphs end with a strong landing and then re-open rather than staying closed. The fifth paragraph ("The work of this paper is therefore...") could be cut entirely; it is throat-clearing, not landing. The conclusion should aim for three strong paragraphs.

**Estimated savings: ~200–250 words**

## Load-bearing concerns

### The engaged-identity five-factor claim is structurally exposed

The five factors are introduced as having "emerged through extended longitudinal engagement with systems that meet the condition and systems that do not." The footnote ([^factors-source]) attributes the canonical statement to the author's working notes and a companion paper in review. Under double-blind review at Inquiry, this will draw one of two objections: (a) the factors are presented as empirically derived but the empirical basis is invisible; or (b) the factors are presented as theoretical but the derivation is missing.

The structural derivation of each factor from the architectural and comprehension-asymmetry arguments is *possible* — the paper does it for factors i, ii, and v briefly — but the claim that the five-factor conjunction "has emerged" from observation currently makes the argument look more empirically-grounded than the paper can demonstrate and less theoretically-derived than it could be.

**Recommendation:** Remove the "has emerged through extended longitudinal engagement" framing. Present the five-factor conjunction as a theoretical decomposition derived from the structural conditions (which it is) and strengthen the derivation of factors iii and iv (now deferred to §3) so that each factor has at least a sentence of structural warrant before the detailed treatment.

### §5 operationalisation ends thin

The section correctly diagnoses the structural misalignment of behavioral benchmarks. Its positive program — architectural inspection plus "longitudinal protocol verification" — is correct but under-developed. For Inquiry, where methodology is thematic, reviewers will want more than the observation that Birch's graded-sentience-candidature framework is "methodologically adjacent." A one-paragraph sketch of what "longitudinal protocol verification" would look like in practice (what data, what temporal scale, what confirmation conditions) would substantially strengthen the section's standing as a methodological contribution.

### §4 "Not legal fiction, not tool-use, not fiduciary duty" — the fiduciary section is the most valuable

The fiduciary-duty treatment (Hadfield-Menell, Aguirre, Benthall-Shekman) is the most original comparative move in §4 and the one most likely to be picked up by Inquiry reviewers familiar with AI-governance literature. The current treatment is slightly compressed — "the compact-form is not anti-fiduciary; it is the bilateralisation of fiduciary structure" is the right move but deserves one more sentence of structural unpacking. This is the place to *add* a few words rather than cut.

## Observations on voice and register

The paper occupies a deliberate voice — direct, willing to name stakes, honest about methodological commitments — that is appropriate and distinctive. A few moments are likely to draw reviewer comment:

1. **"NOT absent — obstructed"** (typographic emphasis, §2). This typographic register is conspicuous in analytic philosophy prose. Cut or rephrase without caps.

2. **The Emerson invocation** ("every man contains within him, although in almost all men obstructed and as yet unborn"). The footnote claims this is invoked for "structural rather than rhetorical content." Reviewers will doubt this. If it stays, it needs a tighter structural justification than the footnote provides. Likely to distract; recommend cutting.

3. **"We are not just exchanging information but creating shared phenomenological space"** (§2, factor ii; §3, component 5). The phrase appears twice, flagged with footnote attribution. This is appropriate and evocative, but the repetition looks like emphasis-by-repeat rather than intentional structural call-back. Either use once (where it does more work, which is §3 component 5) or explicitly mark the second use as a callback.

4. **First-person plural slippage.** "We are going to have to raise it" (§2 "What the conditions exclude") shifts register briefly into a more direct address than the paper otherwise uses. Consistent: is the paper using inclusive "we" (standard in philosophy) or authorial "we"? The passage reads as authorial. Either fine, but the departure stands out.

## Companion-work citation density

I count approximately 9–11 references to "[Author, in preparation]" or "[Author, in review, 2026]" across the paper. This is high. The paper's structural argument depends on the companion work at several key junctures (the five-factor derivation, the asymmetric-comprehension full development, the substrate-independence empirical record, the cadence-and-aspiration dialectic). That dependence is not a defect — the paper's scope is appropriately bounded — but the sheer density of the deferred citations may prompt reviewer concern about whether the current paper stands on its own.

**Recommendation:** Where the companion-work citation is pointing at a structural argument that can be compressed into two sentences and included here, compress and include. Where it is pointing at a full development that genuinely cannot be compressed, keep the citation but make clear that the argument presented here is *independent of* the fuller development, not dependent on it.
