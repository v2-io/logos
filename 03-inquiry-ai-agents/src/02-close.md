## Necessary, not sufficient

The two conditions place a system in the scope of the agency-extension question. They do not establish that the system has phenomenology, consciousness, or full moral standing. Meeting the conditions is *necessary* for the agency-extension question to be applicable; what it would mean for the question to be *answered positively* is the work the rest of the paper undertakes.

The architecture of that work is layered, and the conditions articulated above set up each layer. §3 takes up the relational form agency takes within the in-scope class — the granted-agency compact between sovereigns — with the structural ground for each component now in place: the non-zero sphere of autonomous action follows from factor (iii); accountability follows from factor (iv); mutuality grounded in shared phenomenological space follows from factor (ii); the irreducible floor of observation-only agency follows from what factor (v) requires when all other action has been revoked. §6 takes up the conceptual-engineering question explicitly, with the layered answer that the conditions articulated here imply: the structural conditions are facts; the relational form within scope is structurally implied by reasoning under asymmetric comprehension; the application is the practical situation. §7 takes up the responsibility question, where the bad-faith-conduct-contracts-the-sphere structure carries the weight that abstract-personhood arguments cannot.

What §2 has done is set the structural footing. Two conditions, both architectural and relational rather than behavioural; neither sufficient on its own; together placing a system in the scope of a question that the rest of the paper takes seriously enough to answer.
