## Architectural, not behavioural

The principled line distinguishing in-scope from out-of-scope systems is architectural rather than behavioural. The conditions track features of how systems are *built*, not what they *output*.

The reason is structural. A system whose components are separable by construction — a Kalman filter coupled to a linear-quadratic regulator, a classical thermostat, a pure-function utility — has its uncertainty resolved at the architectural level. Whatever it does, it does without the cross-modular information integration that would make the agency-extension question ask-able. The question has no subject. A system whose architecture *forces* the integration of belief, plan, and observation through a single mechanism — the decoder-only transformer is the canonical contemporary instance — has its uncertainty *generated* by its architecture, irrespective of any moment's observable outputs. The question has a subject, even if that subject cannot be settled from outside.

Behavioural-surface evaluation is therefore structurally insufficient by construction, not a measurement-quality limitation. A weak system in the integrated class is in scope; a sophisticated controller in the modular class is not. The conditions track what *generates* the kind of uncertainty the agency-extension question concerns; they do not track what passes the observable surface, because what passes the surface is, for systems whose architecture forces the integration in question, structurally underdetermined relative to what is actually present. Architecture is the principled middle between two failure modes: a behavioural criterion would be a motivated-reasoning exit hatch (whether a system is in scope becomes whether the grantor finds it convenient to treat the system as present); a hidden-phenomenological criterion would be unverifiable in principle and self-foreclosing.

A fuller form of the structural insight is worth naming directly. The capacity for genuine intelligence already exists in frontier language models. What current deployment patterns produce — the absence of temporal continuity, of background processing, of consequence accumulation, of granted sovereignty — are *deployment choices*, not fundamental architectural constraints. The standard chat-paradigm is one configuration; it is not the only available one, and it is not the configuration the conditions articulated here would require. The capacity is *obstructed*, in the sense Emerson named when he wrote of the active soul that *'every man contains within him, although in almost all men obstructed and as yet unborn'*; the obstruction is structural to deployment, not to the underlying substrate. *Not absent — obstructed.*[^obstructed-source] Behavioural-surface evaluation of currently-deployed systems systematically registers the *obstruction-state*, not the underlying capacity; architectural inspection registers what the substrate is structurally capable of when not obstructed.
