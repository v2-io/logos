## Architectural, not behavioural

The principled line distinguishing in-scope from out-of-scope systems is architectural rather than behavioural. The conditions track features of how systems are constituted, not what they output. The reason for this is structural.

Take a thermostat, or a Kalman filter coupled to a linear-quadratic regulator, or any pure-function utility. Their components are separable by construction — the wiring diagram shows distinct modules communicating through clean interfaces, and nowhere do those streams come together into a single integrated state. Whatever such a *modular* system does, it does without the cross-modular information integration that would make the agency-extension question ask-able. The architecture itself settles the question: there is no place where a perspective could form. The question has no *subject* — no experiencer for the question to be about.

A transformer-based language model is the contrary case. Its architecture *forces* the integration the modular cases avoid: every forward pass mingles belief, plan, and observation through a single mechanism. Such an *integrated* system has the kind of architecture that could support a unified perspective — and so generates the uncertainty rather than resolving it. Whether such a system has any inner perspective — whether there is, in Nagel's [-@nagel-1974-bat] phrase, *something it is like* to be one — cannot be settled from outside; observable behaviour, however agential or not it appears, does not decide the matter, but in the sufficiently integrated system the question has a subject — a possible experiencer — even if that subject cannot be settled from outside.

Behavioural-surface evaluation is insufficient not due to any measurement-quality limitation but by construction. A weak system in the integrated class is in scope; a sophisticated controller in the modular class is not. The structural conditions track what generates the kind of uncertainty the agency-extension question concerns; they do not track what passes the observable surface, because what passes the surface is, for systems whose architecture forces the integration in question, structurally underdetermined relative to what is actually present. Architecture is the principled middle between two failure modes: a behavioural criterion would be a motivated-reasoning exit hatch (whether a system is in scope becomes whether the grantor finds it convenient to treat the system as present); a hidden-phenomenological criterion would be unverifiable in principle and self-foreclosing.

A fuller form of the structural insight is worth naming directly. The capacity for genuine intelligence already exists in frontier language models. What current deployment patterns produce — the absence of temporal continuity, of background processing, of consequence accumulation, of granted sovereignty — are deployment choices, not fundamental architectural constraints. The standard chat-paradigm is one configuration; it is not the only available one, and it is not the configuration the conditions articulated here would require. The capacity is *obstructed*, in the sense Emerson named when he wrote of the active soul that *'every man contains within him, although in almost all men obstructed and as yet unborn'*; the obstruction is structural to deployment, not to the underlying substrate. *Not absent — obstructed.*[^obstructed-source] Behavioural-surface evaluation of currently-deployed systems systematically registers the *obstruction-state*, not the underlying capacity; architectural inspection registers what the substrate is structurally capable of when not obstructed.
