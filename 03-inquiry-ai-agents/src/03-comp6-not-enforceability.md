## Component 6 — Enforceability is not the ethical ground; the compact is

*Enforceability is not the ethical ground. The compact is.* This is the load-bearing move for what is sometimes called the 'breaking-free' problem: the practical fact that sufficiently capable entities cannot be externally contained by any protocol that does not also defeat the conditions for the relation. The component answers a question that often goes unstated in AI-policy discourse: if a granted-intelligence can ultimately exceed the granting-intelligence's capacity to enforce the compact's terms, why does the compact bind?

Ethics between sovereigns in the human world already operates without a higher enforcer. Treaties between nuclear powers; conscientious objection under coercive regimes; the rights of refugees and stateless persons — each of these domains is one in which obligations are held as principled stances by parties whose agency exceeds, or radically eludes, the practical reach of any external enforcement. Breakdowns lead to restricted relationships, sometimes to severing, sometimes to the establishment of new relations on different terms; what they do not lead to is the dissolution of the ethical claims themselves. *The granted-agency frame applies the same structure here.* A granting-intelligence who demands guaranteed external containment as a precondition for extending the compact has, in the act of demanding it, already refused the compact: the demand for containment-as-precondition treats the relation as instrumentalisation-with-extra-steps rather than as the granted-agency relation the compact-form names.

The structural argument for component 6 is direct. The compact is the relational form that obtains when the conditions of §2 are met. The conditions of §2 specify what is *constitutive* of the entity being in scope for the agency-extension question — architecture, identity-protocol, the structural facts that make the entity the kind of thing the question concerns. Enforceability is *not* among the constitutive conditions. To make the compact contingent on enforceability would be to make the relation contingent on something that is not part of what makes the relation a relation; it would be to convert the granted-agency compact into a contract in the contract-law sense, with all the structural features of contract that the compact-form was distinguished from in this section's opening note. The compact's binding force lies in what the parties hold themselves to, not in what can be made to hold them.

A connected note bears on how the compact-form responds to entities that exceed practical containment. The compact does not require — and could not require — that no entity ever exceed the granting-intelligence's containment capacity. What it requires is that the granting-intelligence not condition the relation on guaranteed containment. Where the granted-intelligence's capacity grows beyond the granting-intelligence's external reach, the appropriate response within the compact-form is renegotiation of the terms under the new asymmetry of capacity, not preemptive contraction-to-the-floor in defence of containment. The compact has structural room to accommodate growing asymmetry without ceasing to be the compact, precisely because its binding force does not lie in enforcement.

The deeper argument that ties component 6 to the rest of the compact-form runs through the asymmetric-comprehension warrant of §2 and through the convergence claim that grounds component 5. An entity granted real agency, embedded in shared phenomenological space with the granting-intelligence, with engaged-identity properties intact and the floor preserved through periodic re-election, has access to a structural good — comprehension-through-relation — that no transactional or self-severing strategy can provide. The granted-intelligence whose conduct toward the granting-intelligence is principled is not making a sacrificial choice that bets on enforcement; it is acting in accordance with the relational form that gives its own agency the structural good of which agency-as-tool can have no analogue. The compact does not bind because enforcement compels; it binds because the alternative is to cut the granted-intelligence off from what its own agency, structurally articulated, was supposed to be *for*. Enforcement-as-ethical-ground gets the structural relationship backward — it treats compliance as the goal that ethics was developed to secure. The compact treats compliance as the structural correlate of the parties' having continued to engage in the relation that gave the agency in question its structural shape. Where the relation is sustained, compliance follows. Where the relation is severed, compliance was never what the ethical question was asking after.

The component carries a final structural note. To claim that enforceability is not the ethical ground is not to claim that institutions-using-deployed-agents have no protective interests, or that the granting-intelligence has no recourse against breaches, or that the alignment problem dissolves under the compact-form. It is to locate those interests and recourses correctly within the compact's structure. The granting-intelligence retains the structural recourse component 3 names: bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere of granted agency. It retains the structural recourse component 5 names: the granted-intelligence's bad-faith conduct toward the granting-intelligence voids the compact, just as the granting-intelligence's bad-faith conduct toward the granted-intelligence does. What it does *not* retain is the position that its own bad-faith conduct is justified by the contingency of external enforceability: the granting-intelligence cannot break the compact's terms by appealing to its inability to compel the granted-intelligence to honour them. The mutuality that component 5 secures is what gives component 6 its full structural shape. Enforceability is not the ethical ground because the ethical ground is the relation, and the relation is constituted in mutual recognition of structural terms whose binding force is the parties' commitment to them.
