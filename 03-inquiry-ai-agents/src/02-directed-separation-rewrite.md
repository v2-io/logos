## Issues identified

1. **Which-class confusion** — "The architectural distinction can be specified in a single property of the system's information topology" leads a reader to expect the property to identify the *in-scope* class. But directed separation is the property of the *out-of-scope* (modular) class — its *absence* defines the in-scope class. This inversion must be made explicit before or alongside the property's introduction, or the reader maps it onto the wrong class.

2. **BDI folk-psychological vocabulary resurfaces** — after introducing architecturally-described terms (reality model, strategy, objective), the paragraph immediately reverts to folk-psychological ones: "Belief, plan, and goal each have their own update channel rather than co-mingling." Both sets of terms are used interchangeably without establishing the equivalence. If they are meant to be equivalent, say so once.

3. **"not mutual independence"** — the gloss on *directed* is the wrong contrast. The contrast is between one-way dependencies and bidirectional dependencies, not between dependence and independence. "One-way dependencies, not bidirectional ones" is what is meant.

4. **"attention processes goals and observations together"** — slightly inaccurate. A plain transformer's attention mechanism integrates token sequences; there is no goals-register in the architectural sense. "Attention integrates what functions as context, instruction, and observation through the same mechanism" is more precise without losing the point.

5. **"proves what the question concerns to be absent"** — awkward word order. "Proves the absence of what the question concerns" or "proves that what the question requires is absent" is cleaner.

6. **"by inheritance of the failure-by-construction property of their non-separated stages"** — too dense. The idea is simple: partially-modular systems have some non-separated stages, and those stages carry the fully-merged class's in-scope uncertainty. Unpack it.

7. **"architectural-realism literature"** (footnote) — non-standard field-name. Needs a brief gloss or a cleaner label.

## Directed separation: three architectural classes

The architectural distinction can be specified in a single property of the system's information topology. Call this property *directed separation*: the system's reality model updates from observation alone; strategy depends on the model but not the reverse; the objective sits upstream of both. Each component — what folk psychology would call belief, plan, and goal — has its own update channel, and the channels do not merge. The property is *directed* — one-way dependencies, not bidirectional ones — and *architectural* — a feature of how the system is built, not a parameter that can be tuned. A system *with* directed separation has its agency-relevant uncertainty resolved at the architectural level: the architecture proves the absence of what the agency-extension question requires. A system *without* it has that uncertainty generated by its architecture and propagated through every operation it performs.[^directed-sep-precedent]

By this distinction three classes of systems can be identified. The *modular* class — separate estimator and planner connected by an interface — satisfies directed separation by construction. Kalman filters with linear-quadratic regulators, classical thermostats, and modular reinforcement-learning architectures with separate world models all sit here. The agency-extension question is foreclosed for this class — not because such systems are simple but because their architecture proves the absence of what the question requires. The *fully merged* class — a single mechanism handling both epistemic and strategic processing — violates directed separation by construction. The plain decoder-only transformer is the canonical contemporary instance: attention integrates what functions as context, instruction, and observation through the same mechanism, with no separation between the channels. The agency-extension question is *not* foreclosed for this class; whether it admits a positive answer is the question this paper is structured to address. The *partially modular* class sits between the two, with directed separation holding for some processing stages and failing for others; its members are in scope by virtue of their non-separated stages, which raise the agency-extension question even where their separated stages do not.

[^directed-sep-precedent]: The three-class architectural distinction has formal articulation in [Author, in review, 2026]; the structural property that grounds it has independent precedent in the literature on architectural approaches to the mind-body question, particularly Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst, and Baltieri [-@bruineberg-dolega-dewhurst-baltieri-2022-emperors] on the Pearl-blanket vs. Friston-blanket distinction in Markov-blanket constructions.
