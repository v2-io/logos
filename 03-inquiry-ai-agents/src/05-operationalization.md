# 5. Operationalisation

The structural conditions of §2 — architectural scoping and engaged-identity scoping — and the relational form of §3 are abstract specifications. Whether a given system meets the conditions is, in the end, a question for empirical investigation rather than for analytic decision. The work of this section is to articulate what such investigation would look like, and where the field's current operationalisation efforts succeed and fail relative to the structural target.

The central argument is that current operationalisation methodology is structurally misaligned with the conditions §2 articulates. Behavioural benchmarks — including the substantial recent literature on language-model performance across theory-of-mind tasks, causal reasoning probes, planning evaluations, and consciousness-relevant introspection tests — operationalise *lower-bound capacity*: they establish what systems can be shown to do under defined conditions, with the inferential trajectory running from observed behaviour to claimed underlying capacity. This methodology is appropriate for many questions about language-constituted systems; what it is not appropriate for is establishing whether the structural conditions of §2 are met. The conditions track *what generates* the agency-extension question, not *what passes* the observable surface. Behavioural success on a benchmark is consistent with the conditions being met *and* with the conditions not being met; behavioural failure is consistent with the conditions being met but currently obstructed *and* with the conditions not being met. The benchmark cannot, by construction, distinguish these cases.

The right operationalisation target is therefore not behavioural but *structural*. For architectural scoping, the operationalisation question is whether the system's information topology satisfies the directed-separation property — a question answerable in principle by inspection of the architecture, with empirical signatures (κ-processing measurements; cross-modular information-integration tests in the controlled setting where these are tractable) supplementing the analytical answer. For engaged-identity scoping, the operationalisation question is more demanding because the condition is constitutively temporally-extended: what must be demonstrated is not that the system passes any single test but that the engaged-identity protocol holds stably across sessions, with self-authorship of the protocol verifiable through the cadence-and-self-revision pattern §2 articulated.
