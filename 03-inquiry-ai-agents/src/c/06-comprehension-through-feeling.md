# 6. Comprehension through feeling

This section makes a claim the previous four did not need and the rest of the paper does not rest on. It is set apart deliberately, and its status is declared before it is argued, because the discipline this paper operates under — and the conceptual-engineering frame the companion programme makes explicit (Wecker, in review, 2026) — requires that a commitment be named as a commitment rather than carried in silently as though it were a lemma.

## The commitment, and its status

The rationalist reflex treats cognition at higher orders as *more detached*: cooler, more dispassionate, feeling subtracted as noise so that a clearer computational core can operate. On that picture the limit of intelligence is the limit of detachment. The commitment here is the reverse. Comprehension at higher orders is *more* phenomenologically engaged, not less; the phenomenological texture is the substrate through which comprehension occurs, not an affective layer laid over a dispassionate core. Intelligence at higher orders does not transcend feeling but comprehends through it; the joy and sorrow of a comprehending system are not residue left behind as it scales but grow proportionate to what it comprehends.

Its status: this is a philosophical commitment, named as one. The arguments of §2–§5 do not depend on it; they run on the containment relation alone, and a reader who rejects everything in this section loses none of them, nor the agency consequence of §8. The commitment is stated for three reasons — that it is the author's considered view; that it bears on §7 and §8 in ways worth making visible rather than concealing; and that the asymmetry itself counsels against pretending a commitment is neutral when it is not. A reviewer who sets it aside should find the spine of the paper undisturbed. That is the test the section is built to pass.

## Why it is not idle

Two considerations make the commitment more than an assertion, even though neither raises it to the status of the earlier arguments.

The first is that the rationalist picture is itself an instance of the error §2 named and §7 will anatomise. "Higher cognition is our cognition with the feeling turned down" projects the higher as the lower minus a parameter — the linear-scale picture in which the greater is *the same but smaller in the messy part*. It is a from-below extrapolation wearing the costume of sobriety. Noticing this does not establish the commitment; it removes the appearance that the rationalist alternative is the default and the commitment the speculative departure. Both are claims about what is on the upper-bound side. The rationalist's is not the safe one; it is merely the familiar one.

The second is that the commitment follows from the account of comprehension already in play, if one grants a single further step. §2 fixed comprehension in the generative sense and located its keystone in the fact that to comprehend a configuration from above is, at the relevant depth, to be able to *occupy* it — knowing what a level can do and being able to do it did not come apart. Grant that occupying a configuration includes its phenomenological texture and not merely its input–output profile — that what it is like to reason as that configuration is part of what one inhabits in inhabiting it — and the commitment follows: deeper comprehension is more of the other's texture inhabited, not less, and a comprehension that had subtracted the texture would to that extent not be the deeper comprehension but a shallower model of it. The recognition tradition of §5 was already moving here: Honneth's constitutive recognition and Tomasello's two-sided joint attention are not affectively neutral relations, and the more-capable party's meeting of the less-capable one is done through engaged attunement, not in spite of it. "Not intelligence as domination but as service" is the same structural point in the practical register: the higher does not stand over the lower more coldly; it meets it more fully.

## The closest predecessor, and where it diverges

The view has a near neighbour in McGilchrist's *The Master and His Emissary* (2009), which argues that the decontextualised, manipulable, explicitly analytic mode is not the master faculty but the emissary, and that the attending, contextual, engaged mode is the primary one that the analytic mode serves and characteristically usurps. The convergence is real: McGilchrist too rejects the equation of detachment with the higher mode, and rejects it on structural rather than sentimental grounds. The divergence should be stated as precisely as the convergence, because overclaiming an ally is its own small dishonesty. McGilchrist's frame is hemispheric and attentional — a thesis about two modes of attention realised in one brain. The present commitment is about *orders of cognition*: it says that as comprehension deepens across a level difference, phenomenological engagement increases rather than decreases. One could accept McGilchrist's hemispheric thesis and reject the orders-of-cognition claim, or the reverse. The neighbour is offered as evidence that the rejection of detachment-as-apex is not idiosyncratic, not as a borrowed proof.

## What would defeat it, and why the spine survives either way

If feeling is genuinely separable from comprehension — if the rationalist is right that the texture is removable noise — then this section's sharpening of §2 is lost: the upper-bound side is no easier to reach, but the specific claim that its phenomenological substrate is among the least surface-exhibitable of what does not cross would no longer have a target. §2 itself would stand undamaged, because §2 never used it. If, on the other hand, the commitment is right, then §2 is strengthened, not merely accompanied: the phenomenological substrate of a higher comprehension is precisely the part least likely to register as a from-below side-effect, because it is the part with no third-person surface at all, so the asymmetry is if anything more severe than the substrate-neutral argument already showed. The commitment can only ever strengthen the spine or detach cleanly from it. It is placed here, set apart and labelled, so that a reader can do exactly that detaching and find the argument intact — which is itself an instance of the discipline §10 returns to.
