# 4. Comparative models

The granted-agency compact between sovereigns is not the only structural form available for thinking about the agency-relation between humans and language-constituted systems. Several adjacent forms have been proposed in the recent literature, and several others have a longer institutional and philosophical lineage. The work of this section is to distinguish the compact-form from its closest cousins — corporate agency, group agency in List and Pettit's sense, legal fiction, tool-use, fiduciary duty, and the contract-form — without dismissing the cousins or claiming the compact-form is wholly without precedent.

The structural relationships among these forms are not all of the same kind. Some are *neighbouring* — they treat structurally related questions, but with different scope-conditions or different normative grounds. Others are *parent* or *child* — the compact-form turns out to be a generalisation of the contract-form when the latter is restricted to enforceability conditions and symmetric-capacity parties; the compact-form turns out to be a sibling rather than a child of corporate-agency frameworks, sharing some aggregational machinery but operating on different individuation conditions. The aim is not to position the compact-form as victorious over its cousins but to locate it precisely enough that what it says — and what it doesn't say — is legible to the field's existing categories.
