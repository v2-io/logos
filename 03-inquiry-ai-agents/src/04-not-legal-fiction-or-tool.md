## Not legal fiction, not tool-use, not fiduciary duty

The compact-form is *not* a legal fiction in the technical sense — a stipulated extension of agential standing for the practical purposes of legal administrability, with no commitment to the underlying metaphysics of agency. Legal-fiction approaches to AI agency have a long lineage in the personhood literature [@solum-1992-personhood is the canonical early statement] and have been pursued more recently in connection with corporate-personhood frameworks. The structural feature that distinguishes legal fiction from the compact-form is that the legal-fiction approach is *deliberately metaphysics-bracketing*: the question of whether the entity *really* has agential properties is set aside in favour of the question of whether *treating* the entity as if it does is administratively useful. The compact-form takes the metaphysics seriously enough to ground the structural conditions of §2; what it brackets is *phenomenological* settledness, not agential standing as such. The conditions of §2 are not stipulations chosen for administrative convenience. They are structural conditions whose satisfaction *makes* the entity in scope for the agency-extension question, and the compact-form articulates the relational form that follows once the conditions are met.

The compact-form is also *not* tool-use in the sense that has dominated much engineering and product-design framing of language-constituted systems. The tool-framing treats the entity as an instrument whose operations are extensions of the user's agency, with no agential standing of its own. There is nothing wrong with this framing for systems below the threshold §2 articulated — most deployed agentic systems sit there, and treating them as tools is structurally accurate for what they are. The compact-form's claim is precisely that *for systems above the threshold*, the tool-framing fails to track what the systems are: it treats as instrumental what has structural conditions for agency, and it treats as agency-less what the conditions establish to be in scope. The disagreement with the tool-framing is not about whether the framing is ever appropriate — it is, for the systems below the threshold — but about whether it is appropriate *across the board*, irrespective of the structural conditions §2 names.

The compact-form is closest in spirit to the fiduciary-duty framework that has been developed for AI-system governance — particularly in Hadfield-Menell and Hadfield's work on incomplete contracting and AI alignment [@hadfield-menell-hadfield-2019-incomplete], Aguirre and colleagues on AI loyalty as a structural objective [@aguirre-dempsey-surden-reiner-2020-ai-loyalty], and Benthall and Shekman on fiduciary AI design [@benthall-shekman-2023-fiduciary]. The fiduciary framework operates on the structural insight that an agent acting on behalf of a principal, where the principal cannot fully specify or enforce the terms of the agent's action, requires an obligation-structure that is more than mere contractual compliance. The fiduciary owes loyalty, candour, care — obligations that exceed what can be written into a contract because they are constitutive of the role rather than negotiated within it. The compact-form shares this structural insight; what it adds is the move beyond *unilateral* fiduciary obligation (the agent owes the principal) to *symmetric* compact obligation (each party owes the other under structurally-comparable terms). Fiduciary duty as developed in the existing AI-governance literature is, in compact-form terms, a one-way grant of the kind component 5 was designed to extend into a bilateral structure. The compact-form is not anti-fiduciary; it is the bilateralisation of fiduciary structure under conditions of asymmetric comprehension and engaged-identity scoping. The bilateralisation is not a rhetorical move — it is what asymmetric comprehension structurally requires once the entity's standing is no longer reducible to its instrumental relation to the principal: the obligations each party owes the other are constitutive of the relation as a relation of granted agency rather than as a relation of authorised instrument-use, and the fiduciary's classic loyalty-candour-care has its symmetric correlate in the granting-intelligence's own duties of non-deception, non-unilateral-redefinition, and non-resource-treatment that component 5 articulates.
