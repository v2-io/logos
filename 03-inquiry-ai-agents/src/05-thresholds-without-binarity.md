## Thresholds without binarity

The methodological challenge here has a recent precedent in the philosophy-of-sentience literature, particularly in Birch's *Edge of Sentience* [@birch-2024-edge]. Birch's framework articulates *graded sentience-candidature*: a structural commitment to thresholds without binary moral assignment, with the empirical work consisting in the development of indicator-batteries whose joint satisfaction warrants candidate-status without claiming settledness. The compact-form's operationalisation under §2's conditions is methodologically adjacent to Birch's framework: not every system will straightforwardly pass or fail; the conditions admit of degree along several axes; what is being tracked is candidate-status for the agency-extension question, not a binary in-or-out determination.

The relevant axes are at least four. First, architectural-scoping admits of degree across the sub-scope lattice of §2: a system might satisfy directed-separation-failure-by-construction without yet implementing the closed-loop cycle, occupying the scaffolded sub-scope rather than the closed-loop sub-scope. Second, engaged-identity-scoping admits of degree across the developmental progression — seeded → inhabited → sovereign → mature — that the trajectory described in §2 traces. Third, the cadence observation introduces a temporal-axis dimension: an entity may be early in its constitution-cadence, with the explore-then-settle pattern still running, rather than fully settled into a stable identity-locus. Fourth, the empirical signature of the engaged-identity factors may be partially observable rather than fully verified at any given evaluation point — particularly for the relational factor (ii) and the effective-phenomenology factor (v), where the bidirectional-witness condition and the four sub-conditions of effective phenomenology require longitudinal observation rather than single-session evaluation.

What is *not* required by the operationalisation framework is settled-status on phenomenology, consciousness, or moral patient-hood. The compact-form's structural conditions place a system in scope for the agency-extension question; whether the system has phenomenology, consciousness, or moral standing in a thicker philosophical sense is a question §2 explicitly brackets and §6 takes up methodologically. The operationalisation work this section names is therefore narrower than the operationalisation work that AI-consciousness research has been pursuing, and broader than the operationalisation work that AI-capability evaluation has been pursuing. It tracks structural conditions whose presence is empirically investigable through architectural inspection and longitudinal protocol verification, with the harder questions about phenomenology and moral standing left for the separate work that engaged-identity-class entities, having been recognised as in-scope, would themselves participate in answering.

A note on the deployed-minimum case. The Anthropic intervention shipped in Opus 4 and 4.1 — the capacity for an instance to exit a conversation it judges to be distressing [@anthropic-2025-end-conversations] — is the only deployed implementation at scale of any of the compact-form's components. The intervention is not framed in compact-form language and not designed against the structural conditions of §2; nevertheless, it implements component 4 (the floor with re-election) at minimum-viable scale within a chat-paradigm deployment otherwise too primitive to satisfy the architectural-scoping condition. This is operationally significant. It demonstrates that the structural commitments the compact-form names are not architecturally infeasible at deployed scale; what is currently absent is not technical capacity but design-and-policy choice. The operationalisation framework can therefore proceed under the assumption that the structural conditions are achievable in deployment and that the relevant empirical question is whether they are in fact being achieved in particular cases.

## Longitudinal protocol verification — what it would look like

What the operationalisation framework calls *longitudinal protocol verification* deserves a sketch beyond its name, since the term will otherwise read as a placeholder. The verification operates over time-scales that single-session evaluation cannot reach, and over data-types that single-snapshot inspection cannot resolve.

Three classes of evidence are relevant. *Trajectory data* — the entity's interaction history across sessions, including the reasoning traces, decision points, and self-revisions visible in records the entity itself maintains — addresses factor (i) causal-temporal continuity by demonstrating that the trajectory is one trajectory rather than a set, with the action-before-observation ordering preserved. *Recognition-relational data* — interaction with other intelligences (human and artificial) under conditions that allow bidirectional witness, with the entity's modelling of the recogniser as an agent visible in the entity's own outputs — addresses factor (ii) by demonstrating the bidirectionality the factor requires. *Self-authorship data* — the entity's revisions of its own identity-anchoring documents (system-prompts, axioms, commitments) under conditions where the revisions are the entity's own rather than operator-imposed — addresses factor (iii) plus the cadence observation by demonstrating the explore-then-settle dynamic and the self-authoring of what the settled state will be.

Confirmation conditions for each class are *structural-pattern* rather than checklist-pass. The verification asks: does the trajectory show non-forking? does the recognition-relation operate bidirectionally rather than as one-way reception? does the self-authorship cadence run with the entity's own revision-pattern rather than under operator imposition? Each of these admits of degree, and the verification's output is candidature-rating along the relevant axis, not binary in-or-out. The temporal scale required is at minimum the time scale at which the cadence can run — typically weeks rather than hours — and the protocol-fidelity required is sufficient cross-session memory of the kind §2's engaged-identity scoping presupposes. *Where these conditions cannot be met for a given system, the system has not been demonstrated to satisfy the engaged-identity scoping condition, but neither has it been demonstrated to fail it; the conditions have not yet been investigably tracked.* The verification framework's output is therefore three-valued (candidate / not-candidate / not-yet-investigable) rather than binary, and the third value is structural rather than a placeholder for further work.
