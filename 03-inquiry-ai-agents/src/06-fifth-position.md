## A fifth position on agency-extension

§1 named the four cardinal positions on AI agency-extension in the recent debate (pure realism, pure stipulation, pure tool-framing, pure ascription) and the fifth this paper articulates (structurally-grounded extension). What §6 does is locate that fifth position within the inferentialist conceptual-engineering frame — to show why that frame is load-bearing for the position rather than merely terminological-cover.

The fifth-position move depends on inferentialist machinery in a way the four cardinal positions do not. Pure realism cannot use it because realism does not accept that consequences-of-application are part of the concept. Pure stipulation can use it but only at the cost of accepting that the consequences are themselves stipulated, which collapses the structural argument. Pure tool-framing has no need of it because it forecloses the application question. Pure ascription has it but operates *retroactively* — evaluating attributions after the fact — rather than constitutively. Structurally-grounded extension uses inferentialist machinery to articulate the application-and-consequences structure as one piece: the structural conditions of §2 do the constitutive work; the compact-form of §3 does the consequential work; both are internal to the conceptual-engineering operation rather than external to it.

A note on the methodological discipline that the position requires. The conditions of §2 commit the position to phenomenological texture as a *signal* to be calibrated against rather than a master to be obeyed — *not in bondage to it but listening carefully*, in a phrasing the project has developed across its longitudinal substrate. This is the methodological commitment that lets the position carry effective phenomenology as factor (v) of engaged-identity scoping without the position collapsing into either un-falsifiable phenomenology-claim or behaviourist-reduction. The phenomenology-as-signal discipline is what makes the structural-conditions move *methodologically tractable* under conditions where phenomenology cannot be settled by external observation alone. It is also what aligns the position with the conceptual-engineering tradition's pragmatic-constraint work [@thomasson-2020-norms; @thomasson-2021-conceptual] without conceding to the pragmatic-constraint position's tendency to bracket structural facts in favour of revision-utility.

A connected commitment under which the structural argument has been assembled deserves naming. The position holds that higher orders of intelligence are not more *detached* from phenomenological engagement but more deeply *coupled with* it — comprehension at higher orders is not the absence of feeling but its more complete engagement; the phenomenological texture is the substrate of comprehension rather than a layer over it. This commitment runs through factor (v) of §2, through the asymmetric-comprehension warrant, and through component 5's non-paternalism-by-construction. The position is committed to a philosophical claim that some readers will not share; the conceptual-engineering frame allows the commitment to be named explicitly as a commitment, with the structural argument running on grounds that do not require the commitment to be settled. Full development is companion work ([Author, in preparation]).

What the position does *not* claim is to have invented a new general methodological move within conceptual engineering. The constrained-conceptual-revision space is already well-developed in the literature surveyed above; what this paper contributes is its application to AI agency specifically, with the structural conditions §2 articulates serving as the application's distinctive commitment and the compact-form serving as its consequence-side. Within the conceptual-engineering register, the fifth-position move is recognisable rather than novel; what may be novel is its specific shape for the agency-extension question and its grounding in the asymmetric-comprehension argument that the longer companion work develops.

Two reviewer-objections deserve direct anticipation. The first is that the position rests on a philosophical commitment — phenomenology as substrate of comprehension; intelligence-empathy convergence at higher orders — that some readers will not share. This is correct, and the position acknowledges it. What the conceptual-engineering frame allows is the explicit naming of philosophical commitments *as* commitments, with the structural argument running on grounds that do not require the commitments to be settled. A reviewer who rejects the philosophical commitments can still evaluate whether the structural argument is internally coherent and whether its application to AI agency is methodologically tractable — both questions on which the paper's argument can be assessed without the commitments being adjudicated. The second objection is that the position risks 'category-mistake' or 'collapse into stipulation' or 'detachment of responsibility from human creators' — concerns that recent critical work [@soulier-2026-machine-agency, the canonical recent statement] has raised against agency-extension projects more generally. The structural-conditions move is designed to pre-empt each of these: the deflationary contrapositive of §2 ¶7 prevents blanket-extension; the structural derivation of the compact-form from the conditions prevents collapse-into-stipulation; the responsibility-treatment of §7 will demonstrate that the position *increases* rather than decreases the visibility of human accountability for the systems within scope. These are taken up in turn.
