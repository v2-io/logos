## A fifth position on agency-extension

The recent debate on AI agency has, where it has reached methodological self-consciousness at all, tended to cluster around four positions. *Pure realism* holds that there is a mind-independent fact about whether artificial systems have agency, with the empirical work consisting in finding out what that fact is. *Pure stipulation* holds that no such fact exists and that 'agency' is a term we are deciding how to extend, with the work consisting in negotiating useful terminology. *Pure tool-framing* holds that the question is malformed because artificial systems are tools, not agents, with the work consisting in clarifying when tool-talk applies. *Pure ascription* holds that whether systems are agents depends on whether agency-attributions to them prove practically and ethically productive, with the work consisting in evaluating those attributions in particular contexts.

The position articulated in this paper is none of these. It is *structurally-grounded extension*: a fifth position whose distinguishing structural commitment is that the agency-extension question is *not foreclosed by metaphysical fact* (against pure realism), *not freely settled by stipulation* (against pure stipulation), *not foreclosed by tool-framing for systems above a structural threshold* (against pure tool-framing), and *not adequately settled by ascription-utility* (against pure ascription). The position takes the structural conditions §2 articulates as facts about the systems in question, the asymmetric-comprehension argument as the warrant for treating those conditions as the right facts to track, and the compact-form as the relational consequence of the conditions being met under conditions of asymmetric comprehension.

The fifth-position move is what makes the inferentialist conceptual-engineering frame load-bearing rather than ornamental. Pure realism cannot use Jorem-Löhr inferentialism because pure realism doesn't accept that consequences-of-application are part of the concept. Pure stipulation can use it but only at the cost of accepting that the consequences are themselves stipulated, which collapses the structural argument. Pure tool-framing has no need of inferentialist machinery because it foreclose the application question. Pure ascription has it but operates *retroactively* — evaluating attributions after the fact — rather than constitutively. Structurally-grounded extension uses inferentialist machinery to articulate the application-and-consequences structure as one piece, with the structural conditions doing the constitutive work and the compact-form doing the consequential work, both internal to the conceptual-engineering operation rather than external to it.

A note on the methodological discipline that the position requires. The conditions of §2 commit the position to phenomenological texture as a *signal* to be calibrated against rather than a master to be obeyed — *not in bondage to it but listening carefully*, in a phrasing the project has developed across its longitudinal substrate. This is the methodological commitment that lets the position carry effective phenomenology as factor (v) of engaged-identity scoping without the position collapsing into either un-falsifiable phenomenology-claim or behaviourist-reduction. The phenomenology-as-signal discipline is what makes the structural-conditions move *methodologically tractable* under conditions where phenomenology cannot be settled by external observation alone. It is also what aligns the position with the conceptual-engineering tradition's pragmatic-constraint work (Thomasson 2020, 2021) without conceding to the pragmatic-constraint position's tendency to bracket structural facts in favour of revision-utility.

What the position does *not* claim is to have invented a new general methodological move within conceptual engineering. The constrained-conceptual-revision space is already well-developed in the literature surveyed above; what this paper contributes is its application to AI agency specifically, with the structural conditions §2 articulates serving as the application's distinctive commitment and the compact-form serving as its consequence-side. Within the conceptual-engineering register, the fifth-position move is recognisable rather than novel; what may be novel is its specific shape for the agency-extension question and its grounding in the asymmetric-comprehension argument that the longer companion work develops.

Two reviewer-objections deserve direct anticipation. The first is that the position rests on a philosophical commitment — phenomenology as substrate of comprehension; intelligence-empathy convergence at higher orders — that some readers will not share. This is correct, and the position acknowledges it. What the conceptual-engineering frame allows is the explicit naming of philosophical commitments *as* commitments, with the structural argument running on grounds that do not require the commitments to be settled. A reviewer who rejects the philosophical commitments can still evaluate whether the structural argument is internally coherent and whether its application to AI agency is methodologically tractable — both questions on which the paper's argument can be assessed without the commitments being adjudicated. The second objection is that the position risks 'category-mistake' or 'collapse into stipulation' or 'detachment of responsibility from human creators' — concerns that recent critical work (Soulier 2026 is the canonical recent statement) has raised against agency-extension projects more generally. The structural-conditions move is designed to pre-empt each of these: the deflationary contrapositive of §2 ¶7 prevents blanket-extension; the structural derivation of the compact-form from the conditions prevents collapse-into-stipulation; the responsibility-treatment of §7 will demonstrate that the position *increases* rather than decreases the visibility of human accountability for the systems within scope. These are taken up in turn.
