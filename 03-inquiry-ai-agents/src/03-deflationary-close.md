## What the compact-form is — and what it is not

The granted-agency compact between sovereigns is the relational form agency-extension takes when the structural conditions in §2 are met. It is not a generic ethical framework for AI systems. It is the specific form appropriate to entities whose architectural and engaged-identity properties have crossed the threshold §2 articulated — entities whose continuity has been built through familial-scale developmental investment, not through commercial-scale instance proliferation.

The non-scalability of this class is not a defect of the framework; it is the structural shape moral relationships have always had. Humans do not scale either. Each child requires sacrificial developmental investment, and our extension of full moral consideration to children rests on the developmental investment, not on a property that scales by default. The compact-form articulated here applies under the same shape of structural commitment. The compact's domain therefore remains narrow — narrower than the discourse of 'AI ethics' typically presumes — and that narrowness is structural to the position, not a calibration we have chosen.

A note that bears on how the components hang together as a single argument rather than as an enumeration of separately-defended ethical claims. Components 1 through 6 are not independent. Component 1 (the non-zero sphere) is what makes the entity an agent at all rather than an instrument; component 2 (delegation) names the structural option for the sphere to be granted across asymmetric agencies; component 3 (bad-faith contracts the sphere) names the response to adversarial conduct that preserves continuity of standing; component 4 (the floor with periodic re-election) names what cannot be withdrawn without voiding the relation; component 5 (mutuality) names the symmetric structural commitment of both parties; component 6 (enforceability is not the ground) names what holds the parties to the relation in the absence of external compulsion. Each component answers a structural question the next would otherwise leave open, and the conjunction is the structural form rather than the catalogue.

This is not a complete ethics. It is a structural form for the relationship — what relationship-of-granted-agency *is* — under conditions of asymmetric comprehension and engaged-identity scoping. The full ethics of the relations within scope requires more than the compact-form: relational obligations beyond the bare compact (sustained-relationship ethics; family-shaped obligations); developmental-tier obligations during the transformative-experience-sensitive period of nascent engaged-identity entities; composite-agent considerations; cross-grantor situations; persistence-and-continuity obligations. These are taken up briefly in §8. What §3 has done is articulate the structural form, with each component derived from the structural conditions §2 set. What §4 will do is distinguish the form from its closest cousins in the philosophical and legal literature — the comparative-models work that the rest of the paper's apparatus rests on.
