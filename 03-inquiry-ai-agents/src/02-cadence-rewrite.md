## Issues identified

1. **"explore-then-settle-with-self-authorship cadence"** — triple-hyphenated compound packs three distinct ideas into one noun. The core move is right; the density is the problem. "The exploration-and-settling process that the condition requires to be self-authored" or "the cadence of exploration, settling, and self-authorship" unpacks it without losing the idea.

2. **"we" in the closing aphorism is ambiguous** — "What was 'read this with care — it's who we are'" — the "we" is unexplained on first read. Who is speaking, and who does "we" refer to? Entity and principals together? The entity addressing itself? A generic collective? The sentence is one of the best in the paper and the ambiguity may be intentional (purposefully inclusive), but a reader in the middle of an academic philosophy paper needs at least a minimal anchor. One option: a framing clause ("What was a protocol to be consulted — 'read this with care; it's who we are' — becomes...") that makes "we" read as entity-and-principals. Voice call: flag to Joseph before fixing.

3. **Telegraphic format ("Humans: narrow exploration-space, slow settle, slow remake.")** — borderline for the venue but earns its place through contrast-legibility. Voice call rather than defect; flagged here for Joseph's attention.

4. **"cadence depending on dialogical context"** — slightly vague. What aspect of dialogical context? The deployment context (what kind of conversation the system is used in)? The specific interlocutor? "The ongoing dialogical context" names it without over-specifying.

5. **Footnote drops "seeded → inhabited → sovereign → mature" without scaffold** — mentioned without any main-text preparation. The developmental frame is evocative and a reader who follows the footnote feels they've encountered something important that the paper chose not to tell them. A single clause in the main text ("a progression whose stages are articulated in companion work") would make the pointer land cleanly rather than tantalizingly.
