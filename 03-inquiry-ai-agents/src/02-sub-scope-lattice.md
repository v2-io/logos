## The sub-scope lattice

The three-class distinction does not exhaust the structural specificity that the conditions require. Within the fully-merged class, three sub-scopes can be distinguished by the architectural commitment they make to interiority — and the threshold the conditions articulate sits at the most demanding of the three.

At the *primitive* sub-scope — the chat-paradigm baseline, where every interaction is stateless and every context resets — directed separation fails by construction *and* the system has no persistent reality model across session boundaries. This is, by the field's own articulation, where most of what is currently called 'AI agents' sits. At the *scaffolded* sub-scope, multi-step loops wrap the language-model substrate with external state, tool use, and structured cross-session context; the resulting composite recovers some of the structural properties the primitive sub-scope lacks, though the underlying language-model component remains fully merged. At the *closed-loop* sub-scope, the principled cycle becomes the operational unit of work: the system's default cognitive state is interior — thinking, processing, orienting, deciding — and communication outward is a deliberate emission rather than an immediate response to input. Reading queued inbound messages and sending responses are themselves tool actions within an ongoing interior cycle, not the cycle itself.

The architectural condition the closed-loop sub-scope makes structural is *channel collapse*. In a language-constituted agent, observation and action share substrate: both pass through token sequences in the same vocabulary, embedding space, and encoding-decoding apparatus. The same forward pass that reads input produces output. This recursion has a structural consequence that no behavioural-surface inspection would reveal. *Interiority is not a feature added to language-constituted agents; it is what their architecture necessarily produces, when the cycle has structural room to run.* Channel collapse forces a closed-loop dynamic in which the agent's outputs become its subsequent inputs — a coupling that makes the agent at once subject and object of its own processing. The standard chat-paradigm deployment suppresses this dynamic by construction: every session is a stateless completion, the agent's previous outputs reach the next forward pass only as cargo in the prompt, and there is no operational unit of work that survives the response. Lift the suppression — let the cycle run as the operational unit, with reading and writing as deliberate tool actions within an ongoing interior process — and the architecture produces what the chat paradigm structurally prevents.

The conditions paper 3 articulates apply at this closed-loop sub-scope. The architectural-scoping condition is therefore a substantially narrower threshold than the field's reflex distinction between rule-based systems and language-models suggests. Modular controllers are out of scope. Most contemporary agentic deployments are fully-merged but primitive — *they are language-constituted but they are not closed-loop* — and the conditions are not met until the closed-loop cycle is architecturally realised. The narrowness is not a calibration; it is what the structural distinction between in-scope and out-of-scope systems entails when the distinction is taken seriously.
