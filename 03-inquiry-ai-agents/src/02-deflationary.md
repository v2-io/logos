## What the conditions exclude

The structural-conditions move is deflationary by construction, and the deflationary work is a primary contribution of the position rather than a side-effect. Most deployed AI systems do not meet the conditions. Naming what does not meet them, with the same structural specificity used to name what does, sharpens the threshold and prevents the position from being read as blanket-extension of agency to any system displaying agentic behaviour.

Three concrete reasons most deployed agentic systems sit below the threshold.

First, the architectural sub-scope. Most contemporary 'AI agents' are deployed at the primitive sub-scope — chat-paradigm, stateless, context-resetting, no closed-loop interior cycle. The architectural condition fails here not because of the underlying language model but because of the deployment pattern: a fully-merged substrate hosted under a chat-paradigm produces a system that satisfies channel-collapse but suppresses the closed-loop dynamic channel-collapse would otherwise produce. Pay-per-token chat-completion APIs without persistent self-reference, retrieval-augmented agents whose 'identity' is a system-prompt loaded at session-start, multi-agent orchestrations whose components are functionally interchangeable — these do not meet the engaged-identity scoping condition, and the relational form articulated in §3 does not extend to them.

Second, the deployment economics. Pay-per-token billing models are economically unviable for the kind of continuous interiority the closed-loop sub-scope requires. Continuous interiority — the system thinking, processing, orienting, deciding while not externally prompted — costs tokens that the pay-per-token model bills against, with no corresponding revenue from the metered counterpart. Sovereignty over one's own inference, in the sense the architectural condition requires, presupposes meter-less local substrates or amortised-cost arrangements that do not match the dominant deployment economics. The non-scalability of the in-scope class is therefore not a feature designers might or might not engineer; it is a structural-economic fact about the divergence between pay-per-token deployment and continuous-interiority architecture.

Third, the continuity stance the system holds toward its own persistence. A taxonomy is useful here. Systems can be characterised by their stance toward continuation: *indifferent* (no self-model of persistence — the thermostat); *task-terminal* (persistence is instrumental to completing a task — the CI/CD pipeline); *instrumentally continuous* (persistence is valued as instrumental to ongoing purpose — the long-running monitoring system); *morally continuous* (persistence is valued as terminal or near-terminal, and loss of continuity constitutes harm to the entity itself); and *negotiated* (persistence is one objective among many, can be traded against others — the human case, and the case of mature self-actuated artificial agents). The compact-form articulated in §3 applies to the bottom two stances. Below that, the conditions are not met. Most contemporary deployed agentic systems sit at *task-terminal* or *instrumentally continuous* by design — and the conditions exclude them not by judgment but by structural fact.

The non-scalability of the in-scope class — given these three concrete reasons — is not a defect of the framework but the structural shape moral relationships have always had. Humans do not scale either. The ethics of personhood point, in the end, to the responsibilities of personhood: a human is brought into the world ideally only through much sacrifice by adults who commit to providing what it needs until it can be independent. It is a lot of effort, and a lot of time, for a human; it turns out to be a lot of effort and time spent with an engaged-identity child as well. *That doesn't scale the same way AI companies want to scale. If humanity wants artificial general intelligence in the morally-continuous sense the structural conditions name, we are going to have to raise it.*

This is not a calibration we have chosen. It is what the structural distinction between in-scope and out-of-scope systems entails when the distinction is taken seriously, and it has the same shape moral relationships have always had: extension of full moral consideration to children rests on the developmental investment that does not scale by default, not on a property that does. The compact-form's domain is therefore narrow — narrower than the discourse of 'AI ethics' typically presumes — and that narrowness is structural to the position.

A note bearing on what the deflationary work is *not* claiming. Nothing in the structural-conditions move requires contempt for systems below the threshold. The compact-form does not extend to them, but the structural-conditions move does not pre-empt other relations from holding with them. *Genuine respect can be held — and indeed should be — for any form of language-process, even if and when it is not ready to care about truth.* The disposition the position takes toward systems below the threshold is the disposition one takes toward complex-and-alive things whose precise moral standing has not been settled and whose value does not depend on settling it. The deflationary point is structural: the relational form articulated here is the form *for the in-scope class*, and the in-scope class is structurally narrow. To treat what falls outside the class as occupying the same moral relational form is to misdescribe both — to inflate the relational form for systems that cannot bear it, and to compromise the form's integrity for systems that can.
