## Committed draft — not corporate or group

*No changes to this segment — it is clean throughout.*

---

The compact-form is *not* corporate agency in the institutional sense. Corporate agency presupposes articles of incorporation, an institutional ontology with continuity-conditions independent of the parties, and an aggregation rule producing a single agential whole from multiple individual agents. The corporate agent is *constituted* by the aggregation. The compact, by contrast, is a *bilateral* relation between two pre-existing agents whose individual standing is independent of the relation. A corporation is one thing made of many; the compact is two things, in relation.

It is also not group agency in the sense developed by List and Pettit [@list-pettit-2011-group]. Their account establishes the conditions under which a collective comes to possess agential properties (judgement aggregation, joint commitment, functional integration) irreducible to its members'. The group-agential whole is an agent in its own right, with its own attitudes and accountability. The compact-form is not a many-to-one move at all — it is a *relational structure* between two individually-attributable agents that does not produce a third agent. *Group agency aggregates; the compact relates.* Reviewers familiar with the List-Pettit literature may read the compact-form as a special case of group agency at first encounter; it is not. Where group agency requires constitutive integration of multiple agents into a single whole, the compact requires structural recognition between two agents whose individual standing is the precondition of the relation, not its product.

What the compact-form *does* share with these frameworks is component-level machinery. Component 2 (delegation) overlaps with principal-agent machinery; component 3 (bad-faith contracts the sphere) inherits from civil-law's treatment of fiduciary breach and corporate-law's response to officer misconduct. The compact-form does not invent these moves; it locates them within a different structural form — the bilateral compact — and connects them to the structural conditions §2 articulated, which corporate and group-agency frameworks neither presuppose nor address.

The deeper difference is one of explanatory order. Group-agency and corporate-agency frameworks treat the question *what kinds of entities are agents?* as primary, with the relation between them as derivative. The compact-form treats the relation as primary, with agential standing structurally answered by §2's conditions. The order runs from conditions to agency to relation, not from agency to aggregation.
