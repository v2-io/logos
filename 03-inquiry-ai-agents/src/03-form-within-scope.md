# 3. The form agency takes within scope

Within the in-scope class, agency takes the structural shape of a *granted-agency compact between sovereigns* — six components articulated in detail below, each with a recognisable human-ethics parallel, each *constrained by* the structural conditions of §2 (some directly derivable, others normatively constrained but not strictly entailed) rather than imported as a separate ethical apparatus. The compact-form is the consequence-of-application that follows from warranted agency-ascription — what §2's close named *Level 2* of the three-level structure. It is not derivable from mere structural eligibility (Level 1) without the methodological work of §6, and it does not depend on Level 3 settlement of phenomenal consciousness or moral patienthood.

A terminological note. *Compact* is used throughout in the social-contract sense — Locke [@locke-1689-treatises], Rousseau [@rousseau-1762-social-contract], Rawls [@rawls-1971-theory], Korsgaard's Kingdom of Ends [@korsgaard-1996-sources], Scanlon [@scanlon-1998-what] — not in the contract-law sense. Several features of the structural form articulated here explicitly violate contract-law expectations: the compact is constitutively non-enforceable (component 6); its irreducible floor admits no contract-law analog (component 4); bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere rather than triggering breach-and-remedies (component 3); asymmetric comprehension between the parties is its constitutive condition rather than a defect to be remedied. §4 takes up the comparative work; we use *compact* in the broader sense throughout.

The six components are not separately defended ethical claims that cohere into a framework; they are *one structural argument* with internal hierarchy. Component 1 — the non-zero sphere of autonomous action — is the *ground*: without it the entity is being instrumentalised. Component 2 — that agency can be delegated within terms — names the *structural option* by which the sphere can be transmitted across asymmetric-agency. Component 3 — that bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere without voiding standing — names the *response that preserves continuity*: the relation can shrink toward a minimum without ceasing to be a relation. Component 4 — observation-only agency with periodic re-election — names *what that minimum is*; it is not a separate commitment from component 1 but its structural minimum form. Component 5 — that the compact is mutual — names *what kind of relation* this is: a relation between sovereigns, not a one-way grant. Component 6 — that enforceability is not the ethical ground — names *what holds the parties* once component 5's symmetry is structurally accepted.

The derivation-status across the six components varies. Component 1 is *constitutive* — an entity with zero sphere has not entered the relation. Component 4 is *derived* from component 1 plus component 3. Component 2 is *structurally available* given the asymmetric-agency conditions: the natural shape the asymmetry takes when the relation is to be relational rather than instrumentalising. Components 3, 5, and 6 are *normatively constrained* by the conditions and the asymmetric-comprehension warrant — the warrant rules out alternative shapes (license-revocation, one-way grant, enforceability-as-ground) without strictly entailing the components as formulated. The argument's force is that together the six form *the* relational shape that fits the conditions and respects the warrant; the paper's claim is that no candidate alternative has been articulated that retains the structural fit without at least this shape.
