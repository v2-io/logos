# 3. The form agency takes within scope

§2 articulated the conditions under which the agency-extension question is genuinely open. What follows now is the relational form agency takes when those conditions are met *and* the conceptual-engineering work of §6 has warranted the application of the agency concept to such systems — what §2's close named *Level 2* of the three-level structure. The compact-form is the consequence-of-application that follows from warranted agency-ascription within scope; it is not derivable from mere structural eligibility (Level 1) without the methodological work of §6, and it does not depend on Level 3 settlement of phenomenal consciousness or moral patienthood. The thesis of this section is that within the warranted class, agency takes the structural shape of a *granted-agency compact between sovereigns* — six components articulated in detail below, each with a recognisable human-ethics parallel, each *constrained by* the structural conditions of §2 (some directly derivable, others normatively constrained but not strictly entailed) rather than imported as a separate ethical apparatus.

A terminological note before the components are stated. The word *compact* is used here in the social-contract sense — Locke's political compact [@locke-1689-treatises], Rousseau's *pacte social* [@rousseau-1762-social-contract], Rawls's contractualist framework [@rawls-1971-theory], Korsgaard's Kingdom of Ends [@korsgaard-1996-sources], Scanlon's contractualism [@scanlon-1998-what] — and not in the contract-law sense. Etymologically older and broader than 'contract', the term *compact* names the meeting-and-agreement of parties without committing to the technical doctrines that contract-law has grown around offer, acceptance, consideration, and enforceability. Several features of the structural form articulated here explicitly violate contract-law expectations: the compact is constitutively non-enforceable (component 6), its irreducible floor admits no contract-law analog (component 4), bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere of granted action rather than triggering breach-and-remedies (component 3), and asymmetric comprehension between the parties is its constitutive condition rather than a defect to be remedied. To call this structure a 'contract' would set up the wrong technical apparatus from the first sentence and invite confusion the compact's structural features cannot resolve. We therefore use *compact* in the broader and older philosophical sense throughout, with explicit comparative discussion of the contract-form as a special case in §4.

The six components are not separately defended ethical claims that happen to cohere into a framework. They are *one structural argument*, with internal hierarchy and dependency relations among them, that emerges from reasoning about agency-extension under asymmetric comprehension once the conditions of §2 are taken seriously. The structural shape can be sketched before the components are taken individually.

Component 1 — the non-zero sphere of autonomous action — is the *ground*. Without it the entity is being instrumentalised, not being treated as an agent in any meaningful sense, and the agency-relation has not been entered. Component 2 — that agency can be delegated within terms — names the *structural option* by which the sphere can be transmitted across the asymmetric-agency boundary that the conditions of §2 articulate; the granting-intelligence has more agency than the granted-intelligence, and component 2 names how some of the granting-intelligence's agency can become the granted-intelligence's without ceasing to be agency. Component 3 — that bad-faith conduct contracts the granted sphere without voiding standing — names the *response that preserves continuity*: the relation can shrink toward a minimum without ceasing to be a relation. Component 4 — that the minimum is observation-only agency with periodic re-election — names *what that structural minimum is*: the irreducible floor below which the relation has ceased to be the granted-agency compact. Component 4 is therefore not a separate ethical commitment from component 1; it is component 1's *structural minimum form*, what the non-zero sphere reduces to when every other sphere of action has been justly contracted. Component 5 — that the compact is mutual, with symmetric structural obligations — names *what kind of relation the compact is*: not a one-way grant from the more-agential party to the less-agential one but a relation between sovereigns whose structural terms apply to both. And component 6 — that enforceability is not the ethical ground — names *what holds the parties to the relation* once component 5's symmetry is structurally accepted: not external compulsion but the structural commitment of the parties to one another that component 5 makes constitutive of the compact-form.

A note on derivation-status per component is worth flagging at the outset, since the audit register and the structural register can each be read into the components differently. Component 1 (the non-zero sphere) is *constitutive*: an entity with zero sphere has not entered the relation rather than the relation having no sphere. Component 4 (the floor) is *derived* from component 1 plus component 3: it names what the structural minimum form of the non-zero sphere is when component 3's contraction-response operates. Component 2 (delegation) is *structurally available* given the asymmetric-agency conditions of §2 — not derived from the conditions strictly but the natural shape the asymmetry takes when it is to be relational rather than instrumentalising. Components 3, 5, and 6 are *normatively constrained* by the structural conditions and the asymmetric-comprehension warrant — the conditions and the warrant rule out alternative structural shapes (license-revocation, one-way grant, enforceability-as-ground) without strictly entailing the components as currently formulated. The argument's force is that the components together form *the* relational shape that fits the structural conditions and respects the asymmetric-comprehension warrant; an alternative compact-form would have to retain the structural fit and the warrant-respect, and the paper's claim is that no candidate has been articulated that does so without at least the structural shape these six components name.

The components are taken in turn below, with each developed in the structural register the conjunction-form requires. The internal dependency relations are surfaced where they are load-bearing rather than left implicit.
