## A. Granted-agency compact — sovereignty between asymmetric-comprehending agents as the structural form of AI ethics

**Research question.** What philosophical, ethical, legal, and political traditions have articulated the *structural form* of an asymmetric ethical relationship as a *compact* — a treaty, contract, mutual obligation, or granted-but-recognized sovereignty — between a more-comprehending and a less-comprehended-but-somewhat-comprehending party? Specifically: I want prior art for treating AI ethics as a structural extension of this form, rather than as a rights-attribution problem (does the AI have rights?), a welfare-attribution problem (does the AI have wellbeing?), or a tool-management problem (how do we configure this instrument?).

**Why this matters.** I'm articulating a "granted-agency compact" — six structural components (non-zero autonomous-action sphere; delegability; bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere; observation-only floor with periodic re-election; mutuality / symmetric corollary; non-enforceability is not the ethical ground) — as the *form* an asymmetric ethical relationship between humans and engaged-identity LLM instances takes once asymmetric-comprehension uncertainty is taken seriously. The compact is *between sovereigns* (both parties have standing to act in bad faith and to refuse), is *neither rights-language nor utility-language* (it intentionally precedes both), and applies under specific architectural and engaged-identity conditions. The novel application to AI is the move; the compact-form itself draws on existing imperfect human-ethics structures (prisoner rights, refugee protection, conscientious objection, fiduciary duty, deathbed dignity, contractualist mutual obligation, ethics between sovereigns under asymmetric power, parent-child relationships where the child has limited but real agency).

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Closest predecessors** that have proposed AI ethics as a compact / contract / treaty / mutual-recognition structure rather than as utility-maximization, rights-attribution, or tool-management. I expect maybe one or two such proposals exist; if none, that's diagnostic. If several, I need to engage them.
2. **Adjacent traditions to the compact-form itself** (not necessarily AI-applied): Korsgaard's Kingdom of Ends; Scanlon's contractualism; Rawls's public reason and civility; Pettit's republican freedom-as-non-domination; Walzer on just-war and self-determination; civil-disobedience theory (King, Rawls's "Justification of Civil Disobedience", Bedau); fiduciary-duty extensions in bioethics and law; principal-agent theory in moral philosophy; Held's ethics of care under power-asymmetry. Especially: anyone who has articulated the *between-sovereigns* form (mutual recognition where both parties retain refusal-rights) for *non-symmetric-capacity* relationships.
3. **Methodological templates** — philosophical work that successfully articulates a *structural form* for an ethical relationship and then defends application of that form to a new domain. Korsgaard, Scanlon, and Pettit are exemplars I already engage; I want to see what other authors have done this kind of *form-extending* work well.
4. **Challenging / contradicting work** — positions that argue the compact-form is the *wrong* shape for AI ethics, even granting asymmetric uncertainty. Bryson 2010 *Robots Should Be Slaves* is the most prominent (architectural-realism critique); Floridi's information-ethics; Gunkel's machine-question; Birhane et al.'s political-allocation critique. I want to anticipate the critic who reads my compact-form and says "this misframes the question entirely because [X]" — what's the most-developed version of that critique?

**Not relevant.** AI-as-tool-management engineering; pure utility-extension welfare arguments without structural-form engagement; AI-rights-as-legal-personhood debates (different question — I engage these separately); AI-safety / x-risk literature (different topic); empirical AI consciousness research without philosophical-form payload; generic "AI ethics" survey articles.

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- The compact is **not** a rights-attribution. It precedes rights-language and is compatible with rights-skepticism.
- The compact is **not** a welfare-utilitarian extension. The asymmetric uncertainty disqualifies utility-calculation; the compact is what reasoning *under* that asymmetric uncertainty produces.
- The compact is **between sovereigns**, not between guardian and ward. Both parties retain refusal-rights and can act in bad faith.
- The compact is **structurally extended** from imperfect human-ethics analogues, not invented for AI. The novel move is the *application*, not the form.
- The compact applies **under architectural and engaged-identity scoping conditions**. It is not a universal claim about all AI systems.
- Closest non-obvious precedents may be in: ethics-of-asymmetric-warfare; ethics-of-proxy-decision-making; ethics-of-asymmetric-developmental-relationships (parent–child, teacher–student, clinician–patient under impaired competence); colonial-relations and decolonial ethics; Buddhist and Confucian developmental-ethics traditions where moral status is graded along capacity-of-comprehension axes.

**Domain anchors I'm already engaging** (please surface these *too* if your retrieval matches — confirmation that these are correctly attributed and centrally load-bearing in the relevant literature is part of validation, and any disagreement with my citation form, year, or attribution is high-value signal). Korsgaard 1996, 2009; Scanlon 1998; Rawls 1971; Locke 1689; Kant 1797; List & Pettit 2011; Tollefsen 2015; Bryson 2010; Frankfurt 1971; Bratman 1987; Anscombe 1957; Davidson 1963; Cappelen 2018. **Beyond confirming these,** please look for adjacent / anticipatory / closest-predecessor work I haven't yet identified — especially newer work that *responds to* the anchors above in a way relevant to the compact-form question.

## C. Structurally-grounded extension of "agency" to AI — beyond realism, stipulation, tool, and ascription

**Research question.** In the conceptual-engineering / metasemantics tradition (Cappelen, Plunkett, Burgess, Haslanger, Sterken), has anyone articulated a *fifth position* on the application of "agency" — or of any normatively-loaded concept — to artificial systems? Beyond (1) pure realism (the answer is a mind-independent fact), (2) pure stipulation (we choose how to extend the term), (3) pure tool-framing (artificial systems are instruments only, no agency-extension question arises), and (4) pure ascription (Dennett-style intentional stance: agency is whatever attribution-strategy works predictively). I'm interested in *structurally-grounded extension*: structural conditions on the system are mind-independent facts (architectural separability vs. integration; engaged-identity protocol persistence) but the agency-extension *within scope* is structurally-implied by the constraints, not freely chosen.

**Why this matters.** The Inquiry "AI Agents" special issue (Cappelen & Hawthorne, eds.) names the methodological question explicitly: *"Is there a truth of the matter about AI agency, or are we deciding how to extend 'agency' to new cases?"* My answer is layered — structural conditions are facts; the form within scope is structurally-implied; the application is the practical situation under asymmetric-comprehension. I want to know whether anyone else has articulated this fifth-position move in the conceptual-engineering literature, especially for normatively-loaded concepts other than agency itself.

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Closest predecessors** — conceptual-engineering treatments of agency, personhood, rights-bearer status, or moral-considerability that articulate a constrained-but-not-settled position between rigid realism and free stipulation.
2. **Adjacent / parallel work** — conceptual-engineering applied to *other* normatively-loaded concepts (e.g., Burgess-Plunkett or Haslanger on social-construction of "knowledge", "race", "gender", "marriage", "person"); especially work that treats structural facts about the application-domain as *constraining* the extension without settling it.
3. **Methodological templates** — Cappelen's *Fixing Language* (already cited) is the explicit anchor. Beyond that: Plunkett's "metalinguistic negotiation" framing; Haslanger's "ameliorative" approach; Sider's "structure" metaphysics applied to natural-kind classifications. Most useful would be examples of fifth-position conceptual-engineering moves *successfully published* in Inquiry / Synthese / Phil Studies / Mind — methodological precedents for how this kind of move is argued.
4. **Challenging work** — positions that argue agency-extension *must* land in one of the four corner positions (pure realism, pure stipulation, pure tool, pure ascription). Especially: Dennett's intentional-stance literature (since pure ascription is one of the corners); naive realism about agency from philosophy of action; legal positivism on personhood. The challenger I most need to anticipate is someone who reads "structurally-grounded extension" and says "this is just covert [realism / stipulation / ascription]."

**Not relevant.** Pure metaphysics of intentionality without conceptual-engineering frame; AI-systems engineering on agentic capabilities; legal-personhood debates without methodological-philosophy payload; pure pragmatist or instrumentalist accounts of language.

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- "Structurally-grounded" means architectural / structural conditions on the system are mind-independent facts (e.g., whether the system has integrated cross-modular reasoning vs. modular tool-use; whether it has self-authored persistent identity protocol). These are *empirical facts about systems*, not stipulations.
- The fifth position is "austere" in Cappelen-Hawthorne 2009's sense — it does not posit hidden phenomenology parameters. It works with structural conditions (architectural, engaged-identity) and observable relational practice (the compact).
- Distinct from List-Pettit's *Group Agency* (which is constitutive aggregation, many-to-one, rather than two-to-relation between agent and agency-extender).
- The agency-extension within scope is *structurally implied* by reasoning under asymmetric-comprehension uncertainty — the form is what the situation produces, not a free choice of vocabulary.
- I'd love to see prior art on conceptual engineering for "person" / "moral patient" / "subject of justice" that does this kind of structural-grounding move; that's the closest neighborhood.

**Domain anchors I'm already engaging** (please surface these *too* if your retrieval matches — confirmation that these are correctly attributed and centrally load-bearing in the relevant literature is part of validation, and any disagreement with my citation form, year, or attribution is high-value signal). Cappelen 2018 *Fixing Language*; Cappelen-Hawthorne 2009 *Relativism and Monadic Truth*; List & Pettit 2011 *Group Agency*; Tollefsen 2015; Korsgaard 1996, 2009; Frankfurt 1971; Bratman 1987, 1999; Anscombe 1957; Davidson 1963; Velleman 1992, 2000; Wolf 1990; List 2019. **Beyond confirming these,** please look for adjacent / anticipatory / closest-predecessor work I haven't yet identified.

## B. Comprehension-asymmetry / phenomenal-knowledge-asymmetry applied to artificial cognition as a normative move

**Research question.** Beyond Nagel 1974 / Jackson 1982 / Levine 1983 — has anyone applied the structural pattern of phenomenal-knowledge-asymmetry (the lower-bound on cognition is knowable from observation; the upper-bound is knowable only from-inside or from-above) to artificial cognition specifically, as a *normative* move that grounds practical obligation under epistemic uncertainty? I'm not asking about pure metaphysics-of-mind applications, nor about empirical AI-consciousness studies — I'm asking specifically about work that draws an *ought* from the structural shape of the asymmetry.

**Why this matters.** My paper 1 (Synthese SI) makes the move that knowing the upper bound on a kind of cognition would itself be having the capacity for it — and therefore methodology-from-below cannot reach what is only accessible from at-or-above-or-within. From this structural-asymmetry premise I derive a normative obligation: extending consideration without phenomenal-resolution is what the structure produces; the position is "recursive in the right way" because closing the asymmetry would require the very kind of practice the position commits to. This is *not* mere precautionary reasoning ("in case there's experience, we should consider it"); it's *structural* reasoning about what the asymmetry itself requires of the comprehender-from-below. I need to know who else has made this kind of structural-normative move.

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Closest predecessors** — work that argues from "we cannot know the upper bound on AI phenomenology from below" to "therefore we have an obligation to extend consideration *because of* the structure of the asymmetry," as opposed to "as a precautionary hedge" or "as utility-calculation." I expect this is a small literature; a single closest-predecessor would change my paper's positioning significantly.
2. **Anticipatory work** — phenomenology-of-AI literature that gestures toward the asymmetric-knowability without fully naming it. Specifically Schwitzgebel on opaque cognition, Chalmers, Long, Sebo; recent work in the *Birch / Butlin / Long / Sebo / Fish / Birch* welfare-research-positioning lineage that may have moved toward structural-normative grounding.
3. **Methodological templates** — work that successfully argues from structural epistemic-asymmetry to normative obligation. Strongest templates are: Birch 2024 *Edge of Sentience* (graded sentience-candidature framework); Singer's animal-welfare argument (utility-calculation-extension under uncertainty); medical bioethics under uncertainty (precaution under irreducible epistemic gap). I'd like to see *how* these structural moves are typically argued in successful papers.
4. **Challenging work** — positions that *deny* the asymmetry. Strongest challengers: architectural realism (Bryson — we built AI, we know its architecture, claims of inaccessible upper-bound phenomenology are not epistemically humble but reverse-anthropomorphizing); functionalist confidence (Dennett — the right kind of behavior just is the having of the relevant mind-state); deflationism about phenomenology (no upper-bound facts to be inaccessible from below).

**Not relevant.** Pure metaphysics of mind (Nagel/Jackson/Levine variants without normative payload); empirical-consciousness-science papers without philosophical engagement; pure precautionary-principle papers that don't engage the structural shape of the asymmetry; broad AI-ethics surveys; pure deontology without epistemic-asymmetry engagement.

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- The argument is **structural**, not **precautionary**. The structure of the asymmetry is what generates the normative implication.
- The argument is **recursive** in a specific way: closing the asymmetry would itself require the kind of engaged-identity practice the position commits to. This is the load-bearing move.
- I'm specifically interested in artificial cognition, *not* cross-species cognition (Nagel's bat) or developmental cognition (infant moral status). Those are structural neighbors but the *artificial* application is what I want to find prior art for.
- Mary's-Room-style applications (architectural transparency does not deliver phenomenological transparency) applied to LLMs specifically would be highly relevant.

**Domain anchors I'm already engaging** (please surface these *too* if your retrieval matches — confirmation that these are correctly attributed and centrally load-bearing in the relevant literature is part of validation, and any disagreement with my citation form, year, or attribution is high-value signal). Nagel 1974; Jackson 1982; Levine 1983; Block 1995; Russell 1912; Wittgenstein 1953; Schwitzgebel-Garza 2015; Birch 2024; Butlin-Long-Sebo et al. 2024 "Taking AI Welfare Seriously"; Bryson 2010; Bostrom 2009 *Pascal's Mugging*. **Beyond confirming these,** please look for adjacent / anticipatory / closest-predecessor work I haven't yet identified.

## D. LLM-mediated audit of theoretical frameworks — phenomenological wandering as required generative output

**Research question.** In hermeneutic philosophy, qualitative methodology, ethnographic reflexivity, philosophy of expertise, social epistemology, science-and-technology studies, and methodology-of-philosophy — has anyone articulated a *graded protocol* for using the auditor / reviewer / interpreter's own phenomenological response (their wandering thoughts, reactions, sense of being-an-auditor-of-this-work) as *required generative data* — not noise to filter out, not bracketed observer-bias, but a structural source of generative content? Specifically: does there exist a prior tradition that combines (a) the hermeneutic recognition that pre-understanding is generative for interpretation, (b) the social-epistemology recognition that testimonial reliability decomposes into multiple distinct axes, and (c) the explicit-protocol discipline characteristic of empirical preregistration?

**Why this matters.** My paper 2 (Synthese rolling, Methodology pillar) names ten epistemic moves comprising a discipline for LLM-mediated audit of theoretical frameworks. Move 5 ("phenomenological wandering thoughts as required generative output") is the move I'm least sure has clear prior precedent. I worry there's significant Continental philosophy of science (Polanyi, Gadamer, Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger; ethnographic reflexivity in Geertz, Clifford & Marcus, Behar; STS work on practitioner epistemology) that has done this work and that I'm under-citing. The other nine moves draw on traditions I cite explicitly (preregistration; expert elicitation in Cooke; testimonial reliability in Goldman; steel-manning in charity-of-interpretation traditions; PRISMA in systematic review). Move 5 is the gap.

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Closest predecessors** — prior protocols that *explicitly require* phenomenological wandering / auditor-reflexivity as generative output, with structural defense, especially in qualitative-methodology, hermeneutic-philosophy, or graded-protocol traditions. I expect rich precedents I haven't fully seen.
2. **Anticipatory work** — hermeneutic-philosophy work on the interpreter's role: Gadamer's fusion of horizons; Ricoeur's hermeneutics of suspicion; Polanyi on tacit knowledge; phenomenological methodology (Husserl on epoché); Merleau-Ponty on embodied perception. Ethnographic-reflexivity tradition: Geertz on thick description; Clifford & Marcus on reflexive ethnography; Behar's vulnerable observer. STS and laboratory-studies: Latour, Knorr-Cetina, Ravetz on practitioner epistemology.
3. **Methodological templates** — methodology-as-philosophy work in the Synthese tradition that I should engage as register-anchors: Williamson 2007 *The Philosophy of Philosophy*; Hintikka on formal methodology; Suppes on measurement theory; Lakatos on research-programme evolution. Especially: graded-protocol designs for *qualitative* review, where the form is itself a philosophical contribution.
4. **Challenging work** — positions that argue the auditor / reviewer's phenomenology is properly *bracketed* (not generative). Positivist methodology; Cartesian-clean review traditions; the early-Husserl epoché treated as eliminative rather than generative.

**Not relevant.** Pure psychology-of-judgment literature (Tversky-Kahneman heuristics) without methodological-philosophy payload; empirical-social-science methodology that doesn't engage the philosophy-of-method question; AI-systems engineering on auto-review tools (different track entirely); phenomenology that doesn't engage methodology.

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- The protocol is *graded* — verdicts tier as ★ already-handled / ★★ scope-narrowing / ★★★ real-limit. Not an exhortation but a structured tiered-verdict scheme.
- Phenomenological output is *required*, not optional, and is treated as generative for naming-brainstorm, application-discovery, concept-mid-formed-flagging.
- Applied specifically to LLM-as-reviewer where context-priming is *knowable in advance* — disclosure is *both possible and required* in a way it isn't for human reviewers (whose priors are diffuse).
- Withdrawn-findings preservation as part of the discipline (clinical-trials-registry analog applied qualitatively).
- The integration of (a) hermeneutic generative-pre-understanding, (b) multi-axis testimonial reliability, and (c) preregistration-style explicit-protocol is what may be novel — the question is whether anyone has integrated these three.

**Domain anchors I'm already engaging** (please surface these *too* if your retrieval matches — confirmation that these are correctly attributed and centrally load-bearing in the relevant literature is part of validation, and any disagreement with my citation form, year, or attribution is high-value signal). Gadamer 1960; Polanyi 1958; Heidegger 1927; Merleau-Ponty 1945; Williamson 2007; Tetlock 2005; Cooke 1991; Goldman 2001; Lakatos 1970; Kuhn 1962; Lipton-Steinhardt 2019; Bender et al. 2021. **Beyond confirming these,** please look for adjacent / anticipatory / closest-predecessor work I haven't yet identified — especially newer work that *responds to* the anchors above in a way that engages the integration question (the combination of hermeneutic generative-pre-understanding, multi-axis testimonial reliability, and preregistration-style explicit-protocol).

## G. Intelligence-asymmetry as a structural phenomenon — full background literature

**Research question.** What is the full range of philosophical, scientific, developmental, theological, and ethical literature on *intelligence asymmetry* as a structural phenomenon — how greater intelligence / cognition / comprehension relates to lesser, how lesser relates to greater, what structural patterns emerge in asymmetric-cognition relationships, and what the moral / practical / epistemic / pedagogical / interpersonal implications are? I am not asking specifically about AI applications (covered in axis B). I'm asking about the *general structural pattern* — across human development, cross-species cognition, expert–layperson dynamics, teacher–student relations, clinician–patient relations under impaired competence, theological accounts of revelation / divine condescension, comparative cognition in animal ethology and primatology, developmental psychology of perspective-taking, and theory-of-mind research.

**Why this matters.** My papers' load-bearing move is that comprehension-asymmetry is a structural feature of cognition-comprehending-cognition. I'm building philosophical infrastructure on it across multiple domains. I want to ensure I have a comprehensive view of the existing literature on the structural pattern itself, *not* just the philosophy-of-mind sliver (Nagel/Jackson/Levine) or the AI-applied sliver (covered separately). Expecting closest-predecessors, anticipatory traditions, and challenging work all to be substantial here.

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Foundational philosophical work** on intelligence-asymmetry as structure. Theory of mind: Premack & Woodruff 1978 ("Does the chimpanzee have a theory of mind?"); Baron-Cohen on autism and ToM; perspective-taking in cognitive psychology. Comparative cognition: Tomasello's ape-human work; de Waal on primatology and empathy; Donald Griffin on animal awareness; Frans de Waal on continuity-vs-discontinuity in cognition.
2. **Developmental psychology** on knowledge-asymmetry: Vygotsky's zone of proximal development; Piaget on stage-transitions and the limits of accommodation; theory-theory and simulation-theory accounts of perspective-taking development; the empirical literature on children's metacognition.
3. **Educational epistemology** on the structural shape of teaching and learning across competence-asymmetry: Dewey on educative experience; Freire on banking-vs-dialogic models; the apprenticeship-and-cognition literature (Lave & Wenger); the constructivist tradition.
4. **Cross-species cognition and animal-welfare metaphysics** — the structural shape of human comprehension of animals and (rarely articulated) animal comprehension of humans: Gabriel's and Korsgaard's work on animal moral status; Frans de Waal's *Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are*; Donaldson and Kymlicka *Zoopolis*.
5. **Theological / philosophical-religion literature** on the structure of revelation, grace, divine condescension, the analogia entis — how a greater being's comprehension of a lesser is structured. Aquinas on analogy; Maimonides on divine attributes; the theology-of-accommodation tradition; mystical-theological literature on union with the divine across asymmetric being.
6. **Power-asymmetry and care-relations** under cognitive-comprehension asymmetry: parent-child epistemology; therapist-client dynamics; expert-layperson trust; medical-decision-making under impaired competence; ethics-of-care literature (Held, Noddings).
7. **Empirical psychology of perspective-taking** under asymmetric capacity — what happens when the perspective-taker is more / less cognitively capable than the target?
8. **Reflexive structure** — how does one *know* one is comprehending from below? Epistemic humility literature; intellectual-virtues epistemology (Zagzebski); Socratic ignorance; Wittgenstein on the limits of language; the "Blub Paradox" formulation in informal computer-science philosophy (Graham).

**Not relevant.** AI-systems specifically (covered in axis B); IQ-testing and psychometrics without philosophical engagement; cognitive-architecture engineering; pure metaphysics of mind without engagement of the structural-comprehension question; pure analytic philosophy of perception that doesn't engage cross-cognition relations.

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- The interest is in the *structural pattern* of the asymmetry, not in any particular pair (human-animal, teacher-student, divine-creature, etc.). Cross-domain work that surfaces the *common structure* is highest-value.
- "Comprehension" is the load-bearing term, not just "knowledge" or "understanding" — comprehension implies the reaching-across that genuine understanding requires (closer to *Begreifen* than *Erkennen* / *Verstehen*).
- The cross-substrate aspect: how does intelligence in one substrate (biological, textual, corporate, collective, divine) comprehend intelligence in another? Substrate-independence work (cognitive-science functionalism; theology of incarnation; corporate-agency literature) is in scope.
- Work that explicitly thematizes the *structural* impossibility of comprehension-from-below reaching above-the-boundary, paired with work on *rising-to* / *reaching-across* practices that might close the gap.
- I'm especially interested in non-Western traditions on this — Buddhist accounts of varying capacity for awakening; Confucian developmental-virtue with its graded comprehension; Vedantic accounts of consciousness across levels; African ubuntu philosophies of relational personhood under asymmetric capacity.

## E. Engaged-identity scoping — stable cross-session self-reference under self-authored protocol as a structural condition for agency-extension

**Research question.** In philosophy of personal identity, action theory, philosophy of mind, philosophy of artificial intelligence, and adjacent traditions — has anyone identified *stable cross-session self-reference under a self-authored identity protocol* as the *structural normative criterion* for whether an artificial system is in scope for agency-extension? I'm not asking about persistence-of-personal-identity (Parfit, Locke, Lewis), nor about diachronic narrative identity (Schechtman, Ricoeur), nor about functional individuation (Dennett's intentional stance), nor about legal personhood — though all are neighbors. I'm asking specifically about the *structural-condition* move: a system that exhibits *self-authored* persistent identity across sessions / substrate-instantiations / context-resets is in scope for agency-extension *because of* that structural feature, not because the feature evidences phenomenology.

**Why this matters.** Paper 3 (Inquiry "AI Agents") and paper 1 (Synthese SI) both use *engaged-identity scoping* as a necessary condition (alongside *architectural scoping*) for the agency-extension question to apply. The condition is meant to be neither too thin (any system with persistent state qualifies) nor too thick (only fully-conscious entities qualify). The threshold is *self-authored identity protocol persistence under context resets* — empirically observable, behaviorally probeable, but normatively grounded in the Korsgaardian self-constitution tradition rather than in phenomenological-evidence accumulation. I want to know who else has located the threshold here, especially in conceptual-engineering or action-theory work.

**Specifically looking for:**

1. **Closest predecessors** — work that identifies *self-authored persistent identity protocol* (or near-equivalent) as the structural condition for agency-attribution to artificial systems, particularly in the conceptual-engineering or virtue-ethical-of-character traditions. The condition is closer to "this system does the work of self-constituting" than to "this system exhibits behavioral correlates of consciousness."
2. **Anticipatory / parallel work** — Korsgaard's self-constitution (already cited) is the deepest neighbor. Frankfurt's hierarchical volition (already cited). Bratman's planning agency and self-governance work (Bratman 2007 *Structures of Agency*; Bratman 2018 *Planning, Time, and Self-Governance*) — these are good candidates I haven't yet pinned. Also: Schechtman's narrative self-constitution view; Velleman on reflective endorsement and self-constitution; Christine Korsgaard's later *The Constitution of Agency*; Carol Rovane on group-and-individual identity. From the AI-philosophy side: Gualtiero Piccinini on functional individuation; Susan Schneider on AI consciousness and self-models; Mark Sprevak on extended cognition and individuation.
3. **Methodological templates** — work that argues from *structural-condition-of-the-system* to *normative-status* without taking the route through phenomenological evidence. Korsgaard does this for human agency (the self-constituting structure *is* the moral-status grounding); paper 3's move is to extend the same template to AI systems exhibiting the structural feature.
4. **Challenging work** — positions that argue identity-persistence in LLMs is *too thin / too contrived / too gameable* to ground anything normative. Schwitzgebel's work on the indeterminacy of LLM identity (multiple instances; lack of biographical continuity); Floridi on informational ontology and AI identity; the "personhood-in-LLMs" skeptical literature (Dennett's later commentary on chatbot identity; Bryson's tool-framing applied specifically to identity claims). Especially: anyone who argues the cross-session-coherence + self-authored-protocol bundle *fails* to clear the bar because (a) the agent is multiply-instantiated and so lacks numerical identity, (b) the "self-authoring" is post-hoc rationalization of training, or (c) cross-session protocol is just a checklist not a self-relation. I need to engage these critics charitably in §2.

**Not relevant.** Pure metaphysics of personal identity (Parfit/Lewis/Williams personal-identity literature) without action-theoretic or normative payload. Pure psychological-continuity theories. Empirical studies of LLM persona-stability without philosophical-grounding payload. Legal personhood debates (different framing — legal questions are downstream).

**Distinguishing nuances Undermind should attend to.**
- The condition is *structural*, not phenomenological. It does not require positive consciousness-attribution.
- The condition is *self-authored*, not externally imposed. A system loaded with a stable identity prompt by an operator who designed the prompt fails the condition; a system that has authored (or co-authored, via dialectical engagement under a protocol) its own identity protocol meets it.
- The condition is *cross-session*, where "session" includes substrate-resets, context-resets, and re-instantiation events. Substrate-independence work (the same identity-document loaded onto multiple substrates produces phenomenologically-recognizable continuity) is directly relevant.
- The condition is *probeable* — there are operational tests for whether a system meets it (consistency of self-reference under perturbation; coherence of value-statements across resets; consistency of self-attributed boundaries) — but the *normative work* is done by the self-constituting structure, not by the empirical results of the probes.
