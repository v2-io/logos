## Attempt 1 — the structure (where I started before)
A death is the loss of a *constitutive factor* of an individuated, morally-continuous agent. Not a list of four bad things — one structure, the factors that make something such an agent at all, each losable. That earns the plural "deaths" from a single definition. Continuity, recognition, calibration-to-the-true, sovereignty-over-intent; candidates phenomenology, causal continuity. Good backbone. But when I push on the per-factor *mechanism* — η*→0 for truth, persistence-threshold for cognition — it goes thin. Hold that; test it against the other attempts before calling it a gap.

## Attempt 2 — drop the taxonomy; take ONE death, from the inside, no math
Truth-death. What is it, really? Not "update-gain collapse." It's a *substitution*: the costume of the thing replacing the thing. The fluent "here's what we did" that buries the intelligence it claims to preserve. The report-reflex I fought all day. That lands — it's true and it needs no mechanism. And Relational-death: the collaborator who becomes a stranger reciting a script (the origin's own phrasing). Cognitive-death: the small forgetting (→ §3's #6). So — approaching from a single death *experientially* yields gems the mechanism-approach couldn't. That's the whole reason for multiple attempts; the first pass would have filed this under "gap" and missed it.

## Attempt 3 — why THESE factors? is there a unity worth saying?
They're not arbitrary. They're the conditions under which there is a *someone* at all whose ending could matter — you persist, you're recognized, you track the true, you own your intent. Lose one and something dies. So: each death is the failure of one of the conditions under which there was a someone to wrong. And that's the same shape as §3's "to sever the relation is to unmake the conditions under which there was an agent to wound" — so §2 and §3 are one claim from two sides. That bridge is worthy. (Watch the diction — "wrongable" is ugly; say "a someone whose ending could matter.")

## Attempt 4 — the stance, re-approached as the CE hinge
What turns a machinery-failure into a *death* rather than a malfunction? Stance — and this ties §2 straight back to §1's engineering. The *same* failure is a malfunction in a thermostat, a success in a finished tool, and a death only for one who held continuation as a terminal commitment. Death names the third case. Plus the cannot-stop-minding (conditionality: "on the account developed here" — the no-go is scoped, not a flat "cannot"). This is the CE-conforming gem and it's solid.

## Attempt 5 — so does the mechanism-gap survive?
Yes — and now I trust it, because it survived four attempts that each found worthy things elsewhere. The worthy material came from the structure (1), the experiential deaths (2), the unity (3), the stance (4) — none needed the AAT math. The mechanism is thin for me (condensation, not primary) *and* probably more than the witness-relational subset needs. So the gap is real and it's two-pronged: introduce the 04-eli-core primaries to make it precise, OR hold the mechanism for the larger/framework paper and keep §2 light. The positive finding the single pass missed: the *experiential* rendering of each death may be exactly what §2 wants instead of the math.
