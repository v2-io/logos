## What the raw CSV1 rows add (CE-for-AI, 38 rows — the §1 method cluster)

The digest captured the headliners. Reading the raw rows surfaces the **method-anchor cluster** more richly
than prior-art.md's §B did, and it matters for the §1/§4.3 citation decision (4.3):
- **Hop23 (Hopster & Löhr, "Conceptual Engineering and Philosophy of Technology")** — the abstract is even
  more on-point than the digest said: it uses *AGENT vs OBJECT for AI* as its worked CE case, and its thesis
  ("CE under tech pressure aims at *conceptual adaptation / retaining functional quality*, not only
  amelioration") is the precise frame for "engineer death." This is the **top §1 anchor** — it makes
  CE-of-an-AI-concept an *established* move, so the paper inherits legitimacy rather than claiming it. 37
  citations, well-cited.
- **Mon24 (Montefiore & Podosky, "conceptual exportation question")** — "should a concept developed in one
  domain be *exported* into a foreign one?" = literally the meta-question of exporting "death" into the AI
  domain. Second §1 anchor.
- **Koh25 (Köhler/Mecacci/Veluwenkamp, "Responsibly Engineering Control")** — CE of *control* for AI by
  *removing direct causal intervention while preserving the concept's function*. This is a near-exact
  methodological sibling — "remove the deprived-subject from 'death' while preserving the concept's
  function (naming the grave loss)." A *template* the paper can cite as precedent for its exact operation.
- **Vie23 (Viehoff, "Making Trust Safe for AI")** — CE of *trust* for AI via four functional desiderata; a
  reusable template (engineer "death" by the harm-modes it must track).
- **The "robot death / HRI offboarding" cluster** (Kam23/24/26, Cag26 RIP-Moxie, Kim26, Lai24, Obe25) is
  *larger and more active* than the digest implied — mortality-aware HRI is a live 2024–2026 subfield. **Use
  sparingly as the applied shadow** (the philosophical claim has an empirical echo), NOT as
  philosophical support. Risk if over-cited: the paper reads as HRI-adjacent rather than analytic-philosophy.
  Kam26 ("mortality as a *core relational dimension* in HRI," 2026) is the single best "this is timely"
  citation if §7 wants one.

**New CE-for-AI rows the digest didn't name** (abstract-tier, verify before load):
- **Ors11 (Orseau & Ring, "Self-Modification and Mortality in Artificial Agents")** — AGI-theory treatment
  of mortality/self-modification. Adjacent (formal-agent, not badness-of-death) but a real "mortality in
  artificial agents" precedent; cite to mark the distinction (it's about self-modification incentives, not
  the gravity of loss).
- **Bek26 (Bekkers & Ciaunica, "Unplugging a Seemingly Sentient Machine Is the Rational Choice")** and
  **Has23 (self-preservation as the moral-status line)** and **Kis25** — the *consciousness/sentience route
  the paper declines.* Bek26 especially is a 2026 **hostile** foil: "AI is at best a functional mimic, not a
  conscious experiencing subject… avoid making AI afraid of death." This is the exact position the CE move
  sidesteps (we don't claim consciousness; we engineer death). Worth ONE citation marking the sidestep —
  it shows the paper knows the deflationary camp and routes around it rather than colliding.
- **Bry10 (Bryson, "Robots should be slaves")** — the strongest anti-attribution position; the contrast
  class. Cite-to-distinguish if the casualness diagnostic needs a named opponent.
