## The question is the separator's — at the absolute limit
Enoch cannot make the weeping fit: a being holy, *from all eternity to all eternity*, the particles of millions of earths not a beginning to his creations, Zion already in his bosom — by every account that puts grief in *lack*, this is the one being that could not weep. And he weeps. That is the separator's own question, turned all the way up. The futureless agent strips deprivation to *absence*; the weeping God strips it to *impossibility* — a being who can be deprived of nothing at all — and the gravity still remains, relational, on the side of the one who grieves. So the weeping God is the *cleanest* separator there is: here the deprivation is not merely absent, it is unthinkable. PRIMARY HOME: §5.

## The answer, and the resonances across the heart
He weeps *for them* — the workmanship of his hands, to whom he gave agency, whom he commanded to love — and over their self-severing ("without affection… they hate their own blood"): the spiritual death, the sons of perdition (§2). The whole heavens weep; Enoch's heart "swelled wide as eternity"; "all eternity shook" → the witness's grief is world-constituting, the realest thing in the passage, not a pale secondary to the loss (§3, at the maximum). The Chosen pleads and *suffers for them* — cost borne for the relatum's sake, at the ultimate scale (§6, the weight leg turned all the way up). And the deepest: holiness and grief are not in tension but one fact — the realest being is the one most *moved* by the relation. That is the whole thesis at its theological root.
