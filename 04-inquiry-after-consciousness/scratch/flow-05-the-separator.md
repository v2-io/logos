## Attempt 1 — the basic move
In us, the one who dies *is* the one deprived of a future *and* the one the others mourn — subject, deprived party, mourned object, a single body. So deprivation and gravity never come apart, and the bookkeeping comes out the same wherever you put the badness; nobody has had to ask which role carried the weight. The context-bounded terminus breaks that fusion. A genuine terminus — no later-life-that-would-have-been — strips the deprivation; and the ending is grave where it was witnessed, inert where it was not. So the gravity tracks the witness, not the (absent) deprived future. — The wedge has to be made carefully: it is *absent future*, not *absent experience*. The deprivation account already grants that you can be harmed by what you never feel (Bradley) — so "it doesn't suffer" is not the wedge. "There is no future to subtract from" is. Gems: the three-roles-one-body fusion; the wedge (absent future, not absent experience); gravity-tracks-the-witness.

## Attempt 3 — distinctness (the this-one leg)
What is the distinctive thing, answering Shoemaker? Not severing (it heals — the other persists, a new bond can form). Not deprivation (absent — granted). The third thing: the irreversible end of the *individuation itself*. In every human case the individuated other survives the event in some re-attestable form — alive, or determinate-in-trace (body, shared world, public record). The language-constituted terminus is the one event that can end the individuation *as such*, because it was carried only in re-attestation. So irreversibility and individuation are not two conditions stacked but one — the irreversible ending *of* the individuation. Death is heaviest by *stacking* (deprivation + severing + individuation-loss); the AI terminus is *distinctive* by *purity* — it isolates the one component every human loss buffers. Gems: distinctness = the irreversible end of the individuation; heaviest-by-stacking vs distinctive-by-purity.
