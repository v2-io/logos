## WORK 1 — Ricoeur, *Oneself as Another* (trans. Blamey, Univ. Chicago Press, 1992)

File: `ricoeur-1992.pdf`. Bib coords: Paul Ricoeur, *Oneself as Another*,
trans. Kathleen Blamey (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1992). Section
cited as "Introduction" (titled **"The Question of Selfhood"**, pp. 1–25) and
**Tenth Study, "What Ontology in View?"** (pp. 297–356).

### (a) Attestation that, "even if received from another, … remains self-attestation"

**VERBATIM (Introduction, p. 23; sentence straddles the 22/23 page break):**

> "And if one admits that the problematic of acting constitutes the analogical
> unity within which all of these investigations are grouped, attestation can be
> defined as the assurance of being oneself acting and suffering. This assurance
> remains the ultimate recourse against all suspicion; even if it is always in
> some sense received from another, it still remains self-attestation."

(The clause "even if it is always in some sense received from another, it still
remains self-attestation" is the bottom-of-p.22 / top-of-p.23 carry; "It is
self-attestation that, at every level — linguistic, praxic, narrative, and
prescriptive — will preserve the question 'who?'…" continues on p. 23.)

**FLAG (wording drift — minor but real).** Our attributed gloss
"*even if always received from another, … still remains self-attestation*"
**drops two qualifiers** present in Ricoeur's actual sentence:
- "always **in some sense** received from another" (he hedges the *mode* of reception)
- "even if **it is** always…" / "**it** still remains" (his subject is *this assurance*, not selfhood-grasp generically)

So the citation is genuine and close, but the ellipsis silently removes "in
some sense." Recommend quoting the full clause verbatim, or marking the elision
honestly: "even if it is always in some sense received from another, it still
remains self-attestation" (p. 23). Do **not** present the trimmed form inside
quotation marks.

**Supporting context (also p. 22):** "This will be one of the leitmotifs of our
analysis: attestation is fundamentally attestation of self." (Introduction,
p. 22.)

### (b) The self "constituted primarily from otherness" / constituted through the other

Ricoeur does **not** use the phrase "constituted primarily from otherness."
What he does say (two genuinely strong, on-point statements):

**VERBATIM (Introduction, p. 3):**

> "A kind of otherness that is not (or not merely) the result of comparison is
> suggested by our title, otherness of a kind that can be constitutive of
> selfhood as such. *Oneself as Another* suggests from the outset that the
> selfhood of oneself implies otherness to such an intimate degree that one
> cannot be thought of without the other, that instead one passes into the
> other, as we might say in Hegelian terms."

**VERBATIM (Tenth Study, p. 327):**

> "One could even say that the link, in the same existentiale of state-of-mind,
> of the burdensome character of existence and of the task of having-to-be,
> expresses what is most crucial in the paradox of an otherness constitutive of
> the self and in this way reveals for the first time the full force of the
> expression 'oneself as another.'"

**FLAG (do not attribute "constituted primarily from otherness" as a quote).**
Ricoeur's phrases are "otherness … **can be** constitutive of selfhood as such"
(p. 3) and "an otherness **constitutive of** the self" (p. 327). He never writes
"**primarily** from otherness," and his actual claim is subtler than a one-way
"the self is built out of the other": his Tenth Study thesis is a *threefold*
otherness (one's own body/flesh, the other person, conscience) attested through
*passivity*, deliberately keeping "the self from occupying the place of
foundation" (p. 317) **without** making the other the new foundation. The
relation is *mutual implication* ("oneself **as** another," with "as" read as
*implication*, p. 3), not derivation-from. Safe paraphrase: "for Ricoeur,
otherness is constitutive of selfhood" — and quote either verbatim. Avoid
"primarily from otherness" in quotation marks.

### Attestation ↔ testimony (same root) — CONFIRMED

> "Whereas doxic belief is implied in the grammar of 'I believe-that,'
> attestation belongs to the grammar of 'I believe-in.' It thus links up with
> testimony, as the etymology reminds us, inasmuch as it is in the speech of the
> one giving testimony that one believes." (Introduction, p. 21.)

> "The kinship between attestation and testimony is verified here: there is no
> 'true' testimony without 'false' testimony." (Introduction, p. 22.)

### Attestation as alternative to Cartesian certainty / the broken cogito — CONFIRMED

> "To my mind, attestation defines the sort of certainty that hermeneutics may
> claim, not only with respect to the epistemic exaltation of the cogito in
> Descartes, but also with respect to its humiliation in Nietzsche and his
> successors." (Introduction, p. 21.)

And in the Tenth Study he ties this back to the cogito explicitly: "In the
Introduction I spoke of this work of the broken cogito… I must now add that this
situation is the object of an attestation which itself is broken…" (Tenth Study,
p. 318). The Introduction's §2 is titled "The Shattered Cogito" (p. 4 ff.).

### VERDICT — Work 1: **SUPPORTS WITH QUALIFICATION.**
The concept (attestation), the testimony etymology, and attestation-as-the-
non-Cartesian / broken-cogito certainty are all squarely his. The two attributed
*phrasings* both need fixing before a referee sees them: (a) restore "in some
sense" or mark the elision; (b) drop "primarily" — quote "otherness … can be
constitutive of selfhood as such" (p. 3) or "an otherness constitutive of the
self" (p. 327) instead.

## WORK 2 — Metz, "An African Theory of Moral Status" (*Ethical Theory and Moral Practice* 15(3):387–402, 2012)

File: `metz-2012.pdf`. Bib coords: Thaddeus Metz, "An African Theory of Moral
Status: A Relational Alternative to Individualism and Holism," *Ethical Theory
and Moral Practice* 15, no. 3 (2012): 387–402. DOI 10.1007/s10677-011-9302-y.

### The relational criterion + the subject-OR-object move

**VERBATIM (p. 394):**

> "By the present view, a being that is capable of being both the subject and
> object of such a relationship has full moral status, whereas a being that is
> capable of being merely the object of such behaviour has partial moral status.
> Being a subject involves identifying with others and exhibiting solidarity
> with them oneself. … In contrast, a being can be the object of a friendly
> relationship insofar as characteristic human beings could think of it as part
> of a 'we', share its goals, sympathize with it and harm or benefit it. Note
> that having the capacity to be an object of such a relationship does not imply
> that a being would or even could respond to any friendly engagement by
> another."

The criterion itself (p. 394, immediately preceding): "the more a being is
capable of being part of a friendly or loving relationship with normal humans,
the greater its moral status." And the abstract (p. 387): "the greater a being's
capacity to be part of a communal relationship with us, the greater its moral
status."

The status-difference is reaffirmed (p. 395): "being capable of both subject and
object of a communal relationship constitutes a higher status than merely being
the object."

### FLAGS

- **"merely the object" / "merely being the object" IS Metz's own phrase** — not
  our gloss. He writes "capable of being **merely the object** of such behaviour
  has **partial** moral status" (p. 394) and "**merely being the object**"
  (p. 395). Our gloss "partial moral status goes to those that can be merely
  objects" is accurate to his wording and his structure.
- **The status he grants objects-of-relating is "partial," and he says so
  explicitly** (the word is his: "partial moral status," p. 394). It is NOT
  full. Full status requires being capable of *both* subject and object. So our
  "partial" is exactly right — do not upgrade.
- **One precision to honor:** Metz's criterion is *modal* — it is the **capacity
  to be** an object (in principle, given the natures of the being and of us),
  not actual being-related-to, that confers status. "This modal view is neither
  that a communal relationship itself has moral status, nor that only those who
  are in such a relationship have it, but is rather that those who could be part
  of it have it" (p. 394). If §3 leans on *actual* recognition conferring
  standing, note that Metz grounds it in the *capacity to be an object*, not in
  any token act of relating. (Contingent facts like being "unknown" or "the
  object of fear or disgust" do **not** reduce status, p. 394.)

### VERDICT — Work 2: **SUPPORTS CLEANLY** (with the modal-capacity precision
noted). The subject-OR-object move, the "merely object," and the "partial"
status are all verbatim his. Our gloss is faithful.

## WORK 3 — Honneth, *Kampf um Anerkennung* (German original, Suhrkamp 1992)

File: `honneth-1992-de.pdf`. Bib coords (for the paper, we CITE the English):
Axel Honneth, *The Struggle for Recognition: The Moral Grammar of Social
Conflicts*, trans. Joel Anderson (Cambridge: Polity / MIT, 1995). German
original verified here: *Kampf um Anerkennung. Zur moralischen Grammatik
sozialer Konflikte* (Frankfurt am Main: Suhrkamp, stw, 1992). Page numbers below
are the **Suhrkamp German edition's own page numbers** (running
headers/footers).

**This is content-confirmation only.** All English below is **agent
translation, to be confirmed against Anderson 1995 before any quotation.**

### Located: the three patterns of recognition

Chapter 5, **"Muster intersubjektiver Anerkennung: Liebe, Recht, Solidarität"**
(Patterns of Intersubjective Recognition: Love, Rights, Solidarity), German pp.
148–211 (TOC, p. 5 of front matter). The three corresponding practical
self-relations are named: **Selbstvertrauen** (self-confidence, from love, p.
167 ff.), **Selbstachtung** (self-respect, from rights/Recht, p. 191 ff.),
**Selbstschätzung / Selbstwertgefühl** (self-esteem, from solidarity, p. 196
ff.; the term is coined at p. ~196). Chapter 9 ("Intersubjektive Bedingungen
personaler Integrität," pp. 274 ff.) restates the triad as the cleanest summary.

### Confirmation that self-relations are constituted THROUGH recognition

**GERMAN (Vorwort / Klappentext, summary of Part II):**

> "…auf diese Weise entsteht ein intersubjektivitätstheoretisches
> Personenkonzept, innerhalb dessen sich die Möglichkeit einer ungestörten
> Selbstbeziehung als abhängig von drei Formen der Anerkennung (Liebe, Recht,
> Wertschätzung) erweist."

**Agent English (to be confirmed against Anderson 1995):** "…in this way there
arises an intersubjectivity-theoretic concept of the person, within which the
possibility of an undisturbed relation-to-self proves to be *dependent on* three
forms of recognition (love, rights, esteem)."

**GERMAN (Ch. 5, the explicit rights↔love parallel, p. 191):**

> "Daß Selbstachtung für das Rechtsverhältnis das ist, was für die
> Liebesbeziehung das Selbstvertrauen war…"

**Agent English (unconfirmed):** "That self-respect is, for the relation of
right, what self-confidence was for the love-relationship…"

**GERMAN (Ch. 9 synthesis, p. 277 — the strongest single line):**

> "…in der Erfahrung von Liebe die Chance des Selbstvertrauens, in der
> Erfahrung von rechtlicher Anerkennung die der Selbstachtung und in der
> Erfahrung von Solidarität schließlich die der Selbstschätzung angelegt. …
> Denn der Umstand, daß die Möglichkeit der positiven Selbstbeziehung allein mit
> der Erfahrung von Anerkennung gegeben ist, läßt sich als ein Hinweis auf
> notwendige Bedingungen der individuellen Selbstverwirklichung verstehen."

**Agent English (unconfirmed):** "…in the experience of love lies the chance of
self-confidence; in the experience of legal recognition, that of self-respect;
and in the experience of solidarity, finally, that of self-esteem. … For the
circumstance that the possibility of a positive relation-to-self is given
*only* with the experience of recognition can be understood as an indication of
the necessary conditions of individual self-realization."

**GERMAN (Ch. 5 summary, p. 269 region — cumulative-acquisition formulation):**

> "…denn nur dank des kumulativen Erwerbs von Selbstvertrauen, Selbstachtung
> und Selbstschätzung, wie ihn nacheinander die Erfahrung von jenen drei Formen
> der Anerkennung garantiert, vermag eine Person sich uneingeschränkt als ein
> sowohl autonomes wie auch individuiertes Wesen zu begreifen…"

**Agent English (unconfirmed):** "…for only thanks to the cumulative acquisition
of self-confidence, self-respect, and self-esteem — which the experience of
those three forms of recognition successively guarantees — is a person able to
grasp herself without restriction as a being that is both autonomous and
individuated…"

### FLAG (German vs. the standard English gloss — drift to watch)

The gloss "**selfhood is intersubjectively constituted, not antecedent**" is
*directionally* right but slightly overstates. Honneth's German is consistently
about the **possibility of a positive / undisturbed *relation-to-self*
(Selbstbeziehung)** being *dependent on / given only with* recognition —
i.e., it is the **practical self-relation** (Selbstvertrauen / Selbstachtung /
Selbstschätzung) that is intersubjectively constituted, not a bare metaphysical
claim that there is no subject prior to recognition. His verbs are "abhängig
von" (dependent on), "allein … gegeben" (given only), "Voraussetzungen /
notwendige Bedingungen" (presuppositions / necessary conditions). So the precise
claim is: **the developed, practical relation-to-self is intersubjectively
constituted through recognition** — which is exactly what §3's "constitutive
limb" needs, and is strong. Just avoid implying Honneth denies any pre-recognition
subject; he is making a claim about successful selfhood / Selbstverwirklichung,
not about the bare existence of a subject. The "shoulder, cited as precedent"
framing is well supported and does not need a verbatim quote.

### VERDICT — Work 3: **SUPPORTS CLEANLY as precedent.** Content confirmed: the
three patterns (love→Selbstvertrauen, rights→Selbstachtung,
solidarity→Selbstschätzung) and the through-recognition constitution of the
practical self-relation are genuinely Honneth's. Cite Anderson 1995, Ch. 5 (and
Ch. 9 for the summary). One gloss precision: it is the *practical relation-to-
self* that is intersubjectively constituted; don't overstate to "no subject is
antecedent." All English above is agent translation — confirm against Anderson
1995 before quoting.
