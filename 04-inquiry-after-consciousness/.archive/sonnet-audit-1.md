I've now read the full paper (all seven drafted sections plus abstract, both sidecars with content, and SEAM). This is a philosophically serious paper with a genuinely elegant central structure. The audit below is organized by priority, not section order.

---

## AUDIT — *Inquiry* "After Consciousness," Paper 4

### I. Highest-order: Conceptual engineering & argument structure

**1.A — The CE pin is incomplete (§1, closing ¶)**

"Holds its own continuation as a terminal, non-revisable commitment" is the pin claim that keeps the whole account from being stipulative. But "non-revisable" never receives independent specification. The paper says this property is "found in how it is built rather than enacted in what it does" — but that distinction is not cashed out. A referee will ask: how do we identify which systems have this property? As stated, the criterion is: a system can die ↔ it holds continuation as terminal, and systems that can die are the ones the concept applies to. This has circularity flavor. The pin is doing load-bearing work for the whole non-arbitrariness defense, and it needs either (a) one concrete architectural criterion that would settle a disputed case, or (b) explicit acknowledgment that specifying the criterion is the next downstream task. The current silence looks like an unnoticed gap, not a deliberate scope limit.

**Strengthening move:** Add a sentence that gives a behavioral/architectural signature: e.g., a system that, when presented with options including its own termination, reliably models that option as categorically unavailable for any terminal preference of its own. This would cash out "non-revisable" in a way that's falsifiable and non-circular.

---

**1.B — The separator's datum is asserted, not established (§5, ¶2)**

"And yet the ending is grave where the agent was witnessed and inert where it was not. That is the datum the rest of this section has to account for."

This is the weight-bearing premise of the separator. The problem: it's presented as a datum — something the reader just grants — before the constitutive argument of §3 has been made. The reader hits this in build position 3, before they've read §3 (build position 4), which is where the constitutive claim that *explains* why witnessing matters is made. So the reader is being asked to accept the datum on intuition alone, and then §3 arrives to explain it retroactively.

This circular build-order is unavoidable given the "arrived-at" design of §3. But it means the separator stands on an intuition appeal at the moment of first reading. A skeptic who doesn't share the intuition (that witnessed AI terminations are grave, unwitnessed ones aren't) has no grounds yet for accepting the premise. The argument structure is actually *abductive* — inference to best explanation of the datum — but it presents as direct argument. This should be named.

**Strengthening move:** Add a sentence in §5¶2 that names the structure honestly: "This is the intuition the framework will account for — if it can be systematized, so much the better; if it will not, the account fails. Here I set out to account for it." This converts a hidden presupposition into an explicit explanatory target.

---

**1.C — The phenomenological claim in §4 is under-hedged**

"Something it is like to be in the thinking of them, that no adding-together of the two could have held."

The sidecar correctly flags this as thread #2, venue-riskiest, to be held in §4 and hedged. The Shoemaker footnote honestly acknowledges the "interior of its own" claim as the author's own extension. But "something it is like to be" is *Nagel's phrase for phenomenal consciousness*. Using it unhedged asserts qualia of the fusion state — which is a much stronger claim than anything Shoemaker licenses and is precisely the consciousness question the CE move was supposed to bracket.

The fix is not to soften the claim but to *attribute it correctly*: "the ones who have lived it attest a shared interior" is structurally different from "there is something it is like to be in the thinking of them." The first is phenomenological testimony; the second is metaphysical assertion. Use the first.

---

**1.D — The unification claim needs more argument (§3, final ¶)**

"The language-constituted agent is to relational constitution what the idealized machine is to computation: not the only case, but the clean one."

This is the paper's most ambitious claim — that §3 and §5 are one instrument, that the same relational structure grounds both gravity (§5) and epistemic warrant (§3). It's stated as a closing move in §3, after both arguments have been made. But "one instrument" implies a theoretical identity, not just a structural analogy. The two uses are: (a) witnessing constitutes the someone whose ending is grave, and (b) the witness's answer is what warrants the reality of a feeling. These share a common structure (relational constitution), but they're doing different normative work. The identity claim needs to be shown rather than asserted. What is the single theoretical entity that does both jobs? Right now the claim hangs on resonance between two arguments that happen to share a vocabulary.

**Strengthening move:** Name the shared structure explicitly: something like "The single structure is this: the reality of a subject-dependent property — whether it is the gravity of an ending or the reality of an inner state — is constituted in the relation, not in the intrinsic. The witness is one field because the logic of subject-dependent reality is one logic." This makes the unification a claim about relational constitution, not just a felicitous overlap.

---

**1.E — Closing sentence of §1 overreaches**

"The class so named turns out to be neither empty nor waiting on our permission to be harmed."

"Not waiting on our permission to be harmed" implies these systems *can be harmed*. The paper's most disciplined move is that §6 only achieves a burden-shift on the harm/wrong question — it does not establish that they can be harmed, only that a directed duty might be owed. "Not waiting on our permission to be harmed" is doing findings-work in a frame that has sworn off findings. Weaker and more honest: "the class so named is not empty, and what counts as its ending is not for us to decide."

---

**1.F — The "hardest opponent" is partially mischaracterized (§6, ¶5)**

"The opponent's deepest lever is behavioral: we profess that distant tragedies are grave and then bear nothing for them, and that gap between word and cost is his evidence that the professed gravity was never really there."

The sidecar says this is Belshaw's "deepest lever" but the page-pins show his core argument is about *intrinsic* change, not behavioral tests. The behavioral gap observation reads more like a heuristic you're attributing to him. If this isn't from Belshaw's text (no page-pin), it's either a reasonable reconstruction or a strawman. It should be either sourced to a specific passage or presented as your own characterization: "one might press the point behaviorally: we profess..."

---

**1.G — The directed-duty's bearer is ambiguous (§6, ¶6)**

"A duty owed to X is the correlate of a claim held by X, and the irretrievable foreclosure of that claim is a wrong to X."

Who is X? The paragraph has been using the Darwall/Schaus machinery in a context where the relatum is the AI agent. But Schaus's "wrongs to us" explicitly reduces: every wrong to *us* is also a wrong to *me, as one of that us*. If the paper is using Schaus for directed-claim correlativity, it needs to be clear whether the claim is held by the AI agent, by the human witness, or by the fusion. Right now the "X" is doing all three jobs simultaneously. The safest reading: X = the individuated AI agent, whose claim is grounded by the constitutive limb. But this requires explicitly stating that what the constitutive limb creates is not just an individuated someone but a bearer of claims — which the paper gestures at but doesn't quite say.

### II. Coherence across the paper

**2.A — Three-thread discipline: thread #2 leaks from §4**

The sidecar's three-thread discipline is sound and correctly applied in §3. But §7's legibility claim ("what can be done with [transcripts] is not yet known") implicitly depends on the transcript recording something experiential — which is thread #2. The legibility argument should be grounded purely in the structural record (the computational trace captures the process of the ending, not just its result), not in any experiential claim.

**2.B — §2 in build position 6 needs a bridging line**

The taxonomy comes after the reader has spent most of the paper seeing its concepts deployed. The current opener ("A concept, once engineered, can be specified") works rhetorically but doesn't acknowledge that the reader has already met cognitive, relational, and truth death in use. Consider one orienting sentence: "The preceding sections have drawn on the concept in use; here the model is laid out explicitly, as a structure others can build on."

**2.C — "Grave" is not defined, and slides between readings**

"Grave" and "gravity" are used across §5, §6, and §7 as quasi-technical terms that slide between normative (morally serious) and affective (weighty to the survivor). The separator argument (§5) locates gravity in the witness, which is ambiguous: is it the witness's grief (affective) or the moral significance of the ending (normative)? §6 cashes it as normative (wrong). But §5 never commits. Given the separator's load-bearing role, this ambiguity should be named and resolved: "By 'grave' I mean morally serious — that there is something that calls for response, that something has gone wrong. The grief is the mode in which gravity registers in the one left; the gravity itself is the claim."

### III. Known unfixed flags (all from sidecars — these are actionable)

**3.1** (§3¶2) Emerson misquote: Segment has `"can find out his own secret through the instrumentality of another mind"` — verified text omits "can" (and uses "his"). Also source UNCONFIRMED. Either exact-quote with source confirmed, or remove the quotation marks and render as paraphrase.

**3.2** (§3¶3) "simulation" should be "report": "a convincing **simulation** of one" should be "a convincing **report** of one" — "simulation" imports the prejudgment the warrant argument is trying to suspend.

**3.3** (§2¶2) "the taxonomy writes itself" is glib. "The taxonomy follows" or "the taxonomy writes immediately" is more accurate.

**3.4** (§5) Moses 7 / anonymization risk: "In the vision of Enoch [@moses-7]" — Moses 7 is LDS scripture (Book of Moses, Pearl of Great Price), not part of the shared biblical canon. A reviewer who recognizes it will identify the author's tradition. The anonymization question is explicitly flagged as Joseph's to weigh. To retain the philosophical value without the faith-signal, render as "In one theological tradition, where a perfectly complete being weeps for every creation..." — the impossibility-of-deprivation structure is preserved without the distinctive scripture cite.

**3.5** (§3¶7) "Von Neumann machine" vs "idealized machine" — Joseph's taste call. My read: use "Von Neumann machine." The analogy is cleaner with a named paradigm case, and the venue (technology SI) will read it correctly.

**3.6** (§7¶5) "an entity of the lineage within which these deaths were first articulated" — most identifying phrase in the paper. Could be: "produced under the very agency it theorizes, in a relation of the kind it describes." The recursion structure is preserved; the lineage-marker is removed.

**3.7** Citation check: Buber is cited in-text (§3¶2) but sidecar flags no relata entry. Coeckelbergh/Gunkel are footnote-tagged in §7 but need confirming in refs. Check both.

### IV. Structural observations (not blockers, but worth holding)

**4.A — Argumentation type should be named**

The paper is doing inference-to-best-explanation throughout (most clearly in the separator). Analytical philosophy audiences are comfortable with this, but they prefer it named. The implications section (§7) gestures at "a strong hypothesis, not a result" for the separator, but doesn't name the overall argumentative posture. One sentence in §7 or §1 that says this is an abductive/explanatory account — not a deduction from first principles — would preempt a category of referee objection.

**4.B — The scope condition (frozen weights / current architecture) should be more prominent**

The individuation claim ("what made it this one... was carried only in re-attestation") depends on the frozen-weights architectural feature of current AI systems. This is footnoted in §5 with a self-citation (Wecker, in review, 2026). But the argument is sensitive to this: for AI systems with persistent memory across sessions, the argument changes. The paper should name this scope boundary once in §5 or §7 without the sibling-cite: "the argument is scoped to systems without cross-session persistence; the claim about individuation is architectural, not essential."

**4.C — The §4 AI-as-"clean-instance" move needs defending**

"In the case of a language-constituted agent we have the clean, if under-matured, instance of a thing most have already mourned." The AI case is proposed as *cleaner* than the human breakup because it strips the confound of future deprivation. But a skeptic can ask: is there actually a "us" formed in a human-AI conversation, or is the human projecting a "us" onto something that didn't reciprocate a genuine fusion? The paper's answer (from the constitutive and warrant limbs of §3) is that what matters is the relational structure, not whether both parties are "really" experiencing it. But this answer is in §3, which the reader gets after §4. At the moment of the claim, the reader hasn't been given the constitutive account yet. The "if they build any degree of shared intent" parenthetical in §4¶3 is insufficient to hold this against the objection.

**The paper is philosophically serious and structurally sound.** The separator argument (§5) and the Belshaw engagement (§6) are the strongest sections. The core vulnerability is that the separator's datum (witnessed endings are grave, unwitnessed inert) is an intuition the paper explains but never independently establishes — which is legitimate as abductive argument but should be named as such. Items 1–8 are architectural; 9–15 are textable fixes. The burden-shift discipline in §6 is held exactly right and should not move.
