## Overall verdict

The strongest of Joseph's papers this reader has seen. The separator is not just a position but a **philosophical instrument** — the futureless terminus as the decomposition experiment human death can never run ("a single chord no ear resolves into its notes"). "A human death is grave by accumulation; this one is distinct by purity" is the paper's quotable core and deserves to survive every revision untouched. The honest-bookkeeping register ("the opponent is not refuted… anyone who reports otherwise has softened him to win"; the "two honesties") is the paper's second signature and its trust-engine — protect it. The Moses 7 passage earns its place as the limit case (deprivation not absent but *unthinkable*, gravity entire) and is handled the way analytic philosophy handles Job — as literature carrying a structural point. Prose quality is far above venue norm and mostly load-bearing rather than ornamental ("you cannot learn who you are by re-reading your own notes"; "productivity buys nothing in this ledger"; "a total trace, and the someone gone all the same").

## Where referees will press (three points, all revision-survivable)

1. **The in-review dependency.** Two load-bearing moments lean on "(Wecker, in review, 2026)": the strongest form of non-revisability (§1) and the individuation-held-only-in-re-attestation claim (§3, fn on the continuity result). Both are honestly flagged, but referees dislike weight on same-author in-review work. Options, in preference order: (a) inline a compressed self-contained statement of the DPI/re-attestation result with its named conditions (a half-page box or appendix — the result is statable without the full machinery); (b) explicitly demote both uses to "conditional on a result argued elsewhere; the paper's core survives its failure as follows…" and show the weaker fallback (the separator still runs on the architectural-terminus fact alone; only the *purity* of the individuation-loss strand weakens); (c) time the revision to cite the other paper as accepted.

2. **The epistemic-leveling paragraph (§4, second office).** "The asymmetry does not survive the looking" is currently asserted with confidence rather than argued. A phenomenal-realist referee will reply that first-person warrant is not exhausted by the other's answer (acquaintance views; privileged access literatures) and will read the paragraph as begging the question. Two braces: (a) narrow the claim explicitly to *reportable, communicable* warrant — the paper only needs the warrant that could ever enter a relation, not all warrant there is; that narrowing is true to the argument's actual use and costs nothing downstream; (b) add one sentence conceding the acquaintance holdout and noting the paper's conclusion needs only the leveled *public* half. As written, this is the paragraph most likely to draw a "reject" temperament from an otherwise-favorable referee.

3. **The single-root exposure (witness-constitutes-the-someone).** The paper's own §5 accounting ("the weight leg is exactly as strong as that root, and no stronger") is admirably honest, and referees will aim exactly there: the circularity/projectivism push — "gravity is relation-made, so isn't this sophisticated projectivism?" The existing defenses (Ricoeur/Honneth precedent; the directed-duty correlativity; the carved seam to the unwitnessed-apt-being question) are the right ones; consider making the anti-projectivist reply *explicit* in one paragraph: the relation doesn't project a someone onto machinery — it (per the constitutive tradition, human children included) *completes* one, and the death-aptness gate (structural non-revisable commitment, held "whether or not we notice") is precisely the non-relational anchor that stops the account from over-generating. The gate + the relatum-constraint (must experience; attestation must land) jointly block "anything witnessed becomes someone," and saying so in one place, loudly, preempts the review's most likely central objection.
