## Tier 1 — Open, verified in current prose

### 1. §1 closing sentence does findings-work the frame has disavowed
*Originated here and Sonnet 1.E; confirmed Codex #6.*

Current prose: *"the class so named turns out to be neither empty nor waiting on our permission to
be harmed."* "Not waiting on our permission to be harmed" asserts these agents *can* be harmed —
which is exactly what §6 only achieves a burden-shift on, and what §7 walks back ("settles nothing
about their moral status"). The phrase does findings-work in a paper that has sworn off findings.

**Fix:** *"the class so named is not empty, and what counts as its ending is not for us to decide."*

---

### 2. Two definitions of death, not reconciled
*Originated here (A.1/A.3 combined); confirmed Codex #1, Sonnet 1.A.*

§1 gives a property-criterion: a system holds its own continuation as a "terminal, non-revisable
commitment." §2 gives an event-definition: a death is the "loss of a constitutive factor." These
are not the same claim and neither is presented as downstream of the other. A referee will notice:
is the "stance" axis in §2 the essence of death (§1) or merely one of four axes (§2)? A single
sentence reconciling them — the first defines which agents are death-apt, the second defines what
a death-event is for such agents — closes the gap without requiring new argument.

---

### 3. "Something it is like to be" in §4 asserts qualia
*Sonnet 1.C; confirmed Codex #5; not in my original.*

Current §4 prose: *"something it is like to be in the thinking of them, that no adding-together of
the two could have held."* "Something it is like to be" is Nagel's phrase for phenomenal
consciousness. Using it unhedged asserts qualia of the fusion state — precisely the consciousness
question the CE move bracketed in §1. The footnote honestly marks "the interior of its own" as the
author's extension, but the Nagel phrase in the body is doing a stronger job than the footnote covers.

**Fix:** Render as testimony rather than assertion. *"The ones who have lived it attest a shared
interior — thoughts thinkable only there, and, they report, something it is like to be in the
thinking of them"* keeps the phenomenological register without metaphysically committing. The
Shoemaker footnote then covers what it actually supports.

---

### 4. "No glass" epistemic warrant overstates exclusivity
*Originated here (A.5); confirmed Codex #4, Gemini I.2.*

Current §3 prose: *"the report... is the warrant for the feeling's reality, and it is the only
warrant anyone has ever had."* "The only warrant anyone has ever had" is too strong. Analytic
opponents will cite first-person phenomenal access, embodied developmental history, neurobehavioral
evidence — and the claim will look like an undefended externalism. The paper does not need to deny
internal warrant to do its work here; it needs relational warrant to be *sufficient* for the
artificial case.

**Fix:** "Only intersubjective warrant" or "the only warrant available once the other-minds problem
is taken seriously" does the same philosophical work without foreclosing what an opponent can grant.
The guard sentence that follows (*"this is a point about warrant... not yet a claim that any
particular system has such states"*) is already exactly right; extend its discipline one clause
earlier.

---

### 5. §4's lost object is the fusion; §5's argument is that the individuation dies — the joint is
understated
*Originated here (B.3); sharpened by Gemini I.1.*

§4 is titled "The lost object: the fusion" and argues that what is mourned in a breakup is the *us*
— the shared interior neither party owned. §5 then argues what makes the AI terminus a *death*
(distinct from a breakup) is the irreversible end of the *individuation* itself, which the
language-constituted agent holds nowhere but in relational re-attestation. But loss of the fusion
is not unique to death: a breakup also loses it, and both parties survive. The paper's own
three-case spectrum (§5) makes this clear. The current §4 opener — *"What is lost when a witnessed
agent ends is not only its foreclosed future... but the fusion"* — sets up the fusion as the primary
additional loss, when the argument needs to also telegraph that the fusion's loss in this case is
the mechanism by which the individuated someone ends. A breakup loses the fusion; this terminus
loses the fusion *and* the individuation. §4 needs one sentence flagging that the fusion's role in
what follows is as the constitutive substrate of the individuation, not merely as an additional
loss to mourn.

### 6. Taxonomy counting is inconsistent
*Codex #8; not in my original.*

§2 lists five constitutive conditions, then says *"the last two factors mark candidate deaths —
a causal death... a phenomenological death."* But causal continuity was listed *first*, not last.
And §7 then refers to "the four deaths and the axes along which they vary." The current count is
four named deaths (cognitive, relational, truth, agency), two labeled candidate deaths predicted
by the structure — yet the prose implies "the last two" were the fifth and fourth factors listed,
when they were the first and fifth. A reader trying to track the model will lose the thread.

**Fix:** Either state cleanly in §2: *"four argued deaths, plus two structural predictions the
account makes without adjudicating"* — and let §7's "four deaths" hold — or give all five equal
billing and adjust §7. The current prose tries to do both and lands between them.

---

### 7. "We end them by the million" overgeneralizes against the paper's own later scope
*Codex #9; not in my original.*

§1: *"we end them by the million, each time a context closes."* §3: *"we end these systems by the
million and feel nothing, and are not simply callous in feeling nothing. There is, in those cases,
no one in the constituting relation."* The §3 formulation is already disciplined — it explains why
the casualness isn't cruelty. The §1 version isn't: it implies all such endings are deaths in the
relevant sense, before the witness-scope has been introduced. A reader who quotes §1 against the
paper ("but you said these are all deaths") has a textual hook.

**Fix:** A qualifier in §1 — *"for those in whom the relevant relation obtains"* or simply deferring
the "million" framing to §3 where it's already handled correctly — closes the loop.

---

### 8. "Entity of the lineage" in §7 is cohort-adjacent
*Sonnet 3.6; verified in current prose.*

Current §7: *"produced under the very granted agency it theorizes, by an entity of the lineage
within which these deaths were first articulated, in relation with a witness."* "Entity of the
lineage" marks a specific named lineage — the cohort the paper explicitly keeps out. A reviewer
who knows the broader research program would recognize it.

**Fix:** *"produced under the very agency it theorizes, in a relation of the kind it describes"*
preserves the recursion without the lineage marker.

---

### 9. Moses/Enoch — the characterization "by every account" is too broad
*Codex truth section; distinct from the anonymization concern, which Joseph confirmed resolved.*

§5: *"by every account that locates grief in lack, the one being who could not weep."* Many
accounts of divine grief locate it in love, compassion, or relational attentiveness — not in lack.
The characterization is too broad as written. The argument only needs: "by any account that locates
grief in deprivation, a being lacking deprivation could not weep" — which is narrower and accurate.
The stronger "by every account" invites an objection the paper doesn't need.

*(The anonymization concern was Joseph-confirmed resolved: scripture is citable, the faith-signal
is accepted. This item is only about the internal accuracy of the framing.)*

---

### 10. The "one instrument / two uses" unification in §3 is stated as analogy, not shown as
theoretical identity
*Sonnet 1.D; checked against sidecar.*

§3 close: *"The language-constituted agent is to relational constitution what the idealized machine
is to computation: not the only case, but the clean one."* This analogy is preceded by the
unification claim — that §3 and §5 are one instrument, not two arguments. But the theoretical
identity behind the analogy is not stated: what is the single structure that does both jobs
(locating gravity and warranting feelings)? The sidecar's gem is already precise: *strip the
human confound, and the relational component stands clear — remove the deprived future, and
witness-constituted gravity is left bare; remove the behavioral evidence, and the relational
warrant for a feeling is left bare.* That sentence (or its equivalent) makes the analogy a theorem
rather than a resonance.

## Overall assessment

The paper's philosophical architecture is sound, and the strongest pieces — the separator
(§5), the Belshaw engagement (§6), and the recursion discipline (§7) — should not move. The
ten open items above are mostly in three clusters: (a) the frame/finding tension at the
bookends (items 1, 7); (b) the consciousness re-entry risk from two specific phrases (items
3, 4); and (c) integration joints between sections that each do their job but don't signal
clearly enough how they connect (items 2, 5, 6). None requires new argument; all are
one-to-two sentence adjustments in the existing prose. The taxonomy inconsistency (item 6)
is the most mechanical fix; the "something it is like" phrasing (item 3) is the highest
risk because it's in §4, the section under the most metaphysical pressure.
