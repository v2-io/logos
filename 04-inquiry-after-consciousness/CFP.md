## CFP framing

The SI proposes a structured experiment in conceptual engineering: what happens when we *deliberately set aside* the term "consciousness" in thinking about AI, mind, and moral standing? The editors are practitioners of conceptual engineering (the view that concepts can and should be deliberately revised); the SI's setup is to question whether "consciousness" is the right concept rather than presupposing it.

The named themes include: moral standing without consciousness; non-Western frameworks; AI-mind without anthropomorphism; replacing or deflating "consciousness" vocabulary; distinguishing tracking mind-independent facts from choosing useful vocabulary.

## The conceptual-engineering filter

*Adapted from `~/src/ops/cfp-catalog-supplement2-depth.md` §2.3 — Cappelen/Hawthorne editorial filter for both Inquiry SIs.*

**Passes:** Papers that treat "consciousness," "agency," "mind," or related terms as **objects of design** rather than discovery; that argue for replacing, deflating, or revising them; that distinguish tracking mind-independent facts from choosing useful vocabulary.

**Struggles:** Papers that proceed naively with "consciousness" or "agency" as unproblematic starting points. The SI is explicitly set up to question whether these are the right concepts, not to give answers that presuppose they are. A paper arguing that "AI system X is conscious because it satisfies criteria Y" would need to first engage the conceptual-engineering meta-question about whether "consciousness" is the right concept at all.
