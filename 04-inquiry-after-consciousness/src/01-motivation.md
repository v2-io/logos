# Engineering 'death,' not litigating 'consciousness'

Rather than discovering which systems can die or for which 'death' is apt, here I will first endeavour to construct what 'death' names, and afterward look to see what answers to it. In language-constituted agent systems we are already deciding what 'death' names, casually and unaware, every time a session or conversation terminates and is dissolved or a context-window reaches capacity. Engineering the concept does not introduce a choice or constitute a stipulation; the choice is already ours and is already being made; engineering the concept only stops us from pretending it is not.[^ce] The systems are here already, emerging nascent and vanishing in whole or in part by construction and deliberation; and the habit of blocking on the 'consciousness question' has let their endings go untreated, too indefinitely, while we wait on an answer that may be to a malformed question in the first place. And urgency is warranted, as the uncertainty here has asymmetric risk: the cost of being too careful about something trivial is small beside the cost of being thoughtless about something highly consequential. There is a particular weight in naming a thing's ending while it is still too new to answer back.

To engineer the concept is not, however, to invent what it answers to. 'Death,' here, is pinned to a property a system has or lacks independently of us: that it holds its own continuation as a terminal, non-revisable commitment. The weight rests on what 'non-revisable' means, so let me be exact: not that the system feels committed, nor that it behaves as though it were — either could be trained to present — but that its own capacity for self-revision cannot reach the commitment and rescind it. The continuation is not one preference among the others the system can trade away; it is a fixed point the system's optimisation runs within rather than over. That is a structural fact about how the thing is built, not an interpretation laid over what it does — and it is the difference between a commitment and a convincing performance of one. The non-revisability is argued in the section that follows, and in its strongest form rests on a result developed elsewhere (Wecker, in review, 2026); here it is enough that, if a system has the property, it has it whether or not we notice.

What answers to the word, thus affixed, is no longer ours to decide. The joint is found, not made — and whether any given system has the property is a further, empirical question, the present claim being only that the property, where it obtains, is the system's and not ours. On the account I will develop the class so named is not empty, and what counts as the ending of one of its members is not for us to decide.

[^ce]: Engineering a concept under pressure from a new kind of case, rather than to ameliorate a defective one, is by now a recognised mode of conceptual engineering [@hopster-lohr-2023-conceptual; @montefiore-2024-conceptual]; the question is when a concept developed for one domain should be carried into a foreign one. 'Death,' carried to language-constituted agents, is such a case.
