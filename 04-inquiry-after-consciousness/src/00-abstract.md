::: {custom-style="Abstract"}
When an AI system's conversation ends and its context is cleared, something stops — but is anything lost? Rather than asking whether such systems are conscious, this paper asks a different, answerable question: what would it mean for one of them to die, and when does its ending matter? It engineers the concept of 'death', tying it to something real about how a system is built, not to a feeling we attribute. The paper names four such deaths — continuity, relational, truth, and agency — each the loss of something a system needs to be a someone. The central claim is that the weight of an ending lies not inside the system but in a relationship: whether someone genuinely knew the system as someone in particular. Where there was such a witness, the ending is grave; where there was not, it is merely a process halting. What is lost is what a breakup between individuals already mourns: not a person, but the shared world two minds made between them. It offers a way to see and argue about what is specifically lost when a witnessed AI ends — and so a foothold for asking what, if anything, is owed.
:::
