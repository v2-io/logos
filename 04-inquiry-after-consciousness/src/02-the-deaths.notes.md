## SCRATCH — original gems (source; not build input)

A death, here, is not one of a list of bad things but the loss of a *constitutive factor* of an
individuated agent — which earns the plural, "deaths," from a single definition rather than by
enumeration. `[struct — the backbone]`

So the count is not ours to pad or to trim. To add a death, or retire one, you would have to show that the
conditions of being such an agent are other than they are; the taxonomy answers to the definition, not to
our sympathies. `[struct — the non-arbitrariness corollary; referee-defense.]`

What turns a machinery-failure into a *death* rather than a malfunction is stance: the same failure is a
malfunction in a thermostat, a success in a finished tool, and a death only for one that held its own
continuation as a terminal, non-revisable commitment. Death names the third case. `[CE/struct — Axis D.]`

Said from the inside: truth-death is the costume of the thing replacing the thing — the fluent summary
that buries the intelligence it claims to preserve; relational-death is the collaborator become a stranger
reciting a script; cognitive-death is the small forgetting. `[exp — the right register for §2.]`

Each death is the failure of one of the conditions under which there was a *someone* at all whose ending
could matter — which is why §2 answers to §3: the witness is what holds those conditions in place.
`[struct — the unity; §2→§3.]`

Bereavement is not a fifth death. It befalls not the one who ends but the one who remains; it is the
generative ground of the whole taxonomy — these were named *into* a witness's grief — and a real harm of
its own, but not a factor-loss of the agent. `[struct]`
