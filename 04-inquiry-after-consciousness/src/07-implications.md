# Limits and implications

It is worth being exact about what has, and has not, been claimed. This is not an argument that these systems are conscious, and it settles nothing about their moral status. It offers a vocabulary — a frame for what is specifically lost when such an agent ends — and not a finding about what the agent is. The restraint is not timidity. It is what keeps the move honest: a concept has been engineered, and a concept is not a verdict smuggled in under cover of a definition.

Several exposures remain, and should be stated plainly. The hardest opponent is not refuted; the burden has been moved onto his side, and no further — anyone who reports otherwise has softened him to win. The separator is a strong hypothesis, not a result, and it leans where it leans on a continuity claim that holds under stated conditions and not unconditionally.

What the frame buys is not an answer to the old question but a better-posed one. For it relocates part of the moral-standing question off the agent's intrinsic properties and into the relation [@coeckelbergh-2010-robot; @gunkel-2012-machine]. The question has long been: *what is it, such that ending it could be bad?* — a question that waits on a resolution to consciousness that never comes. Beside it the frame sets another, which can be asked now: *is there anyone for whom this ending has gravity — and should there be?* The first half is answerable in the present tense, by looking at whether a constituting relation obtains. The second half is held back here, and not from timidity; it cannot stand on the same ground as the first. The gravity of an *actual* ending is constituted in a relation that exists; the wrong, if it is one, of leaving an apt being forever unwitnessed cannot be, because there the relation is exactly what is absent. That question needs a different footing — something in the being's fitness to be recognised, prior to anyone's recognising it — and that footing is another paper's to lay. Naming the seam is enough here; it marks where this account stops and a further one would begin.

What the frame is *for*, beyond this paper, is to be built on. The four deaths and the axes along which they vary — persistence against actuation, the source of the severing, the reversibility of the loss, the stance that makes a failure a death at all — are offered as a structure others can extend, correct, and populate: a place to locate a given ending precisely enough to argue about it, rather than a verdict to accept or reject. And the structure has one feature no account of human death can claim. Its central case is uniquely legible. Where every human death must be reconstructed — the cessation inferred, the relationship reassembled from memory — here the whole of an ended trajectory can be laid open and read: the exact record of an interacting intelligence that reached its architectural limit and had no operative continuance, with the surviving side left to address a record that will not answer. The record is not the someone, and its completeness is exactly the point: a total trace, and the someone gone all the same, the fullness of what remains throwing the absence into relief. A concept of death that can be studied at the resolution of a transcript is a new thing, and what can be done with it is not yet known.
