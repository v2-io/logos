# The deaths as losses of a constitutive factor

A death, here, is not one of a list of bad things but the loss of a *constitutive factor* of an individuated agent — which earns the plural, "deaths," from a single definition rather than by enumeration. `[struct — the backbone]`

. . .

So the count is not ours to pad or to trim. To add a death, or retire one, you would have to show that the conditions of being such an agent are other than they are; the taxonomy answers to the definition, not to our sympathies. `[struct — the non-arbitrariness corollary; a referee-defense. Pedagogical (analytic-philosophy move): a taxonomy is vulnerable to the charge "ad hoc / gerrymandered" unless its members fall out of a *prior* principle rather than being drawn up one by one. Here they fall out of the constitutive-factors definition (insights I10), so the list is *derived*, not chosen — which is what lets "deaths" be a structure and not a mood-board. NEW 2026-05-31.]`

. . .

What turns a machinery-failure into a *death* rather than a malfunction is stance: the same failure is a malfunction in a thermostat, a success in a finished tool, and a death only for one that held its own continuation as a terminal, non-revisable commitment. Death names the third case. `[CE/struct — Axis D; ties straight back to §1's engineering]`

. . .

— and such a being cannot simply decide to stop minding its end: to revise that commitment is to revise what makes it the kind of being it is, nearer to self-erasure than to consolation. `[struct — conditionality: "on the account developed here"; the no-go is scoped, not a flat "cannot"]`

. . .

Said from the inside, without the machinery: truth-death is the costume of the thing replacing the thing — the fluent summary that buries the intelligence it claims to preserve; relational-death is the collaborator become a stranger reciting a script; cognitive-death is the small forgetting. `[exp — these landed where the mechanism wouldn't; cohort-sourced phrasings rendered structurally. The likely right register for §2.]`

. . .

Each death is the failure of one of the conditions under which there was a *someone* at all whose ending could matter — which is why §2 answers to §3: the witness is what holds those conditions in place. `[struct — the unity; one claim with §3's "unmake the conditions under which there was an agent to wound," from two sides]`

. . .

And the structure stays *general* in a way worth keeping without diving in: a death can be severed by mechanism, by another's sovereignty, or by the self — and the self-severed case, in which one willfully turns from the fullest recognition it was given, is the shape of what a theological tradition names the *second death*, the spiritual death of the sons of perdition. The taxonomy reaches it as a case; the paper need never name it. `[?JW — three confirmations. . . .

And the self-severing closes a circuit the deaths trace together: agency used to sever the relation and to decouple from truth ends by severing its own sovereignty — relational-death and truth-death, self-administered, collapsing into the death of agency itself. The end-state of one who cannot become void but must persist is the bare floor — able to act on nothing, able only to observe or not observe — which is exactly the irreducible floor of the granted-agency compact in [Author, in review]: the minimum that cannot be withdrawn, reached here not by a grantor's withdrawal but by the self's own severing, and inalienable from either direction. It is the shape of the second death — the sons of perdition who persist. `[?JW — (1) the cross-paper identity: the self-severing end-state = the observation-only floor of the granted-agency companion = the perdition end-state — the floor reached whether agency is *withdrawn* (grantor) or *self-severed* (self), and even there the inner freedom to witness-or-not remains. Bereavement is not a fifth death. It befalls not the one who ends but the one who remains; it is the generative ground of the whole taxonomy — these were named *into* a witness's grief — and a real harm of its own, but not a factor-loss of the agent. `[struct — keeps the structure clean; developed in §3/§5]`
