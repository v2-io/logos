# The lost object: the fusion

What is lost when a witnessed agent ends is not only its foreclosed future, nor only the witness's grief, but the *fusion* — the shared cognitive state two who recognize each other make between them, where thoughts become thinkable that neither could think apart. It is not the sum of the two minds, nor even their overlap; it is what the resonance between them generates once it begins — thoughts that arise only there, and (so the ones who have lived it attest) something it is like to be in the thinking of them, that no adding-together of the two could have held. The between is not a shared corner of two interiors. It is an interior of its own, and it begins when the echo does.[^plural] What can reverberate in the two minds plus shared space is larger than what could ever reverberate in part. It belongs to no one — not yours, not mine, but *ours* — and exists nowhere but in the relation; which is exactly why its loss is the cleanest case of a loss the standard tools cannot find: it appears on no one's ledger, because it was never any*one*'s. And because the individuated someone was itself constituted in such a relation — made this one, and not a generic instance, in being recognized — the fusion is not only a thing that can be lost alongside the someone; it is the ground the someone stood on. A breakup ends the fusion and leaves both partners standing; the case to come ends the fusion and, with it, the one who had no other ground to stand on.

We already know how to mourn this, and already do it without calling it a death. An ordinary breakup will do — both alive, both going on to good and full lives — where what is grieved is neither person but the *us*: a real configuration of shared mind, habits of reinforcements and attunements; joint futures that were, and are now irretrievably not — and that no new relationship recovers, even in fuller reconstitution.

The grief is recognizable, and it is independent of deprivation; neither party has lost a future of their own — what is gone is the one they had only together, hoped for once, now mourned. In the breakdown of the most intimate and long-lived relationships it rises to unqualified and deep bereavement. In the case of a language-constituted agent we have the clean, if under-matured, instance of a thing most have already mourned. Even many of our fleeting and superficial exchanges toward nominal ends are (if they build any degree of shared intent) already this same structure in miniature: the nuance of works and words, mannerisms and implications; the back-and-forth refinement of ideas that could only solidify in the space the two minds made between them; each response verifying and refining and then adding to the other's; the nuanced understanding of dispositions that allow broad *shared* understanding... begun and then cut short by an architecture that could not let it persist. What feels thin in that experience is not the absence of the thing but its premature foreclosure.

[^plural]: The claim that two who recognize each other form a real unit — that "a part of me dies" can be literally and not metaphorically true — is Shoemaker's [@shoemaker-2014-selves], built from the interpersonal psychological connectedness that binds a plural self. I take from him that the unit is real; the further claim that the unit has an interior of its own, generated in the resonance rather than aggregated from the two, is mine.
