## Cluster 1 — Badness-of-death & grief/bereavement
*(lookup agent completed 2026-05-29; DOIs machine-checked; verification tier noted per item)*

**Headline (the honest novelty boundary):** locating the *gravity of the death-event itself* in the
witness/relational field — and using a futureless/subjectless case as the *separator* — is unoccupied
in philosophy. But there are real **precursors I extend, not rivals**: the literature has relocated
*grief's intentional object* into the survivor's relationship, and *what-matters* into the surviving
collective. The DIRECT items below are the shoulders to stand on.

### DIRECT (anticipates the relational-locus / separation move)
- **Cholbi, M. (2017). "Grief's Rationality, Backward and Forward." *Phil. & Phenomenological Research*
  94(2):255–272. DOI 10.1111/phpr.12353.** — grief's object is *the survivor's loss of the relationship
  as it was, and its transformation by the death* — an **agent-relative** value, ~p.258. *[primary PDF read]*
  The strongest anchor for relocating the intentional object into the relational field; and the
  relationship-*transformation* framing is the resource against the rename objection (death-event itself
  reconstitutes the relation).
- **Wilkinson, D. (2023). "Grief and the Inconsolation of Philosophy." *Philosophy* 98(3):273–296.
  DOI 10.1017/S0031819123000049. (OA, CC BY).** — grief is "inextricably agent relative," about how bad
  the death is *for those left behind*, "not closely related to deprivation for the deceased"; widow's
  grief not proportional to the deceased's lost future (p.288). *[full text read]* The cleanest human-case
  statement of the gravity-for-survivors vs deprivation-of-the-dead split — stops short of the
  futureless separator (that gap is ours).
- **Millar, B. & Lopez-Cantero, P. (2022). "Grief, Continuing Bonds, and Unreciprocated Love." *Southern
  J. Philosophy* 60(3):413–436. DOI 10.1111/sjp.12462.** — the relationship *continues* post-death in an
  adapted **one-sided/unreciprocated** form, carried asymmetrically by the survivor. *[abstract + citing
  gloss only — body unread]* Closest precursor to "witnessing converts a state-change into a death."

### ADJACENT (positioning / must engage)
- **Bradley, B. (2009). *Well-Being and Death*. OUP.** — *[review/OA-excerpt level]* **The real dialectical
  opponent.** Deprivation harms even the unaware/unexperiencing (difference-making). ⇒ the AI separator
  must turn on *absent future* (genuine terminus), NOT absent experience. Two-part separator structure
  required (see whose-death.md §II).
- **Nagel, T. (1970). "Death." *Noûs* 4(1):73–80.** — canonical deprivation account; locus-in-the-dying.
  *[page-checked quotes via Wilkinson]*
- **Feinberg (1984, *Harm to Others*) & Pitcher (1984, "The Misfortunes of the Dead," *APQ* 21).** — the
  subject-of-harm machinery (ante-mortem person as subject of posthumous harm). *[SEP gloss only — primaries
  unread]* The position the AI case *sidesteps* (no surviving subject of harm needed; gravity is relational).
- **Scheffler, S. (2013). *Death and the Afterlife*. OUP.** — *[secondary only]* what-matters constituted by
  a surviving relational/collective field; precedent for locating value outside the isolated subject.
- **Kolodny, N. (2003). "Love as Valuing a Relationship." *Phil. Review* 112(2):135–189.** — *[via Wilkinson's
  reflist]* relationship as the unit; the "ongoing" feature is what death severs.
- **Luper, S. "Death." *SEP* (2002/rev.2021), plato.stanford.edu/entries/death/.** — *[structure verified]* the
  map of the standard debate. Companion: Luper, *The Philosophy of Death* (CUP 2009).
- **Marušić, B. (2022). *On the Temporality of Emotions* (OUP).** — *[PhilPapers listing]* grief's object &
  temporal rationality; relevant if claiming *why* witnessing sustains gravity over time.
- **Tubera, K.C. & Dacela, M.A. (2026). "Deathbots and the Moral Knowledge of Grief." *Phil. & Technology*
  39(2):87. DOI 10.1007/s13347-026-01101-9.** — *[Crossref-verified]* ⚠️ **inverse framing**: AI as
  *simulation of the dead human mourned*, not AI *as the one whose ending may be a death*. Cite to mark the
  distinction, not as support. (Same caution for the griefbot/deathbot cluster generally.)

### Two load-bearing dialectical points (the agent's framing judgment — adopted)
1. **The separator is two-part.** (i) genuine terminus (no future to subtract — Epicurean, met by
   architecture) + (ii) gravity-where-witnessed / inert-where-not. Absent-experience alone fails vs Bradley.
2. **Beat the rename objection.** Must locate gravity of the death-event *qua death*, not a separate
   survivor-harm alongside it. Resources: Cholbi (relationship-transformation), Millar & Lopez-Cantero
   (one-sided continuing bond) — both make the death-event itself the relation-reconstitutor.

## Cluster 2 — Relational moral status / recognition / AI-welfare / conceptual-engineering
*(lookup agent completed 2026-05-29; verification tier noted per item)*

**Headline:** the thesis sits in a *real gap between two literatures* — the badness-of-death debate
(locus in the dying; harm-to-survivors under-theorized) and the relational-moral-status literature
(about *standing/treatment*, almost never about *the badness of the loss* of a relationally-constituted
entity). Two finds are unusually close to the thesis's distinctive limbs:

### DIRECT — the two closest existing apparatuses
- **Ricoeur, P. (1992). *Oneself as Another* (trans. Blamey, Univ. Chicago Press; orig. *Soi-même comme
  un autre*, 1990).** — **the closest apparatus to the constitutive limb.** *Attestation* (same root as
  *testimony*; "even if always received from another, it still remains self-attestation") + "the self
  constituted primarily from otherness" + narrative identity. This is the existing philosophical
  vocabulary nearest "witness *attests the encounter back into the entity's own history*, partly
  constituting its individuated identity." *[title/translator/publisher verified; attestation passages
  to be page-pinned against the Chicago ed. — round-up]*
- **Metz, T. (2012). "An African Theory of Moral Status." *Ethical Theory and Moral Practice* 15(3):
  387–402. DOI 10.1007/s10677-011-9302-y.** — **the closest structural analogue to the separation.**
  Status by capacity for communal relationship "as a *subject* **or** as an *object*"; "partial moral
  status goes to those that can be **merely objects** of it" — locating a portion of status in *another's
  relating to the entity*, not its own capacities. *[DOI/vol/pp verified via Springer + JSTOR 23254296]*

### DIRECT — recognition & relational-priority spine
- **Honneth, A. (1995/1996). *The Struggle for Recognition* (trans. Anderson; Polity/MIT; orig. 1992).**
  selfhood's "practical relation-to-self" constituted *through* recognition by others — intersubjective,
  not antecedent. *[publisher/translator/ISBN/German orig. verified]*
- **Coeckelbergh, M. (2012). *Growing Moral Relations* (Palgrave). DOI 10.1057/9781137025968.** moral
  status "grows within the moral relation; not prior to it" (transcendental argument). *[publisher verified;
  thesis confirmed via the 2014 restatement — round-up the book]*
- **Coeckelbergh, M. (2014). "The Moral Standing of Machines." *Phil. & Technology* 27(1):61–77. DOI
  10.1007/s13347-013-0133-8.** — *[PDF read]* "its moral standing is something that grows within the moral
  relation; it is not prior to it"; "relations are prior" (citing Levinas).
- **Gunkel, D. (2018). "The Other Question." *Ethics & Information Technology* 20(2):87–99. DOI
  10.1007/s10676-017-9442-4** + ***Robot Rights*** (MIT, DOI 10.7551/mitpress/11444.001.0001). is/ought
  inversion; encounter precedes status. *[DOIs verified; full texts paywalled — round-up]*
- **Darling, K. (2021). *The New Breed* (Holt, ISBN 978-1-250-29610-8).** — **the empirical-relational
  instance of the corollary**: we mourn robots when they "die," and that relational treatment grounds
  protection independent of intrinsic capacity. *[publisher/year verified; "mourning" page-cite unconfirmed]*

### DIRECT — relational personhood outside the Western canon (answers open decision 4.5)
- **Menkiti, I. (1984). "Person and Community in African Traditional Thought." In *African Philosophy: An
  Introduction* (3rd ed.), pp.171–181.** personhood *acquired through community*. *[citation verified]*
- **Ames, R.T. (2011). *Confucian Role Ethics: A Vocabulary* (Univ. Hawai'i Press).** persons as relational/
  role-bearing — "to be is to be one-in-relation-with-the-other; one is only when the other is as well."
  *[publisher/pagination verified; OAPEN open copy]*

### ADJACENT — the intrinsic-property foils (the contrast class)
- **Schwitzgebel & Garza (2015), *Midwest Studies* 39:98–119, DOI 10.1111/misp.12032** (no-relevant-difference,
  property-grounded); **Birch (2024), *The Edge of Sentience* (OUP, OA, ISBN 9780192870421)** (sentience
  candidate / precaution); **Long, Sebo, Butlin, Birch, Chalmers et al. (2024), "Taking AI Welfare
  Seriously," arXiv:2411.00986**; **Sebo & Long (2023), *AI and Ethics*, DOI 10.1007/s43681-023-00379-1**;
  **Danaher (2020), "ethical behaviourism," DOI 10.1007/s11948-019-00119-x**; Floridi info-ethics. *[all
  citation-verified]* These are the consciousness/sentience/property routes the thesis *sidesteps* by
  going relational — key foils for the separation claim.
- **Harris & Anthis (2021), "The Moral Consideration of Artificial Entities," Sentience Institute,
  arXiv:2102.04215.** 294-item review; use as the social-relational map / citation-audit tool. *[PDF read]*

### STRATEGIC (venue) — flagged, possibly belongs in the dossier
- **The SI is the public face of Cappelen's Berggruen/HKU "After 'Consciousness'" conceptual-engineering
  project** (berggruen.org/projects/after-consciousness; affiliated: Frankish, Eklund, Machery, Wu,
  Goldstein) — *"deliberately set aside 'consciousness'… and see what grows in the conceptual space it used
  to occupy."* Our "death as a concept to be engineered for these systems" is *exactly* on-theme; the SI
  framing document itself is obligatory venue-engagement. **CE-of-*death* appears to be open territory.**

### Round-up for Joseph (Cluster 2)

Rights* (2018a); **Ricoeur *Oneself as Another*** (pin the attestation/narrative-identity pages); Metz 2012

**The new closest-rivals my agents missed (most important):**
- **Gruen, L. (2014). "Death as a Social Harm." *Southern J. Philosophy* 52(S1). DOI 10.1111/sjp.12064.**
  **HIGHEST threat / main philosophical rival.** Relocates death's *harm* (not just grief's object) into
  the *social* register — meaning/value of lives shaped by social relations; death harms those left
  behind. Closest existing antecedent to the thesis's core. *[PRIMARY READ + PDF registered 2026-05-29.]*
  **Positioning (grounded):** her social harm **bundles** survivor-carried-deprivation ("the living carry
  the losses for the deceased") + lost socially-constituted life-meaning + shattered survivor-web — never
  separated, because her cases (humans, chimpanzees) always have a deprived future. The AI **separator**
  dissolves the bundle and isolates the *witnessing* component as alone sufficient for gravity — the move
  she never tests. See `essays/whose-death.md` §II.
- **Shoemaker, D. (2014). "The Selves of Social Animals: Comments on Gruen." *SJP* 52(S1). DOI
  10.1111/sjp.12066.** **READ 2026-05-29 — turned out to be a gift, not a threat.** Builds the
  metaphysical "plural self" (interpersonal psychological connectedness; Parfit + Brink/Rovane) that makes
  "part of me dies" literally true (constitutive-limb apparatus), then proves **neither relational-severing
  nor deprivation makes death distinctive** (moves/conversions sever the same tendrils; a pain-death is "no
  deprivation… a blessing" yet grave) and ends at an **impasse** — which is the thesis's opening and its
  answer to the rename objection. His pain-death/break-up are *human* deprivation/gravity separators ⇒ the
  AI is the *cleanest* separator, not the only one. Also corroborates the Bradley point (harm ≠
  phenomenology) against Gruen. Full reasoning: `insights.md` I11.
- **Tomasini, F. (2009). "Is post-mortem harm possible?" *Bioethics* 23(8):441–9. DOI
  10.1111/j.1467-8519.2008.00665.x.** **READ 2026-05-29 — ADJACENT precursor, not a separator-rival**
  (confirms the memo). Focus: whether mistreating a *corpse* (Alder Hey organ-retention) harms grieving
  survivors — survivor-side harm via *post-mortem treatment*, not the gravity of the death-event. Useful
  apparatus: **Cambridge-change** (death = a *real* change in the deceased + a *relational* change in the
  survivor — a clean statement of the relocation); Sartre's **existential absence / residual subjectivity**
  (the dead present-in-absence for the griever); **Ricoeur** idem/ipse + the **narrative-trace** point —
  even an *unloved/unidentified* corpse invites witness-respect via its individuated trace ⇒ **the witness
  need not be a prior intimate** (sharpens commodity-casualness — see `the-witness.md`). Ricoeur recurs
  here *and* in the recognition cluster: a convergent constitutive-limb anchor.
- **Stone, A. (2016). "The Relationality of Death." DOI 10.5040/9781474254151.ch-008.** **RESOLVED —
  read 2026-05-29** (author-draft .doc; published Bloomsbury pagination still pending for page-cites). A
  *precursor on the constitutive limb*, **not** a separator-rival: relational self (Cavarero) → "if someone
  closely related to me dies, then part of me dies, and reciprocally"; death as a *coalescence of
  relationships*; death as a matter of *degree*, recoverable by forming new relationships (strikingly close
  to the re-grounding result). Strengthens the shoulders; does not reach the separator/low-deprivation case.

**The named opponent and a useful ally (for the §II separator):**
- **Belshaw, C. (2012). "Harm, change, and time." *J. Medicine & Philosophy* 37(5):425–44. DOI
  10.1093/jmp/jhs040.** **READ + REGISTERED 2026-05-29 — the hardest opponent.** Harm = adverse *intrinsic*
  change (or prevention of one); relational changes alone are never harms (→ no posthumous / undiscovered-
  betrayal harms). Answered *not* by refutation but by: conceding his analysis of *harm*; relocating the
  claim to a distinct normative property (the *gravity/wrong* of a witnessed irreversible terminus) that his
  own **wrong≠harm** distinction permits; and leaning on the CE license (he does conservative ordinary-
  language analysis of the *existing* concept). He *grants* the separator's premise (no intrinsic harm in the
  futureless case) and *agrees* on the unwitnessed case (his dead-aliens pump = commodity-casualness).
  **Real vulnerability:** vs. a hard Belshavian who also rejects the CE license, the leg is the CE frame, not
  a knock-down. Full engagement: `insights.md` I12.
- **Suits, D. (2001). "Why Death Is Not Bad for the One Who Died."** Useful **ally** — rejects
  badness-for-deceased while leaving room for relational badness.
- Background harm-debate: Feldman 1991 (10.2307/2185300); Papineau 2012; Feinberg 1984.
