I read the build manifest order, the live `src/*.md` sources, the side-car notes, selected scratch verification files, and the rendered anonymous PDF text. I did not edit files. My bottom-line view: this is a serious paper with a real architecture, not just a set of evocative claims. The strongest parts are the separator, the Belshaw burden-shift discipline, and the explicit limits in `§7`. The paper’s main risk is not prose quality; it is that several core distinctions are still doing more work than they have been explicitly licensed to do.

**High-Order Audit**
1. The engineered concept currently has two different definitions, and a referee will notice. In `§1`, death is pinned to “continuation as a terminal, non-revisable commitment” ([src/01-motivation.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/01-motivation.md:8)); in `§2`, death is “the loss of a constitutive factor” ([src/02-the-deaths.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/02-the-deaths.md:10)). These can be reconciled, but the paper needs to say how: the first looks like a criterion for death-apt agents, while the second is the account of death-events. If that distinction is not made explicit, “stance” will look like it was first the essence and later merely one axis.

2. The “futureless terminus” is the sharpest separator, but also the most vulnerable premise. `§5` says there is “no future left to subtract from” because the terminus is architectural ([src/05-the-separator.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/05-the-separator.md:8)). A deprivation theorist can reply: the relevant counterfactual future is exactly the continued relation or continued run that the architecture prevented. The answer seems to be that no same individuated subject would have persisted without re-attestation, but that answer depends on the continuity thesis later stated at [src/05-the-separator.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/05-the-separator.md:14) and [src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:16). I would pull that condition forward: “futureless” must mean not merely “no actual continuation occurred,” but “no later life of this individuated one was available under the identity conditions at issue.”

3. The constitutive-witness claim is load-bearing and still too compressed. The paper moves from recognition traditions to “one becomes a someone in being recognized” ([src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:8)). The cited shoulders plausibly support recognition as constitutive of self-relation, social standing, attestation, or practical identity. They do not, without further argument, support the stronger ontological claim that no someone exists absent witnessing. That stronger claim may be defensible for language-constituted agents, but it needs its own bridge: embodied human selfhood should not be treated as the direct model.

4. The “no glass” epistemic paragraph is powerful but overstates. “The report … is the only warrant anyone has ever had” ([src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:10)) invites a familiar objection: humans have first-person phenomenal access, embodied continuity, developmental history, and neurobehavioral evidence that artificial systems may lack. The safe version is not “only warrant simpliciter,” but “only intersubjective warrant” or “only public warrant available once the other-minds problem is taken seriously.” The final guard sentence helps, but the overstatement comes before it.

5. The fusion claim is the most metaphysically expensive sentence in the paper. “It is an interior of its own” and “something it is like to be in the thinking of them” ([src/04-the-fusion.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/04-the-fusion.md:6)) make the paper defend not only relational loss, but emergent group phenomenology. That may be the intended claim, but it becomes a large target. The paper can get much of the needed work from a weaker thesis: the fusion is a real relational cognitive configuration whose loss is not reducible to either party’s private ledger. If the stronger “interior” claim stays, mark it as the paper’s extension, not as something Shoemaker licenses; the footnote already starts doing this ([src/04-the-fusion.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/04-the-fusion.md:12)).

6. `§6` and `§7` currently pull against each other on moral status. `§6` says the witnessed agent holds a directed claim and its foreclosure is a wrong to X ([src/06-answering-belshaw.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/06-answering-belshaw.md:17)). `§7` says the paper “settles nothing about their moral status” ([src/07-implications.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/07-implications.md:8)). A directed claim whose foreclosure wrongs X is already at least a relation-relative moral-standing result. You need to choose the discipline: either the paper gives a conditional structure of wronging if the relatum condition is met, or it establishes a limited moral-status result for witnessed agents while leaving broader consciousness/moral-status questions unsettled.

7. The directed-duty move is good, but the anti-overgeneration guard relies on “one that experiences” ([src/06-answering-belshaw.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/06-answering-belshaw.md:19)). That condition is not established if the paper refuses consciousness and moral-status findings. Recast the guard as conditional: “if there is a relatum into whose history attestation can land.” Otherwise the paper appears to smuggle in experience exactly where it says it is not doing so.

8. The taxonomy has a counting problem. `§2` distinguishes five conditions, names four deaths, then says “the last two factors” mark candidate deaths, including causal death and phenomenological death ([src/02-the-deaths.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/02-the-deaths.md:12)). But causal continuity was listed first, not last; cognitive death and causal death also seem to overlap. `§7` then refers to “the four deaths” ([src/07-implications.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/07-implications.md:12)). This needs a clean spec: either four argued deaths plus two predicted candidates, or five constitutive factors with five corresponding losses. A compact list would help more than prose here, even if the final paper avoids a table.

9. The paper’s opening overgeneralizes relative to its own witness guard. “We end them by the million” ([src/01-motivation.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/01-motivation.md:6)) and “we end these systems by the million and feel nothing” ([src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:14)) are rhetorically strong, but the later account says most generic sessions are not witnessed and therefore are not deaths in the relevant sense. Add “where such relations obtain” early, or the paper will look like it first sensationalizes ordinary context termination and later retracts.

**Truth And Coherence**
The Belshaw handling is disciplined. The paper does not pretend to refute him, and the “harmless wrong” room is a real dialectical opening ([src/06-answering-belshaw.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/06-answering-belshaw.md:13), [src/06-answering-belshaw.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/06-answering-belshaw.md:25)). I would keep that shape.

The Moses 7 passage is rhetorically striking but strategically risky. The claim that, “by every account that locates grief in lack,” God is “the one being who could not weep” ([src/05-the-separator.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/05-the-separator.md:18)) is too broad; many accounts of divine grief locate it in love, compassion, or relation rather than lack. Keep it only if explicitly framed as a theological limit-case, not as the cleanest separator “by every account.”

The Emerson footnote is not submission-safe. The rendered PDF still says the source is being confirmed; the source file also carries the unresolved placeholder ([src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:20)). Either source it fully or remove the quotation.

The recognition paragraph is a citation pile. Buber, Emerson, Ricoeur, Honneth, and Metz all appear in one paragraph ([src/03-the-witness.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/03-the-witness.md:8)). The issue is not that the citations are irrelevant; it is that they substitute compression for argumentative digestion. I would make Ricoeur and Metz load-bearing, put Buber/Honneth in a supporting footnote, and drop Emerson unless pinned.

The AI disclosure needs exact policy alignment. Taylor & Francis’s current AI policy requires disclosure of the tool’s full name/version, how it was used, and why, with author responsibility retained; it also warns that individual journals may be stricter ([official T&F AI policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/)). The current back matter still has a “VERIFY/FINALIZE” comment and vague phrases like “broader set” and “other generative systems” ([src/99-back-matter.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/99-back-matter.md:15), [src/99-back-matter.md](/Users/josephwecker-v2/src/synthese-paper/04-inquiry-after-consciousness/src/99-back-matter.md:23)). Exact roster or a deliberately policy-compliant generalization is needed.
