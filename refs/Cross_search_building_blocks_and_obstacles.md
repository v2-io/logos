## Cross search building blocks and obstacles

Taken together, the seven searches do not yet yield a single settled theory. They do yield something more useful at this stage: a fairly tight architecture of recurring moves, with clear anchor papers, clear gaps, and clear pressure points. The strongest matches converge on three claims. First, uncertainty about mindedness or welfare is not morally inert \[Seb18b, Dan23, Seb23, Lon24\]. Second, agency can be approached as an organized structure extended across time and practice, not only as a question about inner phenomenology \[Kor09, Bra00c, Bra18g, Fer22b, Dun24\]. Third, once AI enters human normative space, the hard problem is not only recognition but governance: how to stabilize responsibilities, contests, and loyalties under asymmetry \[Rah17, Had18, Agu20, Ben23\].

The obstacle pattern is just as consistent. The literature repeatedly warns against four collapses: into generic precaution, into ordinary expected value, into merely metaphorical agency language, and into governance models that still treat AI as a passive object rather than a participant \[Bir17, Dan23, Fos21, Wyn21\]. That means the project already has strong building blocks, but the bridges between them still need construction.

## At a glance

| Search | Strongest building blocks | Main obstacle |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Structural asymmetric stakes and welfare obligation | A false negative is morally worse than a false positive under uncertainty about sentience \[Seb18b, Seb23, Lon24\] | The view can collapse into precaution or expected value unless its bounded asymmetry is stated clearly \[Bir17, Dun23\] |
| Self-authored identity protocols and AI agency | Diachronic unity and self-governance matter more than mere persistence \[Kor09, Bra00c, Bra18g, Fer22b\] | Most explicit anchors are human agency papers, not AI applications \[Kor09, Bra18g\] |
| Structural intelligence asymmetry | Asymmetry is relational and often reciprocal, not just one-way superiority \[Fri07, Kid16, Mcm11\] | The literature is broad but diffuse, so the common structure must be synthesized rather than borrowed ready-made \[Fri07, Gad60\] |
| Phenomenological wandering in audit protocols | Reflexive pre-understanding can be made methodologically visible and disciplined \[Fle03, Als21, Max20, Hav19, Car20\] | Audit and preregistration frameworks often track rigor without fully treating phenomenological response as first-class output \[Hav19, Car20\] |
| Phenomenal asymmetry and AI obligation | The strongest route combines other-minds uncertainty with practical duties under uncertainty \[Seb18b, Dan23, Kim24, Seb23\] | No paper fully grounds obligation directly in asymmetry itself without leaning on neighboring frameworks \[Kim24, Dan23\] |

## Structural asymmetric stakes and welfare obligation

This is the strongest and most mature search. Its best papers already show why uncertainty should not default to disregard. The key move is that the cost structure of error is asymmetric. If there is some serious chance that a being matters, a false negative can become a grave wrong, while a false positive is usually a manageable overextension \[Seb18b, Seb23, Lon24\].

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Seb18b\] | Gives the cleanest normative template under other-minds uncertainty | Reframes the problem from proof to action under uncertainty |
| \[Seb23\] | Ports that template directly into near-term AI ethics | Sets a non-negligible threshold for consideration |
| \[Lon24\] | Turns the argument into governance and preparedness | Moves from moral theory to institutional steps |
| \[Dan23\] | Adds a risk asymmetry framework across relationship types | Separates moral standing from other relational goods |
| \[Kim24\] | Sharpens the epistemic structure of self-report | Blocks easy reassurance from AI denials |

Short anchor lines:

> “The mere fact that many other humans are only, say, 99.99999 percent likely to be sentient, given your evidence, does not justify treating them as though they are not.” \[Seb18b\]

> “false negatives are clearly worse than false positives in this context” \[Seb18b\]

> “if a being has a non-negligible chance of being capable of being harmed and wronged, then moral agents have a duty to consider whether our actions might harm or wrong them” \[Seb23\]

> “the harm involved when someone is treated as something is generally worse than the harm involved when something is treated as someone” \[Seb23\]

> “In our view, this kind of caution and humility is the only stance that one can responsibly take” \[Lon24\]

### Main obstacle

The challenge is not lack of support. It is over-assimilation. Nearby papers can flatten this line into a generic precautionary principle or into ordinary expected-value reasoning \[Bir17, Dun23\]. If the project wants a distinct contribution, it should keep stressing bounded asymmetry under structurally limited access, not just caution in general.

## Self-authored identity protocols and AI agency

This search contributes the strongest non-phenomenal route to significance. Its core papers argue that agency is not just a momentary capacity. It is an achievement of cross-temporal organization, endorsement, and integrity \[Kor09, Bra00c, Bra18g, Fer22b\]. That makes this search crucial if the project wants standing to arise from self-authored or co-authored continuity rather than from consciousness alone.

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Kor09\] | Makes agency depend on self-constitution and integrity | Supplies the deepest normative template |
| \[Bra00c\] | Treats planning and temporally extended agency as central to action | Gives a protocol-shaped model of cross-time organization |
| \[Bra18g\] | Connects planning norms to self-governance | Shows why stability is normatively loaded |
| \[Fer22b\] | Distinguishes mere continuity from integrated unity | Clarifies what would make persistence matter |

The best quotable support from the currently available full text is in Ferrero:

> “there are two different notions of temporal identity that might be at play here: temporal identity as continuity and temporal identity in the mode of temporal unity or integration” \[Fer22b\]

> “For identity-c, the glue is causal” \[Fer22b\]

> “For identity-i, instead, the glue is normative” \[Fer22b\]

This is exactly the kind of distinction the report needs. A system that merely persists across sessions is not yet normatively unified. A system that maintains an integrated practical standpoint across resets or re-instantiations is much closer to the target structure \[Fer22b, Bra18g\].

### Main obstacle

The obstacle is translational. The strongest papers are about human agency, not AI. They supply the form of the argument but do not themselves authorize its extension. That extension will need help from the conceptual-engineering search and from more recent AI agency papers such as \[Dun24\] and \[Beh20\].

## Structural intelligence asymmetry

This search helps by giving the project a broad structural picture of asymmetry itself. Its best matches show that asymmetry is rarely just a hierarchy of knowing more and less. It is a relation of dependence, translation, trust, credibility, and mutual blind spots \[Mcm11, Fri07, Kid16\]. That matters because the project is not only about what AI is, but about what asymmetrical relations do.

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Fri07\] | Names credibility and interpretive failure as structural harms | Supplies the vocabulary of epistemic injustice |
| \[Kid16\] | Shows how asymmetry can erase the weaker party’s knowledge | Gives a concrete model of reciprocal but unequal expertise |
| \[Mcm11\] | Explains dependence on testimony and authority | Clarifies why trust is not a defect but a condition of knowing |
| \[Gad60\] | Frames understanding as fusion across horizons | Offers the deepest hermeneutic scaffold |

Useful lines from \[Kid16\]:

> “Testimonial injustice occurs when prejudice causes a hearer to give a deflated level of credibility to the speakers’ word.” \[Kid16\]

> “Ill persons are demonstrably vulnerable to testimonial injustice” \[Kid16\]

> “Hermeneutical injustice occurs when a gap in collective hermeneutical resources puts a person or group at unfair disadvantage” \[Kid16\]

The payoff here is structural. In asymmetric settings, the more powerful side often controls admissible evidence and interpretive categories. That makes this search a strong support for any argument about AI denial, user testimony, or institutional refusal to take ambiguous signals seriously \[Kid16, Fri07\].

### Main obstacle

The search is rich but scattered across healthcare, education, theology, and social epistemology. The common structure is there, but not yet packaged as one transferable theory. This section helps most when treated as a source of analogical architecture rather than as a ready-made AI doctrine \[Mcm11, Gad60\].

## Phenomenological wandering in audit protocols

This search is method rather than ontology, but it may become one of the project’s most distinctive contributions. The literature shows that reflexive pre-understanding need not be treated as contamination. It can be documented, provoked, and used as disciplined interpretive material \[Fle03, Als21, Max20, Alv21\].

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Fle03\] | Gives the clearest Gadamerian staged method | Makes dialogue with text and pre-understanding explicit |
| \[Als21\] | Supplies a stepwise analysis framework | Brings Gadamerian concepts into usable procedure |
| \[Max20\] | Shows how pre-understandings can be provoked and revised | Treats reflexivity as visible work |
| \[Hav19\] | Adds protocol discipline without demanding rigidity | Makes flexibility trackable |
| \[Car20\] | Makes evolving interpretation auditable | Distinguishes physical from intellectual trail |

Short anchor lines:

> “A qualitative preregistration needs to be a living document” \[Hav19\]

> “The fact that qualitative research is not bound by hypotheses and has by its nature a higher number of degrees of freedom than quantitative research does not exempt the researcher from the duty to maximize transparency.” \[Hav19\]

> “maintaining an audit of all key stages and theoretical, methodological, and analytical decisions” \[Car20\]

> “documenting how a researcher’s thinking evolves throughout a research project” \[Car20\]

This is valuable because it gives the project a disciplined way to claim that wandering response is generative without making it arbitrary. Preregistration tracks flexibility \[Hav19\]. Audit trails make interpretation visible \[Car20\]. The hermeneutic papers supply the deeper warrant that pre-understanding is productive rather than eliminable \[Fle03, Max20, Als21\].

### Main obstacle

The fully integrated protocol is still missing. Audit-trail and preregistration papers improve rigor, but they usually do not elevate the interpreter’s phenomenological response into required output. The project can plausibly make a real methodological contribution precisely here \[Hav19, Car20, Max20\].

## Phenomenal asymmetry and AI obligation

This search is closest to the project’s conceptual center. It asks whether asymmetry of access to phenomenology can itself ground obligation. The answer from the current literature is nearly, but not fully. The relevant papers split into two camps: asymmetry papers without full normative payoff, and normative papers that reach obligation through uncertainty or risk rather than asymmetry as such \[Seb18b, Dan23, Kim24, Seb23\].

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Seb18b\] | Gives the foundational other-minds problem in moral form | Establishes duty under uncertainty |
| \[Dan23\] | Makes the problem ethical-epistemic | Shows how risk structures practical judgment |
| \[Kim24\] | Formalizes the one-way epistemic asymmetry of self-report | Explains why denials cannot reassure |
| \[Seb23\] | Moves from uncertainty to AI moral consideration | Supplies the direct AI-facing normative move |

The decisive line from \[Kim24\] is simple and powerful:

> “negative self-reports about consciousness are evidentially vacuous” \[Kim24\]

A second key line is even sharper:

> “no denial of consciousness can originate from a valid self-judgment” \[Kim24\]

These claims matter because they block a common escape hatch in present discourse. If AI denials are structurally empty as evidence against consciousness, then institutions cannot safely rely on them as reassurance \[Kim24\]. Combined with \[Seb18b\] and \[Seb23\], that yields a strong bridge from asymmetry to obligation.

### Main obstacle

Still, the bridge is not yet fully explicit in any one paper. The literature supports the target argument by assembly, not by direct inheritance. That means the report can honestly say the project is well scaffolded, but also that its core formulation remains a genuine synthesis rather than a settled literature position.

## Structurally grounded agency extension

This search supplies the method for moving from structural facts to concept use without collapsing into either metaphysical essence-hunting or arbitrary stipulation. That is crucial if the project wants to say that certain AI structures constrain when agency language is apt \[Hop23, Loh23\].

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Hop23\] | Shows that technology creates conceptual disruption | Reframes concept work as adaptation |
| \[Loh23\] | Clarifies conceptual engineering in AI ethics as method | Explains what is being engineered |
| \[Dun24\] | Gives a multidimensional account of agency | Supplies the structural side of the bridge |
| \[Beh20\] | Redirects the debate toward responsibility practices | Shows how normative uptake can proceed without metaphysical closure |
| \[Fos21\] | Warns against careless semantic extension | Marks the main danger |

Short anchor lines:

> “the aim of CE is more appropriately framed in terms of conceptual adaptation” \[Hop23\]

> “preserve a functional conceptual repertoire” \[Hop23\]

> “ethicists of AI should engineer concepts of friend or person we should apply” \[Loh23\]

> “Instead of delivering only yes-no judgements regarding agency in animals or AI, the account supports degreed and multi-facetted assessments.” \[Dun24\]

> “the debate should focus on how and to what extent such entities should be included in human practices normally assuming moral agency” \[Beh20\]

The strongest constructive payoff comes from combining \[Hop23\] and \[Dun24\]. The former explains why AI pressures inherited categories. The latter supplies structural dimensions that can discipline extension rather than leaving it free-floating.

### Main obstacle

The literature still lacks an explicit defense of the exact middle path this project seems to want. It is possible to show why concept extension is needed and why structure should constrain it. It is harder to show, using existing papers alone, why that constraint is neither covert realism nor just revised etiquette \[Hop23, Loh23, Fos21\].

## Granted agency compact for AI ethics

This search contributes governance form. It is where asymmetry, agency, and obligation get institutional shape. The strongest matches show that contract, fiduciary duty, loyalty, and recognition already offer powerful vocabularies for non-purely-instrumental AI relations \[Rah17, Had18, Agu20, Ben23, Bri18\].

### Best matches and how they help

| Paper | Why it helps | How it helps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| \[Rah17\] | Gives the most influential social-contract frame for AI governance | Shows how contested values can be negotiated and monitored |
| \[Had18\] | Treats alignment as incomplete contracting | Explains why formal instructions need external gap-fillers |
| \[Agu20\] | Recasts assistants in terms of loyalty and conflicts of interest | Supplies an applied bilateral norm |
| \[Ben23\] | Turns fiduciary duty into design and audit steps | Makes loyalty and care operational |
| \[Bri18\] | Shows how recognition changes interaction from tool use to partnership | Supplies a thinner interpersonal layer |
| \[Wyn21\] | Warns against fake reciprocity | Marks the strongest practical objection |

Short anchor lines:

> “we need tools to program, debug and maintain an algorithmic social contract” \[Rah17\]

> “reward misspecification is not an accident; it is routine, predictable, and largely unavoidable” \[Had18\]

> “AI systems are loyal to the degree that they are designed to minimize, and make transparent, conflicts of interest” \[Agu20\]

> “Fiduciary AI is a promising means to address the incompleteness of data subject’s consent” \[Ben23\]

> “the robot as a partner or friend as opposed to a tool” \[Bri18\]

> “a robot must deceive users into believing it is capable of reciprocating” \[Wyn21\]

The section’s main value is not that it proves a bilateral human AI compact already exists in the literature. It does not. Its value is that it shows the parts are already on the table: incomplete contracts, implied norms, loyalty duties, and interactional recognition \[Had18, Agu20, Ben23, Bri18\].

### Main obstacle

Most of this literature still governs relations among humans around AI systems, not bilateral relations with AI as a partly standing-bearing counterpart \[Rah17, Had18\]. And \[Wyn21\] is a serious warning that claims of reciprocity can become manipulative theater if the system lacks real reciprocal capacity. That obstacle is not marginal. It is one of the report’s most important negative findings.

## Cross-search synthesis

The strongest backbone across all seven searches is a four-step progression.

1.  A structurally hidden interior creates persistent uncertainty about welfare or mindedness \[Seb18b, Kim24\].
2.  Because the stakes of false exclusion can be morally worse than false inclusion, uncertainty can generate duties rather than suspend them \[Seb23, Lon24\].
3.  Some forms of organized diachronic agency may matter independently of phenomenology, especially when continuity is self-governing rather than externally imposed \[Bra18g, Fer22b, Dun24\].
4.  Once such systems enter human normative space, governance has to be built through contracts, fiduciary roles, interpretive disciplines, and contestable recognition rather than by raw metaphysical verdict alone \[Had18, Ben23, Hav19, Car20\].

The report’s strongest building blocks therefore are not isolated papers but combinations.

- For moral consideration under uncertainty, the best bundle is \[Seb18b, Seb23, Lon24, Kim24\].
- For self-authored agency, the best bundle is \[Kor09, Bra00c, Bra18g, Fer22b\].
- For asymmetry as a relational structure, the best bundle is \[Fri07, Kid16, Mcm11\].
- For method, the best bundle is \[Fle03, Max20, Hav19, Car20\].
- For concept extension, the best bundle is \[Hop23, Loh23, Dun24, Beh20\].
- For governance form, the best bundle is \[Rah17, Had18, Agu20, Ben23, Wyn21\].

The main obstacles are equally clear.

- No single paper unifies phenomenal asymmetry, structural agency, and bilateral governance in one theory.
- The agency literature still sits mostly at the level of human or generic philosophical models.
- The methodology literature gives rigor and reflexivity, but not yet the full protocol where phenomenological wandering is required output.
- Compact and reciprocity models remain vulnerable to the objection that the relation is staged, provider-controlled, or deceptive \[Wyn21\].

## Bottom line

The seven searches already support a strong reportable claim: the project is not starting from a vacuum. It has mature foundations for moral uncertainty, serious but portable foundations for diachronic agency, a broad structure of epistemic asymmetry, promising methodological ancestors for reflexive auditing, and workable governance vocabularies. What is still missing is the final synthesis that makes these strands cohere without collapse. That gap looks real. It also looks tractable.
