# Phenomenological wandering in audit protocols

**Research Goal:** Find academic literature relevant to whether there is a prior tradition that articulates a graded or otherwise explicit protocol in which the auditor / reviewer / interpreter’s own phenomenological response — including wandering thoughts, reactions, or sense of being an auditor of the work — is treated as required generative output rather than as noise, bias, or something to be bracketed. Prioritize work that could anchor a methodology-of-philosophy or Synthese-style framing, while also including strong ancestors from qualitative methodology, ethnographic reflexivity, hermeneutic philosophy, social epistemology, science and technology studies, philosophy of expertise, and related methodology-of-philosophy traditions when they genuinely treat reflexive or phenomenological response as structurally generative for interpretation or evaluation. Organize results in a balanced way across: (1) closest predecessors that most explicitly require auditor reflexivity or phenomenological wandering as methodologically productive output, especially where there is a protocol, staged procedure, tiered judgment scheme, or structural defense; (2) anticipatory work that bears on the interpreter’s generative pre-understanding, multi-axis testimonial reliability, or protocolized qualitative review without fully integrating these; (3) methodological templates in philosophy where the form of a qualitative or graded review procedure is itself a philosophical contribution; and (4) challenging or opposing traditions that treat the reviewer’s phenomenology as something to bracket rather than use generatively. Give special attention to the possible integration of three elements: hermeneutic recognition that pre-understanding is generative for interpretation, social-epistemology recognition that testimonial reliability decomposes into multiple axes, and preregistration-style explicit protocol discipline. Downweight primary canonical texts already commonly cited unless later work reconstructs them into a methodological protocol or directly bears on whether this integration has already been achieved. Exclude pure psychology-of-judgment literature without methodology-of-philosophy significance, empirical social-science methodology that does not engage philosophy of method, AI auto-review engineering, and phenomenology that does not connect to methodology.

## Summary of Results

The closest predecessors do not come from philosophy journals but from Gadamerian and hermeneutic qualitative methodology, where the interpreter’s pre-understandings and reflexive responses are treated as explicit analytic material through staged procedures—most clearly the five-step Gadamerian method \[1\], forestructure-elicitation protocols \[2\], reflexive “provocation” of pre-understanding \[3\], and stepwise analysis frameworks \[4\], \[5\].

#### Closest methodological ancestors

- Hermeneutic phenomenology makes pre-understanding productive rather than eliminable: naive reading and comprehensive understanding \[5\], dialogue with participants and text \[1\], silence and slow “wandering” in the hermeneutic spiral \[6\].
- Reflexive material is sometimes explicitly archived as output: reflexive journals \[7\], decision trails \[8\], \[9\], “echoes” in IPA \[10\], and lived-with-data accounts \[11\].
- Alvesson and Sandberg systematize pre-understanding as an interpretation-enhancer and horizon-expander rather than a contaminant \[12\].

#### Adjacent but not fully integrated

- Audit-trail traditions make researcher thinking visible, but mainly for trustworthiness and confirmability rather than as first-class generative findings \[13\], \[14\], \[15\], \[16\], \[17\], \[18\].
- Preregistration for qualitative work supplies protocol discipline and tracked flexibility \[19\], \[20\], \[21\], but does not integrate phenomenological wandering as required evidential output.
- Interpretive-field principles offer evaluative scaffolds for reflexive research \[22\].

#### Philosophy-facing templates and limits

- Reflective equilibrium work shows that philosophy sometimes treats procedure itself as a contribution \[23\], \[24\], \[25\], \[26\], \[27\], \[28\], but these protocols usually formalize adjustment of commitments, not the auditor’s phenomenology.
- The missing synthesis is a single protocol joining hermeneutic generativity of pre-understanding, multi-axis reliability assessment, and preregistered procedural discipline.

#### Opposing line

- Descriptive phenomenology and bracketing traditions still cast the interpreter’s response as something to discipline or suspend \[29\], \[30\], \[31\].

**Hermeneutic research in nursing: developing a Gadamerian-based research method.** ([link](https://doi.org/10.1046/J.1440-1800.2003.00163.X))\
V. Fleming, U. Gaidys, and Y. Robb\
*Nursing inquiry* · Jun 1, 2003 · 610 citations

> This paper takes the stance that although there are many different approaches to phenomenological and hermeneutic research, some of these have become blurred due to multiple interpretations of translated materials. Working from original texts by the German philosophers, this paper reconsiders the relevance of phenomenology and hermeneutics to nursing research. We trace the development of Gadamer’s philosophy in order to propose a research method based in this tradition. Five steps have been identified as a guide for nurse researchers. These are deciding upon a question, identification of preunderstandings, gaining understanding through dialogue with participants, gaining understanding through dialogue with text and establishing trustworthiness.

2\. · 100% match · 2021 · 22 cit/yr\
**Doing a Hermeneutic Phenomenology Research Underpinned by Gadamer’s Philosophy: A Framework to Facilitate Data Analysis** ([link](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069211047820))\
Rasha Alsaigh and I. Coyne\
*International Journal of Qualitative Methods* · Jan 1, 2021 · 117 citations

> Phenomenology is an umbrella term that refers to both a philosophical movement and a variety of research approaches. Hermeneutic phenomenology is a methodology, which is not rigidly set or prescribed. Hence, students and researchers using a hermeneutic phenomenology approach underpinned by Gadamer’s philosophy may struggle to apply his concepts while analysing data. This article describes a new framework that provides guidance on how to analyse data in a research study while remaining faithful to the major tenets of Gadamer’s work (pre-understandings, hermeneutic circle and fusion of horizons) and closely adhering to the central tenets. The framework provides a step-by-step strategy to ensure rigour while maintaining trustworthiness. This may serve as a useful guide for neophyte students and researchers using or considering Gadamer’s version of hermeneutic phenomenology in their studies.

3\. · 100% match · 2020 · 4.1 cit/yr\
**Examining Researchers’ Pre-Understandings as a Part of the Reflexive Journey in Hermeneutic Research** ([link](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406920985718))\
C. Maxwell, Beate Ramsayer, C. Hanlon, J. Mckendrick, and V. Fleming\
*International Journal of Qualitative Methods* · Dec 29, 2020 · 22 citations

> This article considers one of the philosophical sources of reflexivity, the concept of “pre-understandings” as envisaged by the German philosopher, Hans Georg Gadamer. There are a number of empirical research studies employing a Gadamerian approach, and while some authors may describe methods of examining pre-understandings and applying findings reflexively to hermeneutic enquiry, there remains a general lack of sufficient detail given over to the “how” in relation to this process.
