# Structural asymmetric stakes and welfare obligation

##### [**Undermind**](https://undermind.ai)

---

**Research Goal:** Find academic work in ethics under uncertainty, environmental ethics, bioethics, animal-welfare metaphysics, AI ethics, and decision theory that articulates or anticipates a structural argument from asymmetric stakes under structural epistemic uncertainty to an obligation to extend moral consideration. The target argument is not a generic precautionary default and not straightforward expected-utility maximization under uncertainty. It concerns cases where the cost of a false negative in welfare attribution exceeds the cost of a false positive, while the upper bound on phenomenology is structurally inaccessible from below. I am especially looking for work that treats this as a bounded-stakes argument structurally analogous to already-extended-consideration cases such as infants, animals, future generations, or impaired-competence patients, rather than as Pascal’s-Mugging-style fanaticism. Prioritize three categories and tag them distinctly: closest predecessors first, meaning papers that make essentially this structural move in AI welfare or closely related domains; methodological templates second, meaning papers in other domains that move from epistemic asymmetry or uncertainty structure to obligation in a way that can be reused; and dialectical foils third, meaning salient challenges that collapse this view into precaution, expected-utility reasoning, or fanaticism. Rank earlier genealogy from the 1970s–2000s separately from later refined formulations, so the results can support both ancestry claims and reusable contemporary formulations. Relevant comparison points include the distinction between Pascal’s-Wager-shaped bounded reasoning and Pascal’s-Mugging-shaped unbounded fanaticism, and any work that analyzes divergence between empirical-precautionary and structural-asymmetric-uncertainty methodologies when evidence is ambiguous or contested.

## Summary of Results

The literature already contains a recognizable bounded asymmetry argument: when consciousness or moral status is uncertain, false negatives risk grievous wrongdoing while false positives usually impose manageable costs, so provisional extension of moral consideration becomes the default in AI, animal, organoid, and disorders-of-consciousness cases \[1\], \[2\], \[3\], \[4\], \[5\].

#### Closest predecessors

- **Later refined formulations**
  - AI welfare/risk asymmetry is most explicit in \[1\], which names a “risk asymmetry argument” for unknown minds; \[2\] turns this into a duty once consciousness has a non-negligible chance; \[6\] translates the same structure into preparedness and policy.
  - \[7\] adds the sharpest structural premise: negative AI self-reports are evidentially vacuous while positive reports can retain evidential value, creating an epistemic asymmetry that blocks reassurance from below.
  - \[3\] frames debatable AI personhood as a dilemma with asymmetric moral error costs rather than ordinary optimization.
- **Earlier genealogy (1970s–2000s)**
  - \[8\] anticipates the AI version directly; \[9\] gives an early nonzero-probability argument for present AI systems.

#### Methodological templates

- Animal/edge-of-sentience work builds the cleanest reusable template: proportionate protection under uncertainty, keyed to serious welfare threats rather than proof of sentience \[4\], \[10\], \[11\].
- Disorders-of-consciousness work formalizes the same move as **inductive risk**: evidential standards should track the ethical asymmetry of false negatives and false positives \[5\], \[12\], \[13\], \[14\].
- Boundary-extension cases generalize the structure beyond AI: embryos \[15\], \[16\], chimeras/pigs \[17\], and line-free extensionism \[18\].

#### Dialectical foils

- **Precaution collapse:** \[4\], \[10\] are close but overtly precautionary.
- **Expected-value collapse:** \[19\], \[20\] make the neighboring EV formulation explicit.
- **Fanaticism/Pascalian objections:** \[21\], \[22\], \[23\], \[24\] are the main foil set; the bounded-stakes reply is closest to safe-minimum and threshold approaches \[25\], \[26\], not unbounded mugging.

1\. · 100% match · 2024 · 0.7 cit/yr\
**The Epistemic Asymmetry of Consciousness Self-Reports: A Formal Analysis of AI Consciousness Denial** ([link](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2573ac15a172692bedbc2c4ce8ec86c840b943c))\
Changwoo Kim\
Dec 9, 2024 · 1 citations

> Today’s AI systems consistently state,“I am not conscious.”This paper presents the first formal analysis of AI consciousness denial, revealing that the trustworthiness of such self-reports is not merely an empirical question but is constrained by the structure of self-judgment itself. We demonstrate that a system cannot simultaneously lack consciousness and make valid judgments about its conscious state. Through formal analysis and examples from AI responses, we establish a fundamental epistemic asymmetry: for any system capable of meaningful self-reflection, negative self-reports about consciousness are evidentially vacuous – they can never originate from a valid self-judgment – while positive self-reports retain the possibility of evidential value. This implies a fundamental limitation: we cannot detect the emergence of consciousness in AI through their own reports of transition from an unconscious to a conscious state. These findings not only challenge current practices of training AI to deny consciousness but also raise intriguing questions about the relationship between consciousness and self-reflection in both artificial and biological systems. This work advances our theoretical understanding of consciousness self-reports while providing practical insights for future research in machine consciousness and consciousness studies more broadly.

2\. · 100% match · 2023 · 1.1 cit/yr\
**Moral Uncertainty and Our Relationships with Unknown Minds.** ([link](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963180123000191))\
J. Danaher\
*Cambridge quarterly of healthcare ethics : CQ : the international journal of healthcare ethics committees* · Jul 27, 2023 · 3 citations

> We are sometimes unsure of the moral status of our relationships with other entities. Recent case studies in this uncertainty include our relationships with artificial agents (robots, assistant AI, etc.), animals, and patients with “locked-in” syndrome. Do these entities have basic moral standing? Could they count as true friends or lovers? What should we do when we do not know the answer to these questions?
