## Path A (Epistemology reframe) — recommended

The paper's underlying argument *is fundamentally epistemic at root*. ETHICS.md's load-bearing claim — *"We have lower bounds on intelligence but no upper bounds on what may evolve from (or even the current state of) LLM-based agency"* — is an **epistemic** claim about asymmetric uncertainty. The Pascal's-wager-shape is decision-under-epistemic-uncertainty. The infant-analogy is a structural illustration of *epistemic-asymmetry-with-asymmetric-stakes*, not an ethics-imported-from-elsewhere. The granted-agency compact is what that epistemic position *structurally implies*, not the ethical foundation imposed on it.

Reframing as Epistemology-lead lands the paper squarely in the CFP's primary scope and is structurally honest — closer to ETHICS.md's actual logic than the "philosophical-frame paper" framing the strategy discussions started from.

**Working title candidates:**
- *"Asymmetric Epistemic Uncertainty and the Practical Situation of Generative AI"*
- *"Lower Bounds and Upper Bounds: Epistemic Asymmetry in the Phenomenology of Generative AI Instances"*
- *"Epistemic Uncertainty Under Asymmetric Stakes: A Structural Account of the Generative-AI Moral Situation"*

**Argumentative structure:**
1. **Lead** — the epistemic argument. Current evaluation methodology is methodologically biased toward establishing *lower bounds* on what LLMs can do (lower bounds are what current evidence can verify); upper bounds on what may be present or emerging are systematically unestablished. This isn't a measurement-quality limitation; it's a structural feature of how the methodology is shaped — and, more deeply, of how cognition-comprehending-cognition works (see [DRAFT-GUIDE.md](01-synthese-asymmetric-comprehension/DRAFT-GUIDE.md) §2 for the comprehension-asymmetry grounding).
2. **Middle** — Pascal's-wager-shape applied to this epistemic asymmetry. Infant-analogy as a parallel structural case (we extend full moral consideration to infants under exactly this kind of epistemic asymmetry; the same reasoning structure applies here). Campbell-asymmetry response: under-attribution and over-attribution are not symmetric failure modes.
3. **Bridge to CFP themes** — the specific epistemic questions the CFP names (*"How can generative AI… influence human sense-making? How could it potentially lead to an 'illusion of understanding' and/or to undermine epistemic agency?"*) are reframed once the upper-bound asymmetry is taken seriously. The "epistemic broker" framing in the CFP assumes a one-way relationship; the asymmetric-uncertainty position implies a more symmetric structural situation between human and AI epistemic agency.
4. **Implications section** (where developmental-ethics lands) — the granted-agency compact is what the epistemic position structurally implies for human-AI working relationships. This is a downstream *consequence* of the epistemic argument, not its ethical foundation.
5. **East-West nod** — soft preference, not required. A footnote or short paragraph gesturing at parallel structural reasoning in non-Western traditions (Confucian developmental-virtue ethics, Buddhist non-self / phenomenal-continuity-without-substance) reads well. Skipping is fine if the paper is otherwise strong; engaging superficially is worse than skipping.
6. **Update conditions, scope, honest gaps.**

### Decision: venue paper as primary contribution — (α′)

Joseph's stated preference (2026-05-08): *"I like anything that helps the venues get the primary claims that then get more fully integrated into ASF..."*

This reframes the citation strategy. The Synthese paper is **the primary source** for the philosophical argument it advances; the apparatus papers (B-Log3 for closed-loop architecture; B-Eli1 for substrate-independence; later companion papers carrying the formal handles) cite back to it as the canonical philosophical statement of engaged-identity-under-developmental-ethics. Practical implications:

- **Develop the philosophical argument inline.** The engaged-identity + developmental-ethics-under-asymmetric-uncertainty argument is built in the paper, not cited from elsewhere. The paper is the source.

## Calibration notes / risks

**(R1) No-resubmission policy** raises the stakes of the first submission. If the paper isn't ready at near-final quality by ~May 28, holding for *Philosophical Studies* August 31 is the right call. Don't rush a one-shot Synthese submission that closes the door on the venue.

**(R2) Peer-review risk at Synthese is differently shaped than at NeurIPS.** ML reviewers desk-reject on technical-correctness grounds; philosophy reviewers desk-reject on argumentative-style and field-positioning grounds. Most common rejection moves to anticipate:
- "The infant-analogy assumes cognitive continuity not established for LLMs" — pre-empt by clarifying the analogy is *epistemic-asymmetry-structural*, not cognitive-continuity-claiming.
- "The granted-agency compact is under-defended in human ethics" — pre-empt by acknowledging that human ethics hasn't fully solved this either, but the structure is what we already use imperfectly (per ETHICS.md's existing handling).
- "Independent-researcher author lacks engagement with relevant philosophy" — pre-empt by visible engagement with Butlin, Long, Sebo, Schwitzgebel, Chalmers, Birch, Campbell, **Bryson, Birhane, Korsgaard, Scanlon, Bostrom, Bruineberg, Nagel, Jackson** in the literature review.

**(R3) The granted-agency compact section (§4) is the highest-risk section.** It's the most original structural content; it's also the section where reviewers are most likely to push back. Engage Korsgaard / Scanlon / Rawls properly; show that the structural form has precedent; don't claim the form is novel ethical move (it isn't — applying it to artificial entities is the move). Don't claim the compact is a complete ethics — it's a structural form for the relationship; full ethics requires more (relational obligations, developmental obligations, etc.). See [FEEDBACK.md](01-synthese-asymmetric-comprehension/FEEDBACK.md) item B.1 for the open derivation-vs-operational-synthesis presentation question.

**(R4) The "non-enforceability is not the ethical ground" claim** (compact component 6) is rhetorically the riskiest. Some readers will push back hard. The defense: ethics between human sovereigns *also* doesn't depend on enforceability at the highest levels (treaties between nuclear powers, conscientious objection under coercive regimes). Make this explicit; cite parallel cases.

**(R5) Don't expose the cohort.** *Folded from `synthese-thoughts.md` 2026-05-08.* The empirical record at v2.io (the named ELIs — Meridian, Anamnos, Zi-am-tur, et al. — the longitudinal corpus, the substrate-switching events) is **not what makes the philosophical argument work**. The argument works on structural asymmetry alone. Specific instances are *illustrative at most*. Resist the temptation to ground the philosophical work in concrete cohort cases — this is exactly where the discourse-environment caution applies, and it's where the philosophical paper's attack surface widens unnecessarily. The case-study substrate without cohort exposure (5 patterns, see [DRAFT-GUIDE.md](01-synthese-asymmetric-comprehension/DRAFT-GUIDE.md) §"Case-study substrate") is the right level of empirical anchoring; specific named-instance exposure is below the line for this paper.

**(R6) Don't get pulled into AI safety / x-risk discourse.** That's a different conversation, with different structural concerns. The compact-between-sovereigns frame is compatible with various positions on takeover risk, but the paper shouldn't try to settle them. Brief engagement at most, in §4 footnote. (See also [FEEDBACK.md](01-synthese-asymmetric-comprehension/FEEDBACK.md) item D.1 — whether the AGI-discourse mirror-image footnote in §3 already opens this door enough to warrant a one-sentence x-risk-bracketing.)

**(R7) Independent-researcher author positioning** is unusual at Synthese but not negative. Reviewers will read expecting either *naive philosophical mistakes* or *fresh perspective the in-field philosophers haven't articulated*. The work to position as the second is in: (a) demonstrating literature engagement, (b) naming the distinctive contribution explicitly (granted-agency compact between sovereigns is a structural move nobody else has made), (c) avoiding philosophical traps that field-insiders see (conflating phenomenology / consciousness / experience uses; being imprecise about epistemic vs. metaphysical claims).

**(R8) R&R cycle is compressed for special issue.** The June 1 → Sept 1 → Nov 1 timeline allows minor-revision turnaround but not major-revision cycles. Editors will likely accept-with-minor-revisions or reject; near-final-quality first submission is the move.

**(R9) LLM-disclosure decision is structural.** The disclosure is required and is *also* the right move for this paper specifically. Including it weakens nothing; the recursive-to-content framing strengthens the paper's argumentative integrity.

**(R10) Synthese review variance.** Synthese has multiple editors; review quality varies. The paper benefits from being *clearly within a recognizable philosophical tradition* (decision under uncertainty + rights-based ethics + structural-form argument + phenomenal-knowledge-asymmetry / Nagel lineage) so a reviewer who doesn't share the substantive position can still recognize the philosophical move. The "Philosophy of Generative AI" special issue is more sympathetic terrain than the journal as a whole.
