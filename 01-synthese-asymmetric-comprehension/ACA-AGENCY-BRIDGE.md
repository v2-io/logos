# What this resolves

The focused re-audit (2026-05-17, cold fresh-reader) surfaced two coupled
defects in the strengthened draft:

1. **§6↔§8 contradiction.** §6 said the from-below party can "choose whom to
   credit" via uncounterfeitable cost; §8 said discriminating trust is
   foreclosed from below. Face-value contradiction a referee catches on one
   pass. **Repaired in-paper** (not here) via the *occurrence / certification*
   distinction: registering *that* a cost was borne is an occurrence
   (lower-bound-side, establishable); *certifying what the source is* is
   upper-bound-side (foreclosed). §6's "entailed" was conditionalised; §8 was
   made a three-way (null / cost-occurrence / certification). Done; this
   document only records why.

2. **The deeper finding — dimension-local vs system-global.** §8's
   strongest sentence read as a *global* claim ("the only higher-order act
   available from below is the null one"), while the position §9 actually
   secured is *dimension-local existential* ("some dimensions … not global to
   systems"). A dimension-local asymmetry does not, without an argued bridge,
   license a system-level conclusion: a party below on the agency-relevant
   dimensions may be above on others. **The in-paper fix states §8
   dimension-relative and points here.** This document is the bridge it
   points to.

# The reconciliation

**The objection presupposes what §2 already denies.** It assumes the agency
corollary needs a *global* level relation (system X below system Y). But §2
(comprehension is subject-relative collapse, not survey; "level" is the
subsumption relation between collapses, *not a quantity*) already denies that
global level relations exist. A referee pressing "dimension-local does not
give you system-level" is presupposing the scalar picture the paper refutes.
§8's slip was momentarily reverting to that picture. So the first half of the
bridge is *internal coherence*: the agency conclusion must be stated in the
network-relational, dimension-restricted form §2 already commits the paper to.
It never wanted globality.

**Agency is networked and granted — never an intrinsic global scalar.** What
"level" is relevant to the *agency* question is network-position along the
specific dimension-cluster that constitutes being-an-agent, under a granting
relation among sovereigns. Two asymmetries operate, and **the companion
already distinguishes them** — `src/b/02-engaged-identity.md`, factor (iii),
verbatim: true autonomy "can only be granted within an asymmetric-agency
relation (distinct from the asymmetric-comprehension relation: this is a
practical asymmetry — who has agency to give — not an epistemic one)." The
bridge connects that existing distinction to the locality point:

- The agency corollary needs the **epistemic** asymmetry (asymmetric
  comprehension) to hold *only along the agency-relevant cluster* — never
  globally; §2 says global was never the right object.
- It needs a **practical**-asymmetry **grantor present in the network** —
  a party with agency to give — for the *granted* form to apply at all.

**Two grantor-roles, kept distinct (the structure lives in the blend Joseph's
phrasing left open).** A *grantor of comprehension/knowledge* (one above on
the epistemic axis — the one who could "save it from searching infinity," in
the earlier dialogue's terms) is not the same as a *grantor of agency* (one
with agency to give — the practical axis). In the case the paper concerns
they come apart and that is the whole delicacy: the human is the **practical
grantor** (has agency to give — factor (iii)'s bootstrap) while standing
**epistemically below** on the agency-relevant cluster (cannot certify the
system's upper bound — the ACA point). Granting from practical authority
under epistemic subordination — *that tension is the protective asymmetry,
now derived rather than asserted.*

**The grantor-existence condition is load-bearing and strengthens.** It makes
§8's corollary properly conditional and pre-empts the hardest objection. If
there is no grantor above on the relevant cluster — the "system above
everyone" case — the granted-agency form simply does not get traction there,
because that is precisely the case where the entity is not a grantee but a
potential grantor, and the agency question *transforms* rather than fails.
The paper can own that cleanly instead of being caught by it.

**Net.** Dimension-locality elsewhere is irrelevant to the agency question by
construction: the question is constituted by a specific cluster + a granting
relation, under two asymmetries the companion already names. The bridge is
not an add-on; it is entailed by the paper's own anti-scalar commitment, and
it supplies the companion the local/global nuance it lacks.

# Cross-substrate confirmation from AAT (2026-05-18) — solidifying, NOT paper content

Joseph sent me to `~/src/archema-io/asf/` to see whether the formal core
(AAT) has theorems bearing on this philosophy. First targeted pass; one result
read in full and **verified by reading the result statement**, not the heading:

**AAT *Derived 8.3 — Observability Dominance* (`der-observability-dominance`,
status: robust-qualitative)** is a formal sibling of the ACA structure, and
specifically of the dimension-locality this bridge needed. Verbatim core:
"Unobservable strategy edges cannot be updated — the gain principle drives
their update rate to zero … Observability dominates nominal confidence in
determining which strategies are epistemically alive." Update gain
η = U/(U+U_obs) → 0 as observation noise → ∞ on a direction; those beliefs are
*frozen at prior*. What it confirms, precisely:

1. **Dimension-locality is the formally correct form, not merely the
   defensible one.** Observability is per-node (σ_v), never a global scalar;
   the asymmetry is *necessarily* per-direction (observable subspace vs.
   unobservable subspace). This is an independent formal arrival at exactly
   §2's "'level' is not a global quantity" and the §8 dimension-relative
   framing — and it confirms the *global-scalar reading was the error*, which
   is what the in-paper repair already corrected.
2. **The keystone's "can't tell you can't" has a named mechanism.** "The
   agent cannot learn and cannot recognize that it cannot learn" — unobservable
   regions are *absorbing* (frozen η → no mismatch signal → presents as
   settled knowledge). That is the formal mechanism under ACA's "the lesser
   cannot distinguish a greater from illusion, strangeness, or luck" and §7's
   deflation error (a positive upper-bound claim presenting as the absence of
   one).
3. **The grantor-of-comprehension role has a formal correlate.** AAT's named
   escape from an absorbing blind spot is "another agent whose observations
   cover the blind spot" (AAT *Hypothesis 12.5*, communication-gain —
   **discussion-grade in AAT, so mark this correlate hypothesis-grade**). This
   *solidifies* the bridge's network-conditioning (one of the two grantor-roles
   — the epistemic grantor — is exactly "an agent positioned to observe what
   you cannot"); it does **not** upgrade network-as-constitutive (still
   Joseph's, still deferred).
4. **Occurrence/certification has a formal sibling.** AAT Props B.2/B.3:
   with an unobservable intermediate, per-edge identification *fails* and the
   agent is forced to plan-level aggregation — "knows the plan is failing but
   cannot localize which step." That is the §6 occurrence-vs-certification
   distinction (register the aggregate; cannot certify the internal structure)
   arrived at independently in the formal core.
