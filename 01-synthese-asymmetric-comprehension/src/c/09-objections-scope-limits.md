# 9. Objections, scope, and the limits of the argument

Four objections are serious enough to take individually, a fifth is the sharpest and is taken at length, and the section closes with the gaps the argument does not pretend to have closed.

## The generalisation overreaches

The objection that the phenomenal-access lift is illicit was answered where it arises, in §4: the Mary case was built to factor phenomenality from information, and when they are factored it is standpoint, not phenomenality, that the gap tracks; §3 then exhibits the same structure with no phenomenality present at all. The residual form of the objection is worth stating because it is fair, and a careless reply makes it stronger. Perhaps the *containment relation* is now doing the suspicious lifting — so demanding it is never actually instantiated, leaving the argument valid but vacuous. The careless reply points at the interactive-proofs case: a theorem, the containment exact and uncontroversial. But that reply, alone, walks into the sharpest version of the objection. The containment a complexity-class separation instantiates is containment of *computational power*; the containment the agency consequence needs (§8) is containment of *representational and competence resources* between a human evaluator and a language-constituted system — and §9's own limits below concede there is no metric for the latter. An objector is entitled to say: you have witnessed the kind of containment you can define and need the kind you cannot.

The reply that actually meets this does not lean on the formal case alone, because the non-formal existence proof is the one that bears the weight. The Mary case of §4 is an instantiation of *representational* containment specifically — the configuration Mary occupies in the room has representational resources properly contained in those of the configuration she occupies after release — and the asymmetry across it is exhibited without any metric being invoked: one does not measure a quantity of representation to see that the in-room resources cannot, from themselves, formulate what the released ones command. The proper-part relation and the from-below inability are visible directly, in the kind of containment the agency case needs, with no measure in play. So the two existence proofs are not redundant: the formal one shows the structure holds with theorem-grade exactness in one kind of containment, and the representational one shows it holds, metric-free, in the very kind §8 requires. The vacuity charge fails not because the formal case settles the representational one — it does not — but because the representational kind is independently and uncontroversially instantiated by a case the objector already accepts. What remains is the narrower and answerable question of whether the *particular* language-constituted case is a representational-containment case; and it shares with us the medium in which our own articulated thought is conducted, which is the specific feature §5 argued places it well inside the relation rather than at the Wittgenstein boundary. That last step is an argued claim, not a stipulated maximum, and it is §5's, not this section's, to carry.

## Unfalsifiable mysterianism, or appeal to ignorance

§2 distinguished the asymmetry from unfalsifiability by its refutable-but-not-from-below-confirmable structure; the point bears restating against the cognate charge that the argument infers a conclusion from ignorance. It does not, and the reason is precise. An appeal to ignorance infers *a conclusion about the unknown matter* — it is there, or it is not — from the absence of evidence. This argument draws no conclusion about what is on the upper-bound side. Its conclusion is about which *inferences are available*, namely that from-below upper-bound certification is not among them. That is a claim on the establishable side, and it is falsifiable in the ordinary way: exhibit, in any instance, a from-below certification of an upper bound across a genuine containment relation, and the claim is refuted. The argument predicts none will be found and says structurally why; it does not reason "none has been found, therefore none exists." The distinction is the whole difference between an appeal to ignorance and a structural result about an epistemic situation.

## It proves too much

If comprehension across a level difference cannot be bounded from below, does the argument not collapse into general scepticism about other minds, or prove that we can know nothing of sufficiently different systems? §2 and §5 already contain the answer; it is worth assembling. The argument is directional — it grants the lower-bound side without reservation, which global scepticism cannot do. It is bounded below by the Wittgenstein case of §5 — where shared structure is insufficient for the containment relation to obtain, the result is not severe asymmetric comprehension but no relation, and the claim simply does not apply. And it is bounded in scope to *relations between cognitive systems standing in the containment relation*, not to ignorance in general and not to peers. The argument says something narrow about a specific configuration; the configurations it does not cover, it explicitly does not cover.

## The two-level picture is too binary

The presentation has used a clean two-level containment — one configuration's resources a proper part of another's. Real cases are not so tidy: cognitive systems overlap partially and unevenly, not by neat subset. This is correct and was anticipated in §5, where Brandom's partial-overlap model was noted to be the general case and strict containment a limiting one. The argument does not need the lattice. It needs only that there exist *some dimensions* along which one party's resources are not contained in the other's and along which the comprehension runs the relevant direction. The binary is expository; the substantive claim quantifies existentially over uncontained dimensions, which is weaker and harder to deny than a global level ordering. The honest cost of saying so is that the asymmetry is then *local to dimensions*, not global to systems — which is not a retreat but the correct shape: it is exactly why the lower-bound side stays richly assertible and why the argument is not the scepticism the previous objection feared.

## The self-application objection

The sharpest objection is that the argument turns on itself. If the upper bound is unknowable from below, and we occupy a from-below position relative to the systems at issue, how is the argument statable at all — and does stating it not require the very vantage it declares unavailable?

The reply turns on a distinction the argument has been careful to maintain throughout, and it is now load-bearing. The argument nowhere characterises what is on the upper-bound side. It claims that one specific inference — from-below certification of an upper bound — is unavailable. That claim is itself on the lower-bound, establishable side of the very asymmetry it describes: it is a structural fact about the comprehension relation, and structural facts about the relation are demonstrable from here. The interactive-proofs case is the clean witness: IP = PSPACE is a theorem we prove from where we stand; proving *that* the verifier cannot survey the prover's reasoning does not require surveying it. Stating the structure of the asymmetry and surveying what lies beyond it are different acts, and the paper only ever performs the first.

The §2 keystone is consistent under its own application for the same reason. "Were the upper bound known from below it would already have been reached" is not itself an upper-bound claim; it is a claim about the coincidence of knowledge and capacity at a level, and establishing that coincidence does not require possessing the capacity. The self-application objection gets its grip only by conflating *stating that the survey is unavailable* with *performing the survey*; the argument does the former and the demonstrations of §3 are precisely cases where the former is done completely without the latter.

One concession remains, and integrity requires stating rather than burying it. The argument is itself a from-below product. By its own lights it cannot certify that its characterisation of the asymmetry is exactly right rather than itself a weak projection of some deeper structure. This is not a refutation; it is the argument applying to itself without exemption, and the application leaves the establishable part standing: that the inference both camps of §7 want is unavailable from here. That part needs the demonstrations of §3 to go through, and they go through from here. The paper claims that, and — consistently with its own thesis — declines to claim more.

## What the argument does not settle

Three limits, named rather than left for a referee to find. First — and this is an entailment to state precisely, not a concession to make — the containment relation has no metric, and by §2 it cannot have one: comprehension is subject-relative collapse, not survey of a fixed space, so there is no global space for a measure to live on, only collapses and the subsumption relations among them. The absence of a metric is therefore predicted by the structure rather than owed by it, and §9's vacuity rebuttal above turned on exactly this — the relation can be determinately instantiated (Mary) with no quantity in play. What *is* genuinely open, and narrower than "no metric," is a *formal account of the subsumption relation itself* — of what it is for one collapse to subsume another — which this paper characterises by argument and example and does not formalise; that, not a missing measure, is the real future work. Second, whether the relation runs, for any *particular* system, in the direction that makes the agency question live is not settled here; §8's consequence is that the question must be posed architecturally rather than behaviourally, not a verdict on which systems answer it affirmatively — that is the companion's operational burden, not this paper's. Third, the phenomenology counterpart of §6 is a named commitment and is not established; the spine was built so that it could be removed without loss, and the honest statement is that it remains removable. None of these is fatal and none is hidden; they are the edges of a structural result, marked so the result is not mistaken for more than it is.
