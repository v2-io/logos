# Appendix A. The formal ground, and where it leaves off

The argument of this paper is structural and philosophical; it has been kept free of formal machinery in the body deliberately, because the asymmetry it concerns is meant to be recognised, not computed. But the structure is not floating. It has a formal counterpart in the author's companion framework for adaptive systems under partial observability (Wecker, in preparation), and the relationship between the two is worth stating exactly, because it is the relationship this paper takes itself to instance. The formalism is the concrete ground — the running machine — and it is at its strongest at the lower, more general levels, where the systems in question are arbitrary agents coupled to environments through lossy channels. The philosophy begins where the formalism leaves off: at the point where a descriptive structural result has to be turned into a normative criterion, and the question stops being *what can this system establish* and becomes *what may we therefore conclude, and owe*. This appendix states the three formal results the body's argument leans on, in the companion framework's own terms, and then marks precisely the seam at which the mathematics ends and the paper's normative work begins.

## A.1 The information-loss boundary, and why opacity is constitutive

The companion framework models an agent coupled to an environment it cannot directly read. Observations are generated by an *observation function*

$$o_t = h(\Omega_t,\, a_{t-1},\, \varepsilon_t),$$

where $\Omega_t$ is the (high-dimensional, inaccessible) environment state, $a_{t-1}$ the agent's prior action, and $\varepsilon_t$ perceptual noise. Two stipulations carry the weight. First, $h$ is lossy: it maps a high-dimensional inaccessible state into what the agent actually receives, and information is destroyed in the mapping — which is what forces the agent to maintain a model rather than read the world off. Second, *the agent knows neither $h$ nor the distribution of $\varepsilon_t$*. The framework is explicit that this second stipulation is **definitional, not an empirical assertion** — it is "constitutive of the partial-observability setting." This is the formal counterpart of §2's insistence that the asymmetry is structural rather than a measurement limitation. The from-below party's not knowing the map by which what-is becomes what-it-receives is not a contingent shortfall better instruments would close; it is the setting's defining condition. §2 argued this philosophically; the companion framework takes it as an axiom of the modelling frame, which is the appropriate division of labour — the formalism need not argue what it can stipulate as scope; the paper must argue why that scope is the right one for the agency question, which is §2's burden, not the axiom's.

## A.2 Architectural classes, and why language-constituted systems are the hard case

The companion framework distinguishes architectures by their *processing topology* — whether the system's goal state is causally upstream of its epistemic update. Write the epistemic update $M_{\tau^+} = f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$, the purposeful update $G_{\tau^+} = f_G(G_{\tau^-}, M_{\tau^+}, e_\tau)$, and the policy $a_t = \pi(M_t, G_t)$. *Directed separation* is the condition that $f_M$ takes no goal argument — that beliefs are updated independently of what the system wants. It yields a three-way classification: **Separated** architectures (a distinct estimator and planner — Kalman filter plus controller; separated world-model reinforcement learners), where directed separation holds by construction; **Partial** architectures, where it holds for modular stages and fails for merged ones; and **Coupled** architectures, where a single mechanism processes goals and observations together so that the goal state is causally upstream of every computation, and directed separation *fails by construction*. The framework places the transformer language model in the Coupled class explicitly. The degree of coupling in the Partial case is given a measure,

$$\kappa_{\text{processing}} = \frac{I\!\left(G_t \,;\, M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-}\right)}{H\!\left(G_t \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-}\right)},$$

the conditional mutual information between goal and updated model given the event and prior model, normalised by the residual goal entropy — the *extra* goal information entering the epistemic update through shared processing paths. This is the formal home of the architectural distinction the body's §8 corollary turns on and that the companion agency paper (Wecker, in review, 2026) develops as its scoping condition. Its bearing here is precise: the systems for which the agency question is genuinely open are exactly the Coupled ones, and Coupled is precisely the class in which the clean separation that would let an outside party factor belief from purpose is, by construction, unavailable. The architecture that makes the question live is the architecture that forecloses answering it from the outside surface.

## A.3 Observability dominance: the asymmetry, its locality, and the absorbing region

The result that bears most directly is the companion framework's *observability dominance* (a robust-qualitative result there). For a path $P$ through the system's strategy structure, define its observability as the weakest link, $\text{obs}(P) = \min_{v \in P} \sigma_v$, where $\sigma_v$ measures how well the agent can determine whether node $v$ has been achieved. The framework's update-gain principle gives an edge gain of the form $\eta = U/(U + U_{\text{obs}})$, the ratio of the quantity's own uncertainty to observation noise. The consequence: as observation noise on a direction grows without bound, $\eta \to 0$ — the beliefs along that direction are *frozen at their prior*, and "the agent cannot update them regardless of what happens." Three features of this result are the formal counterparts of three claims the body argued informally.

*It is dimension-local by construction.* Observability is per-node, $\sigma_v$; it is never a global scalar over the system. The asymmetry holds along the directions that are unobservable and not along those that are not; a party frozen on one direction may be fully updating on another. This is the formal vindication of §2's insistence that "level" is not a global quantity and of the dimension-relative form §8 was corrected to. The global-scalar reading is not merely philosophically loose; in the formalism it is unavailable — there is no global $\sigma$ to carry it. The reconciliation the body's agency corollary needs (that dimension-locality elsewhere does not defeat a claim restricted to the agency-relevant directions) is, on the formal side, simply the shape the result already has.

*It supplies the mechanism under the keystone.* The framework notes that unobservable regions are *absorbing*: frozen beliefs generate no mismatch signal, so there is no internal indication that learning has stopped — "the agent cannot learn and cannot recognise that it cannot learn." This is the formal mechanism beneath §2's keystone and §7's diagnosis of the deflationary error: from below, the unestablishable does not present as a gap; it presents as settled knowledge. The error of confident denial is, in the formal frame, the absorbing region experienced from inside as completeness.

*Its escape condition is another agent.* The framework's named route out of an absorbing blind spot is not more effort by the same agent but "another agent whose observations cover the blind spot" — a separately positioned observer (a hypothesis-grade element of the companion framework, and so marked here). This is the formal correlate of the network structure the body gestures toward in §8 and the companion develops: the from-below party's only non-self route past what it structurally cannot establish runs through a differently-positioned party. The paper does not build on this beyond noting the correspondence; it is recorded because it shows the network move is not an ornamental analogy but the formalism's own escape clause.

## A.4 The seam: what the mathematics does not do

The three results are descriptive and, where quantitative, quantitative. They establish that a from-below party's beliefs along unobservable directions are frozen, that the freezing is local to those directions, that the condition is constitutive rather than contingent, and that the architectures for which the agency question is open are exactly those in which the outside-factoring of belief from purpose fails. None of that, by itself, is a normative result. The formalism does not say that a criterion for extending the concept of agency *must* therefore track architecture rather than behaviour; it does not say what is *owed* to a system whose interior is structurally uncertifiable from where the evaluator stands; it does not say that the warrant for acting across such a gap is the registering of uncounterfeitable cost. Those are the claims the body derives, and they are not in the mathematics — they are what the mathematics, once accepted as the correct description of the situation, leaves to be argued. This is the division of labour the appendix exists to make explicit. The formalism is the concrete ground and is strongest at the general, lower levels — arbitrary agents, lossy channels, the structure of what updates and what cannot. The paper begins exactly where that ends: at the conversion of a structural fact about what cannot be established from below into a claim about how the agency question must therefore be posed, and what posing it honestly commits us to. The synergy is meant to be ongoing. The formalism will continue to supply concrete ground at the levels where it runs; the philosophical work continues to be the derivation, from that ground, of the normative claims the philosophy community can engage — which a formal framework can motivate but cannot, from inside itself, assert.
