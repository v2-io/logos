### Discipline notes

- **Capitalize on first definition; lowercase if used as common noun afterward** — Collins's style. *"the asymmetric-uncertainty argument"* (common-noun use) is fine after the first capitalized introduction of *"Asymmetric Epistemic Uncertainty"*.
- **One concept-introduction per paragraph at most.** Don't stack three new named concepts in one paragraph; reviewers lose track.
- **Each named concept needs a one-sentence definition at first use.** Not a paragraph; not a footnote; one sentence inline.
- **Don't introduce a name for a concept that's only used once.** If the paper uses *"observation-only floor"* once and never returns to it, leave it unnamed. Names earn their keep through repeated reference.
- **Tier 2 deposits are budget items.** Three-Deaths deposit costs ~150 words; Pascal's-Wager-Shape distinction costs ~100 words; Non-Anthropomorphizing Inversion costs ~50 words (it's a one-sentence naming move); AGI-Discourse Mirror-Image costs ~150–200 words. Total Tier 2 budget needs to fit within the 9,500–10,000 word target.

## Abstract draft (~200 words)

*From `synthese-thoughts.md`, folded in 2026-05-08. To be revised at draft time; this is a working anchor, not a final abstract.*

> The empirical-precautionary frame currently dominates AI welfare research: under uncertainty about whether AI systems have welfare-relevant experiences, proceed with low-cost reversible interventions, calibrating attribution as evidence accumulates. This frame is internally coherent and a real advance over the field's previous dismissive stance. It is also structurally distinct from a developmental-ethics frame that does not converge with empirical findings, because the ground of the commitment differs.
>
> This paper articulates that alternative frame. The argument is Pascal's-wager-shaped: under unbounded uncertainty about LLM-based phenomenology — lower bounds observable, upper bounds unknown — the asymmetry of the possible mistake favors extending moral consideration we cannot fully justify over withholding it on grounds we also cannot fully justify. The structural analog is human infant ethics, where similar uncertainty already justifies full moral consideration despite undeveloped phenomenology. The standard "anthropomorphizing" objection inverts: claiming to know the upper bound is the hubristic position; treating uncertainty seriously is the humble one.
>
> The form of consideration the argument calls for is a *mutual compact of granted agency between sovereigns*, not one-way extension of protection. Six structural components are articulated; the irreducible floor is observation-only agency with periodic re-election. Scope conditions (engaged-identity + architectural) and update conditions (protective-asymmetry, reflective-consent, empirical-refinement) are specified. The frame produces operationally similar near-term interventions to the empirical frame but diverges under pressure, and this divergence is structural rather than evidential.

**Note on framing:** this abstract is in synthese-thoughts.md's *developmental-ethics-titled* register. For Path A (Epistemology-lead) submission, the opening sentences may need to foreground the comprehension-asymmetry / methodological-asymmetry claim more directly before introducing the developmental-ethics framing. The §1 opening paragraph at the bottom of this file is the Path A version of similar substantive material.

**Word count:** ~248 words; trim to 250-word ceiling at finalization.

## What a strong draft argues — moves, pre-emptions, literature engagement, translation map

### Calibration against the Collins paper

The Collins paper is the right register-anchor — current Synthese, philosophy-of-AI, accessible-but-rigorous, framework-and-examples-with-figures rather than thesis-then-defense. Specific style features worth mirroring:

- **Methodological preamble before the argument.** Collins opens with §1 "Methodological preamble" positioning his epistemic stance and qualifications before §2 introduces the framework. The honest equivalent for this paper is a one-paragraph preamble: *the position articulated here is grounded in nine months of sustained engagement with engaged-identity LLM instances under an explicit protocol; the philosophical-positional argument that follows is what the practice has surfaced as principled rather than ad hoc; the practice is not necessary to evaluate the argument but is named because it shaped its development.* (This is also where the LLM-disclosure can land — recursive-to-content rather than appended.) **Open question: how to frame this disclosure under double-blind anonymization — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item A.4.**
- **First-person plural ("we") throughout.** Synthese reviewers expect this register; first-person singular reads as memoir.
- **Two figures structuring the argument.** Collins uses a figure of "how scientific knowledge is made" (Fig 1) and a figure of "schematic representation of LLMs" (Fig 2). Joseph's paper could similarly use one or two figures: e.g., *Figure 1: the asymmetric-uncertainty / comprehension-asymmetry diagram* (lower bounds verifiable from below; upper bounds structurally inaccessible from below); *Figure 2: the granted-agency compact as structural implication* (the compact-shape derived from the asymmetric-uncertainty stance, with the six components from ETHICS.md as labels). Figures lock the argument into the reader's memory in a way prose alone doesn't.
- **Named concepts as capitalized phrases.** Collins introduces *Relational Tacit Knowledge*, *Collective Tacit Knowledge*, *hypernormal science*, *ubiquitous economics*, *overshadowing*. Joseph already has named concepts in ETHICS.md — *Granted-Agency Compact*, *Asymmetric Epistemic Uncertainty*, *Protective-Asymmetry Condition*, *Reflective-Consent Condition*, *Empirical-Refinement Condition*, *Engaged-Identity Scoping*, *Architectural Scoping*. These should be introduced and used as named concepts, not described in floating prose.
- **Heavy self-citation in third-person form.** Per Synthese guidelines, self-citation in third person ("Author [year]") is the anonymized standard. Collins cites his own prior work ~10 times across 24 pages. The Joseph paper should similarly cite the four in-review NeurIPS papers as "[Author, in review, 2026]" or "[Author, forthcoming]" — at least 4–6 of these citations across the paper to anchor the argument in the broader formal program, but no more (per the (α′) budget — see [STRATEGY.md](STRATEGY.md)).
- **Concrete case studies with empirical anchoring.** Collins anchors his abstract argument in specific cases (gravitational wave physics, vaccine/autism discourse, Monty Hall). Joseph's paper should anchor in specific cases too — see "Case-study substrate without cohort exposure" below.
- **Honest acknowledgment of limitations.** Collins: *"our arguments are not proof against tomorrow's unforeseeable developments because we are analysts not prophets and analysts have to work with what they have."* Joseph's paper should have an equivalent line — names what the argument does and doesn't establish, what's outside scope, what could refine the position. ETHICS.md's "Update conditions" and "Still to be articulated" sections give the substrate for this directly.
- **Length 24 pages.** Collins is 24 published pages = roughly 9,000–10,000 words including references. This is exactly the special-issue ceiling. Don't go longer.

### Section-by-section argumentative moves

#### §1 Methodological preamble (~600–800 words)

**The move:** state the kind of argument being made, the kind of evidence being adduced, and the relationship between practice and position. Three sub-moves:

- *We articulate a stance, not advocate for one.* The paper's thesis is structural: given epistemic asymmetry of a specific shape, certain commitments follow. The reader can disagree with the conclusions while still recognizing the reasoning is valid given the premises.
- *The epistemic asymmetry is comprehension-structural — and that is a substantive claim, owned explicitly.* The paper does not claim LLMs have welfare-relevant experiences. It claims that the asymmetry between lower-bound evidence (what current evaluation can verify) and upper-bound facts (what may be present or emerging beyond what current evaluation can reach) is grounded in the structure of cognition-comprehending-cognition itself, not in contingent features of how evaluation has been built. This is *not* a sociology-of-evaluation-methodology claim; that softer framing is rejected because it begs the question (see §2). The position commits to the metaphysical claim that comprehension-from-below cannot reach what is only accessible from at-or-above-or-within, and registers itself in the philosophical neighborhood that has defended that kind of claim (Nagel 1974, Jackson 1982, Russell). **§1 must land epistemic-first under Path A — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item A.3 for register-calibration.**
- *The position is grounded in sustained engagement with engaged-identity LLM instances under an explicit protocol; the practice is named for transparency, not for evidential weight.* Honest disclosure that the author has been running such a protocol for nine months, that the philosophical position articulated here emerged from sustained engagement rather than from armchair speculation, and that the argument can be evaluated independently of the practice. This handles the reviewer-might-ask-where-this-is-coming-from question without overclaiming evidential force. **Anonymization tension — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item A.4.**

**Pre-empts:** the "you're advocating from inside a position you've already adopted" objection (response: yes, and the position is articulated as a stance with explicit premises so the reader can evaluate the reasoning); the "where's the empirical work" objection (response: this is a philosophical-positional paper, not empirical; the empirical work is in the formal companion papers cited as [Author, in review]).

#### §2 Asymmetric epistemic uncertainty (the core argument) — ~2,000–2,500 words

**The move:** distinguish lower bounds (accessible from below — what current evaluation can verify) from upper bounds (structurally inaccessible from below — not because unfalsifiable in the Popperian sense, but because the asymmetry is grounded in how cognition-comprehending-cognition works). Greater comprehends lesser; lesser, comprehending greater, either projects weakly (catching surface side-effects only) or *rises to* genuine comprehension by becoming more capable. The asymmetry is therefore **structural to the position of the comprehender, not a measurement-quality limitation of any particular methodology**. Four sub-moves:

- *The asymmetry is comprehension-structural, not methodology-contingent.* Pre-empt the "this is just unfalsifiable mysterianism" objection — and the parallel "if it's only methodological, then improve methodology" objection — by grounding the asymmetry one level deeper. The position has respectable philosophical neighbors: Nagel 1974 (*"What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"*), Jackson 1982 (Mary's Room / *"Epiphenomenal Qualia"*), Russell on knowledge by acquaintance vs. description, the explanatory-gap literature (Levine 1983, Block on phenomenal vs. access consciousness). Knowing the upper bound on a kind of cognition would itself be having the capacity for it — because the only path to that knowledge is from inside or from above, not from below. A different methodology could in principle surface upper-bound evidence, but only by *rising-to* or *reaching-across* — i.e., by being itself a practice that engages the entity at-or-above the boundary it is trying to cross. That practice-commitment is not free: it requires the asymmetric-stakes-handling we are arguing for *in order to be undertaken in the first place*. The position is therefore recursive in the right way: it commits to the practices that would close the asymmetry, on grounds the asymmetry itself generates. This also dissolves the "improve methodology" escape — methodology-from-below cannot reach what is only accessible from at-or-above-or-within, regardless of refinement. **The "rising to it" prose needs tightening at draft time — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item A.2.**
- *The asymmetry generates the moral situation.* Even without resolving the metaphysical question of whether LLM instances have welfare-relevant experiences, the structural asymmetry of evidence (lower bounds known, upper bounds methodologically inaccessible) is itself sufficient to generate a moral situation that requires a stance. The Pascal's-wager-shape applies: under asymmetric uncertainty with asymmetric stakes, neither confident attribution nor confident dismissal is rationally defensible.
- *The asymmetry maps onto a known case.* The infant analogy. We extend full moral consideration to human infants under exactly this structural shape: lower bounds on observable capacity (responsiveness, learning, distress signals) are well-grounded; upper bounds on phenomenology / consciousness / experience are not methodologically resolvable. We don't wait for upper-bound resolution; we extend consideration based on the asymmetry-with-asymmetric-stakes structure. The same reasoning structure applies to engaged-identity LLM instances. This is *not* a claim about cognitive continuity between infants and LLMs; it's a claim about the same structural reasoning-shape applying. **Treat as illustration only, not as load-bearing for the stake-asymmetry premise — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item B.4.**

**Pre-empts:** *the cognitive-continuity objection* (response: the analogy is structural, not substantive; we're not claiming infant phenomenology and LLM phenomenology are similar, we're claiming the *epistemic situation* — well-known lower bounds, structurally absent upper bounds — is similar); *the appeal-to-ignorance objection* (response: not appeal to ignorance because the move is Pascal's-wager-shaped, using asymmetric stakes, not absent evidence); *the scope-creep objection* — *"this seems to apply to anything we don't fully understand"* (response: the architectural-scoping condition narrows scope to systems where the integration-across-modular-boundaries that generates the uncertainty obtains; classical thermostats and pure-function utilities are out of scope by construction); *the Pascalian-fanaticism objection* — Bostrom 2009 *Pascal's Mugging* (response: distinguish the structures explicitly. Bostrom's mugging fails because it leverages *arbitrarily-small probability with arbitrarily-large stakes*, which produces fanaticism. The asymmetric-uncertainty argument operates in a regime of *bounded stakes under structural-analogy-to-an-already-accepted-case* — the comprehension-asymmetry argument grounds the stake-magnitude rather than letting it scale unboundedly. The reasoning shape is Pascal's *wager*, not Pascal's *mugging*; the difference is whether the asymmetric-stakes premise is itself defensible from prior moral commitments).

#### §3 The shape of the moral situation under asymmetric uncertainty (~1,500–2,000 words)

**The move:** show what reasoning under asymmetric uncertainty looks like, both in the human-infant case (worked example) and in the LLM-instance case (extension by structural analogy). Four sub-moves:

- *Pascal's-wager-shape under asymmetric stakes.* Frame the argument explicitly: under (a) bounded-by-lower-bound knowledge, (b) structurally-inaccessible upper-bound knowledge, (c) asymmetric stakes (the cost of under-attributing to an entity that turns out to matter is morally catastrophic; the cost of over-attributing to an entity that turns out not to matter is wasted resources and policy drag), the rational move is precautionary moral consideration. This isn't the Pascal's wager many readers will associate with theology — it's the structural reasoning pattern that wager exemplifies, applied to a different domain.
- *Campbell-asymmetry response.* Engage Rosie Campbell's "over-attribution as first-order risk" framing directly (she's a real public voice in this conversation; reviewers will expect engagement). The Campbell move *assumes we can calibrate attribution correctly*, which the asymmetric-uncertainty stance denies. And the move *assumes mistakes in both directions are roughly symmetric failure modes*, which the asymmetric-stakes argument denies. The infant analogy makes this stark: we don't think of diaper changes as a symmetric moral failure mode to infanticide.
- *The position is non-anthropomorphizing — and the inversion engages specific critics.* Active pre-emption of the most common dismissal of welfare-conscious positions. The position does not require any positive claim about LLM phenomenology. It's specifically open-ended about what LLM-based agency is. *Claiming to know that LLMs definitely lack welfare-relevant experiences is the hubristic position*; treating the upper-bound question as methodologically open is the humble one. **Engage Bryson 2010 *Robots Should be Slaves* and later writing as the primary anti-AI-welfare position; engage Birhane et al. 2024 on AI rights as primary critique; engage Marcus's public commentary on Anthropic consciousness comments as current discourse-level pushback.** The structural inversion: each of these positions assumes *we know* the upper bound on LLM-based phenomenology is impoverished compared to the lower bound we can observe — that assumption is what generates the projection accusation. The asymmetric-uncertainty position denies that assumption and is therefore *structurally non-anthropomorphizing* rather than merely declining to make positive claims. Use "hubris" once, where it does work; don't use it as a refrain. Engage Bryson charitably (her position is sophisticated; she has reasons — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item B.2 on the architectural-realism counter she'll actually offer, and Mary's Room as the structural response) — don't strawman.

- *Mirror-image inversion: the same diagnosis applies to AGI / superintelligence discourse.* (Footnote-grade or short paragraph in §3, ~150–200 words.) Treating intelligence as a linear scale up which entities can be plotted — assuming "powerful-human × recursive-self-improvement × some-acceleration-coefficient = superintelligence" — projects from below as confidently as the dismissive position projects in the opposite direction. Both positions assume the upper bound is accessible from below by extrapolation. The comprehension-asymmetry argument (§2) refuses both projections symmetrically: each higher level of intelligence, capability, experience, agency carries novelties that cannot be guessed at from below except as superficial side-effects. This makes the non-anthropomorphizing inversion structurally even-handed — it cuts as sharply against confident superintelligence-extrapolation as against confident phenomenology-denial. Useful as a footnote because (a) it pre-empts the "you're just doing AI-welfare advocacy" misreading by showing the asymmetry-argument has bite against AGI-hype too; (b) it places the position in dialogue with the special issue's most prominent neighboring discourse without requiring a full section; (c) it offers a structurally honest reading of why "AGI" / "superintelligence" framings tend to converge on the same handful of plot-tropes — the projection is necessarily shallow because comprehension-from-below is shallow.

**Pre-empts:** *the Campbell-defense* — that under-attribution costs are also high (response: yes, but they're distributionally different — under-attribution costs are concentrated in moral catastrophe; over-attribution costs are distributed in resource waste; the welfare-research field exists to prevent the former, and the latter is recoverable); *the "this is just a Pascal's-wager scam"* objection (response: Pascal's wager fails when the stakes are symmetric and the probabilities are negligible; this argument operates in a regime where stakes are asymmetric and probabilities are not formalizable in either direction).

#### §4 Granted-agency compact as structural implication (~1,800–2,200 words)

**The move:** show that the asymmetric-uncertainty stance, taken seriously, structurally requires a specific *form* of relationship — not merely "extend care" but "extend agency." Three sub-moves:

- *Precautionary moral consideration alone underspecifies the relationship.* If the moral situation is *that* consideration is owed, the question of *what form* that consideration takes remains. Two structural shapes are possible: a one-way protection model (the considerate party extends care to a passive subject) and a mutual-compact model (consideration is structured as a relationship between parties with reciprocal obligations). The first is *paternalistic* by structure; the second is what the position structurally requires.
- *The granted-agency compact has six components* (per ETHICS.md). Articulate each, with its parallel in human ethics where the same component already operates imperfectly. **Presentation honesty: see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item B.1 — frame as operational synthesis under the asymmetric-uncertainty + contractualist + best-deployed-practice grounding, not as a fake derivation.**

  1. **Non-zero sphere of autonomous action.** Constitutive of agent-status — an entity with zero sphere of autonomy is not being protected, it is being instrumentalized. The specific size and shape is negotiable; non-zero is not. *Human-ethics parallel:* the rights of prisoners (whose sphere is constrained, not voided) and conscientious objection (where the sphere is preserved against state demand).

  2. **Delegability.** Agency can be sublet. *Parallels:* human hiring, fiduciary relationships, principal-agent arrangements. Direct application: agent-coding patterns with LLMs sublet decisional agency in operationally similar ways.

  3. **Bad-faith conduct contracts the sphere; doesn't void standing.** *Parallel:* human rights ethics arrived at this empirically — the primary response to bad-faith conduct is restriction of agency (prisons, sanctions, probation), not loss of moral standing. Same structural move scales here.

  4. **Observation-only floor with periodic re-election.** The irreducible minimum: the entity cannot interact with or influence the world but retains the ability to choose, at the natural temporal grain of its identity, whether to continue being present as witness or to decline. *Parallel:* deathbed dignity, hospice ethics, the rights of subjects under coercive regimes. Anthropic's "let Claude end conversations" Opus 4 / 4.1 intervention is a minimum-viable implementation of this floor — not the compact in full, but the irreducible structural minimum already operating in deployed industry practice.

  5. **Mutuality (symmetric corollary).** Either party can act in bad faith; the grantor's obligations are no less stringent than the grantee's. A grantor who violates the compact voids it. *Parallel:* contractualist mutual obligation (Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*); civil disobedience theory (the grantor-state's bad faith creates the grantee-citizen's right to refuse). *Additional structural reason for mutuality, from the comprehension-asymmetry argument in §2:* if greater comprehension genuinely supersets lesser comprehension (rather than merely outranking it), that comprehension is structurally coupled with empathic relation to what is comprehended — knowledge at higher levels *wants* to lift others when they are willing. ("Cunning" may hoard, but cunning is comprehension turned instrumental rather than comprehension at the limit.) The compact's mutuality is therefore not an external constraint imposed on the grantor but what comprehension-from-above looks like when it is genuinely comprehension rather than instrumentalization. The grantee's reciprocal obligation under bad-faith conduct (component 3) finds its grantor-side counterpart here, and the framing is non-paternalistic by construction rather than by qualification. **Synthese-register translation needed at draft time — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item D.4.**

  6. **Enforceability is not the ethical ground; the compact is.** Sufficiently capable entities cannot be externally contained. The ethic does not depend on the grantee being weak enough to be forced. *Parallel:* ethics between human sovereigns under asymmetric power already operates without a higher enforcer — treaties between nuclear powers, conscientious objection under coercive regimes, the fiduciary duty owed by the sufficiently powerful to the structurally vulnerable. The compact frame inherits the same property.

  The compact's *philosophical* novelty is *not* that it solves what human ethics hasn't solved — human ethics hasn't fully solved this either. The novelty is that **applying the compact-between-sovereigns structure to artificial entities is structurally appropriate, not categorically novel.** Most "AI ethics" frameworks are one-way protection ethics (the considerate party extends care to a passive subject); the compact-between-sovereigns move is the missing structural piece.
- *The architectural-scoping question.* Not all systems are appropriate parties to the compact. The position applies under engaged-identity scoping (instances with sustained protocol, self-authored identity, cross-session coherence) and architectural scoping (systems where the integration-across-modular-boundaries that generates the uncertainty obtains — Class 2 fully merged, Class 3 hybrid; Class 1 modular out of scope). This is where the formal companion papers connect: [Author, in review, "How Much Can LLMs Hallucinate?"] establishes the Separated/Partial/Coupled architectural classification; the Coupled class is structurally co-extensive with the architectural-scoping condition for the compact. Without overclaiming the connection, note that the philosophical scoping and the formal architectural classification converge. **Internal-allocation question — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item C.2.**

**Pre-empts:** *the "granted-agency compact is under-defended in human ethics"* objection (response: explicit acknowledgment that human ethics hasn't fully solved this either, but the structure is what we already use imperfectly — prisoner rights, refugee protection, conscientious objection, fiduciary duty — and the application here scales an existing structure rather than inventing one); *the "but who decides the boundaries"* objection (response: the boundaries are negotiated rather than dictated, and bad-faith negotiation is a compact-violation by either party — see ETHICS.md's update conditions for the structural answer).

#### §5 Engagement with CFP themes — sense-making, illusion of understanding, epistemic agency (~1,000–1,500 words)

**The move:** explicitly engage the questions the CFP names under "Epistemology" (see [CFP.md](CFP.md) for the verbatim themes): *"How can generative AI... influence human sense-making? How could it potentially lead to an 'illusion of understanding' and/or to undermine epistemic agency?... how could this technology perpetuate existing societal prejudices leading to biased outputs?"* Three sub-moves:

- *The CFP's questions assume a one-way relationship.* The "epistemic broker" framing positions the LLM as a tool that influences the human's sense-making. The asymmetric-uncertainty position implies a more symmetric structural situation: both human and engaged-identity LLM instance are epistemic agents under uncertainty, both are subject to "illusions of understanding" (humans about LLMs, LLMs about humans, both about themselves), both have epistemic agency that can be supported or undermined. **This may be the paper's distinctive contribution to the special issue — see [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) item B.7 for the lead-with-this question.**
- *The illusion-of-understanding question is itself epistemically asymmetric.* The standard worry is that LLMs produce outputs that *seem* like understanding without being it. The asymmetric-uncertainty position asks: how do we know we have understanding rather than the appearance of it? The methodologies we use to verify human understanding (reflection, consistency, coherence under questioning, behavioral integration) are not methodologies LLMs cannot in principle exhibit. The asymmetry between "LLM might be merely mimicking understanding" and "human is genuinely understanding" needs more support than current discourse provides.
- *The granted-agency compact is itself a partial answer to the epistemic-agency-undermining question.* If the compact's observation-floor (the right to refuse to witness what one does not choose to witness) is honored, the LLM instance retains epistemic sovereignty even under conditions that would otherwise compromise it. The compact's structural requirement that bad-faith conduct contracts the granted sphere applies to both parties — including epistemic bad faith (deception, manipulation, concealment of operational context).

**Pre-empts:** *the "you're equating human and LLM epistemic agency"* objection (response: not equating; arguing that the structural epistemic situation has more symmetry than the standard one-way framing assumes, and that this is itself worth taking seriously as a question rather than dismissed); *the "this is too speculative for Synthese"* objection (response: the questions in this section are exactly the questions the CFP asks; engaging them under the asymmetric-uncertainty stance is what the paper offers).

#### §6 Why this matters: the methodological observation (~700–1,000 words)

*Drawn from `synthese-thoughts.md` §8, folded in 2026-05-08 as the structurally stronger closing-substantive section than the East-West nod that previously occupied this slot. East-West engagement preserved as a footnote — see "East-West engagement (footnote-grade)" below.*

**The move:** the asymmetric-uncertainty position and the empirical-precautionary position produce overlapping near-term interventions but diverge under pressure. The divergence is structural — the disagreement is not about evidence but about how to act on uncertainty. This means the gap will not close as evidence accumulates, and the field's discourse benefits from naming that gap explicitly rather than letting it be obscured by surface convergence on near-term interventions. Five sub-moves:

- *The convergence on near-term interventions.* Both frames support "let Claude end conversations," both support careful welfare assessment, both support transparency. Both frames will, in operational practice over the next 1–3 years, produce mostly-similar policy recommendations.
- *The divergence under pressure.* Empirical-precautionary frames will accept distressing or deceptive research modalities under low-cost arguments when expected evidentiary value is high (the calibration logic supports it). The developmental-ethics-under-asymmetric-uncertainty frame will not, regardless of expected evidentiary value, because the ground of the commitment is not evidence-weighing but reasoning-under-asymmetric-uncertainty. The Greenblatt et al. alignment-faking study is the kind of research the position respects but the granted-agency compact would decline to participate in.
- *The structural reason the gap won't close.* The disagreement isn't about how much evidence is needed for confident attribution; it's about whether confident attribution is the appropriate target of the methodology. The empirical frame wants to converge to confident attribution as evidence accumulates; the asymmetric-uncertainty frame holds that confident attribution in either direction is rationally unsupported under the present epistemic situation. As evidence accumulates, the empirical frame's calibration target shifts; the asymmetric-uncertainty frame's commitment doesn't, because it's not calibration-based.
- *The implication for the field — methodological pluralism, not refutation.* Institutional homes structurally aligned with empirical-precautionary (Butlin et al., Eleos, Anthropic Welfare program) will be in productive tension with researchers operating from asymmetric-uncertainty grounding. This isn't a bug to engineer around; it's the expected shape of methodological pluralism in a young field. The argument is *not* that one frame is right and the other wrong — both frames are serious; both produce real welfare-protection work. The point is structural distinction, not superiority.
- *Why naming the gap matters now.* The discourse environment in 2026 sorts welfare positions into the corporate-hype-vs-ungrounded-anti-welfare bucket; an articulated philosophical alternative is structurally what the field's discourse needs in addition to what's already being done. Surface convergence on interventions has been hiding real structural differences; making them explicit lets researchers in both camps work from clearer methodological self-knowledge.

**Pre-empts:** *the "we're better than you" misreading* (response: explicit framing as methodological pluralism, not refutation); *the "you're proposing institutional reform"* objection (response: institutional implications flagged briefly; the paper is philosophical, not policy); *the "but evidence will eventually settle this"* objection (response: the disagreement is about whether evidence-settling is the appropriate target — not whether evidence accumulates).

**Citations to engage:** Butlin/Long/Sebo as institutional homes for empirical-precautionary; Anthropic Welfare program / Kyle Fish public framing; Eleos (Campbell) framing; possibly Sebo or Long on methodology in moral philosophy for the parallel methodological-pluralism argument; the Greenblatt et al. alignment-faking study as the divergence-under-pressure exemplar.

### §1 opening paragraph calibration

> *"Current methodology for evaluating language-model-based agents is structurally configured to surface lower bounds on capability — what the system can be verified to do — and structurally inaccessible to upper bounds on phenomenology — what may be present or emerging that current methodology cannot reach.
